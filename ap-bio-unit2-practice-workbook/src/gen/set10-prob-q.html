Problem 1 | Sequence similarities in organelles and bacteria

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 2.B, 6.B

Researchers compare a homologous gene from a mitochondrion with versions from different sampled lineages. The listed percent identities are illustrative and measured over the same aligned sequence region.

(a) Identify the closest sequence match to the mitochondrial gene. [2 points]

(b) Explain why the pattern is consistent with endosymbiotic origin. [2 points]

Problem 2 | Double membranes and engulfment

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.B

Mitochondria and chloroplasts have two bounding membranes in many eukaryotic cells.

(a) Name the evolutionary hypothesis connecting these organelles to bacteria. [1 point]

(b) State how engulfment could contribute to a double membrane. [1 point]

(c) Identify one additional bacterial-like trait in the organelles. [1 point]

(d) Explain why a double membrane alone is not conclusive proof. [1 point]

Problem 3 | Replication of endosymbiotic organelles

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.B

Mitochondria in a eukaryotic cell increase in number by division of preexisting mitochondria.

(a) State the process by which mitochondria commonly divide. [1 point]

(b) Explain how this observation resembles bacterial reproduction. [1 point]

(c) Identify a limitation of using division mode as the only evidence of bacterial ancestry. [1 point]

(d) Name another organelle believed to have arisen through endosymbiosis. [1 point]

Problem 4 | Comparing competing evolutionary hypotheses

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 3.C, 6.B

A class proposes two explanations for chloroplast origin. Hypothesis E: an ancestral eukaryote retained an engulfed photosynthetic bacterium. Hypothesis I: a completely new photosynthetic organelle arose entirely from the host internal membrane system.

Evidence Chloroplast observation
Genome Small circular DNA molecule present
Ribosomes Bacterial-like characteristics
Sequence homology Close matches to cyanobacterial genes

(a) Identify evidence predicted more strongly by Hypothesis E. [2 points]

(b) Describe an evidence pattern that favors Hypothesis E over I. [2 points]

(c) Explain why retained nuclear genes derived from the symbiont would not contradict Hypothesis E. [3 points]

(d) Propose a comparative investigation that could test the hypotheses. [2 points]

Problem 5 | Comparing prokaryotic and eukaryotic organization

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.C

Researchers compare a bacterial cell with a plant cell containing mitochondria and chloroplasts.

(a) Identify which cell contains a nucleus. [1 point]

(b) Identify which sample has ribosomes. [1 point]

(c) State one organizational difference. [1 point]

(d) Explain why shared ribosomes do not imply identical cell organization. [1 point]

Problem 6 | Interpreting a simplified evolutionary diagram

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 2.C, 6.B

A proposed history depicts a host cell acquiring a respiratory bacterial symbiont and, in one later lineage, acquiring a photosynthetic bacterial symbiont.

(a) Identify the organelle derived from the respiratory symbiont. [1 point]

(b) Identify the organelle derived from the photosynthetic symbiont. [1 point]

(c) Explain why not every modern eukaryote possesses chloroplasts. [1 point]

(d) State why the diagram is a model rather than a direct historical recording. [1 point]
