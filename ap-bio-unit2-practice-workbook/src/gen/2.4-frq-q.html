FRQ 1 · Identifying the Molecular Basis of Selective Permeability (9 points)

Researchers construct phospholipid vesicles with no transport proteins and use a fluorescent detector to measure the initial passage of three substances under equivalent concentration differences and membrane areas. Rates are arbitrary units (AU). A second vesicle group contains functional glucose carriers; neither group contains an ion channel. The investigators propose that molecule polarity, charge, and membrane proteins influence rates.

Molecule Protein-free (AU) With glucose carriers (AU)
O₂ 80 79
H₂O 10 10
Glucose 1 41
Na⁺ 0.2 0.2

(a) [1 point] Identify the region of the bilayer that produces the primary barrier to Na⁺ movement.

(b) [1 point] Describe one molecular characteristic that allows O₂ to cross readily without a protein.

(c) [2 points] Calculate the fold increase in glucose crossing rate produced by the carriers. Show your setup.

(d) [2 points] Explain why a glucose carrier can increase glucose movement without substantially changing the O₂ crossing rate.

(e) [2 points] Predict the effect of adding functional Na⁺-selective channels to the protein-free vesicles on Na⁺ passage, and justify the prediction.

(f) [1 point] State one appropriate control that would test whether the change in glucose crossing is due to transporter function rather than simply protein insertion.

FRQ 2 · Testing the Protective Role of Cell Walls (9 points)

Investigators expose bacterial cultures to a dilute external solution for 10 minutes. Three treatments are compared: intact walls, enzymatically weakened walls, and walls weakened and then experimentally restored. Each has eight independent cultures. The measured response is the percentage of lysed cells; means are accompanied by SE. Statistical comparisons to the intact group yield the p-values shown, with α = 0.05.

Group Mean lysis ± SE p relative to intact
Intact 8 ± 2% Reference
Weakened 48 ± 4% p = 0.003
Restored 12 ± 3% p = 0.31

(a) [2 points] Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable.

(b) [1 point] Calculate the percentage-point difference in mean lysis between weakened and intact groups.

(c) [2 points] Using the stated α, evaluate the null hypothesis of equal mean lysis between the weakened and intact groups.

(d) [2 points] Explain how cell-wall structure could account for the difference in lysis.

(e) [1 point] Explain why the p = 0.31 result does not establish that restored and intact groups have identical mean lysis.

(f) [1 point] Propose one additional controlled measurement that would distinguish cell-wall mechanical support from a claim that cell walls completely block water.

FRQ 5 · Using Data to Evaluate a Permeability Hypothesis (4 points)

Two groups of identical synthetic liposomes are compared. Group P has only phospholipids. Group Q also contains a protein known to form an ion-selective pore. At matched ion gradients, potassium influx is 0.5 AU in P and 55.0 AU in Q. Oxygen influx is 78 AU in P and 80 AU in Q. All membranes have comparable surface areas. The investigators propose that the pore, not a general breakdown of the bilayer, explains increased potassium influx.

(a) [1 point] State the independent variable in this investigation.

(b) [1 point] Describe one result that supports the investigators’ hypothesis.

(c) [1 point] Explain how the oxygen results help evaluate whether the inserted pore generally damages the membrane.

(d) [1 point] Propose a control that would more directly distinguish pore function from the effects of inserting any membrane protein.
