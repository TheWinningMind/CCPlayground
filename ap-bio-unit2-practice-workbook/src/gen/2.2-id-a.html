01. Surface area-to-volume ratio (SA:V) — The ratio relates exchange surface to the amount of cell contents.

02. Surface area — Membrane area constrains the capacity for exchange across a cell boundary.

03. Volume — Volume correlates with the quantity of cytoplasm and its demands.

04. 6s² — A cube has six square faces, each with area s².

05. s³ — Cube volume equals length × width × height.

06. 6/s — Divide 6s² by s³ to obtain 6/s.

07. 3 cm⁻¹ — Its area is 24 cm² and volume is 8 cm³, so SA:V = 3 cm⁻¹.

08. 6 cm⁻¹ — Surface area is 6 cm² and volume is 1 cm³.

09. Geometric scaling effect — Surface area scales with length squared and volume with length cubed.

10. Declining surface area-to-volume ratio — Increasing cell dimensions lower the available area per unit volume.

11. Microvillus — Microvilli expand the exchange surface of certain cells.

12. Root hair — Root hairs provide additional surface for uptake from soil.

13. Diffusion distance — Greater distances can slow delivery to deeper regions.

14. Membrane folding — Folding increases the membrane area available for exchange.

15. 4πr² — The surface area of a sphere is four pi times the squared radius.

16. (4/3)πr³ — The radius is cubed in the sphere-volume expression.

17. 3/r — Dividing the sphere's surface area by its volume yields 3/r.

18. Agar-cube diffusion model — Different-sized agar blocks model diffusion distance and relative exchange.

19. Side length — Changing the cube's side length changes area and volume by different powers.

20. Metabolic demand — Larger cell volumes contain more material requiring maintenance and exchange.

21. 6 cm⁻¹ — The 1-cm cube has surface area 6 cm² and volume 1 cm³; SA:V is 6 cm⁻¹.

23. 2 cm⁻¹ — The 3-cm cube has surface area 54 cm² and volume 27 cm³; SA:V is 2 cm⁻¹.
