Tonicity, Osmoregulation and Water-Potential Calculations

Problem 1 | Potato-tissue mass change and isotonic solution

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

External sucrose (M) Initial mass (g) Final mass (g)
0.1 5 5.55
0.2 5 5.2
0.3 5 5.0
0.4 5 4.8
0.5 5 4.6

(a) Calculate percentage mass change in the 0.10 M and 0.50 M solutions. [2 points]

  • Point 1: (5.55 − 5.00)/5.00 × 100 = +11%.

  • Point 2: (4.60 − 5.00)/5.00 × 100 = −8%.

(b) Identify the approximate isotonic concentration and explain why. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Approximately 0.30 M sucrose.

  • Point 2: At 0.30 M there is no net tissue mass change.

(c) Calculate the solute potential of the 0.30 M external solution. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Use Ψs = −iCRT and i = 1.

  • Point 2: Substitute Ψs = −(1)(0.30)(0.0831)(298).

  • Point 3: Ψs ≈ −7.43 bar, with the correct sign and units.

Worked calculation: −(1)(0.30 mol/L)(0.0831 L·bar/(mol·K))(298 K) = −7.429 bar.

(d) Predict net water movement in 0.50 M sucrose relative to the tissue and justify. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Water leaves the tissue initially.

  • Point 2: The higher external solute concentration has lower water potential than the tissue.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 4 | Comparing dissociating and nondissociating solutes

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Side Solute Concentration Assumed i
A NaCl 0.20 M 2
B Sucrose 0.30 M 1

(a) Calculate total dissolved-particle molarity (ideal osmolarity) for each side. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Side A: iC = 2(0.20) = 0.40 osmolar.

  • Point 2: Side B: iC = 1(0.30) = 0.30 osmolar.

(b) Calculate the solute potential on each side. [2 points]

  • Point 1: A: Ψs = −(2)(0.20)(0.0831)(298) ≈ −9.91 bar.

  • Point 2: B: Ψs = −(1)(0.30)(0.0831)(298) ≈ −7.43 bar.

(c) Predict the initial net water movement and justify. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Water moves from side B toward side A.

  • Point 2: Side B has the higher water potential (−7.43 versus −9.91 bar).

  • Point 3: The membrane prevents compensating solute diffusion in this model.

(d) Explain why comparing molarities alone gives the wrong prediction. [2 points]

  • Point 1: NaCl contributes approximately two dissolved particles per formula unit.

  • Point 2: Water potential depends on effective particle concentration, not solute formula-unit molarity alone.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.

Problem 5 | Plasmolysis and the cell wall

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Name the observed process. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Plasmolysis.

(b) Identify the net direction of water movement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Out of the cell.

(c) Explain why the plasma membrane separates from the wall. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Water loss reduces turgor and the protoplast shrinks.

(d) Predict what may happen after the cell is returned to a sufficiently dilute medium. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Water can reenter and the protoplast may expand toward the wall again, if the cell remains viable.

Total for Problem 5: 4 points.

Problem 6 | Contractile vacuole activity

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify the organelle involved. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Contractile vacuole.

(b) Explain why freshwater favors inward water movement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The surrounding medium is typically hypotonic relative to the cytoplasm.

(c) Predict the effect of a more dilute medium on water-expulsion frequency. [1 point]

  • Point 1: It increases.

(d) Explain how the response contributes to survival. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Water expulsion reduces excessive swelling and helps maintain water balance.

Total for Problem 6: 4 points.
