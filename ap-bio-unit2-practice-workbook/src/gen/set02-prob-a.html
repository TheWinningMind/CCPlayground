Cell Size: Geometric and Exchange Problems

Problem 1 | Cubic cell models at three sizes

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Side length (µm) Surface area (µm²) Volume (µm³)
1 6 1
2 24 8
4 96 64

(a) Calculate surface area and volume for the 2-µm model. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Surface area = 6(2²) = 24 µm².

  • Point 2: Volume = 2³ = 8 µm³.

Worked calculation: SA = 6s²; V = s³.

(b) Determine SA:V for the 1-µm and 4-µm models. [2 points]

  • Point 1: For s = 1 µm, SA/V = 6/1 = 6 µm⁻¹.

  • Point 2: For s = 4 µm, SA/V = 96/64 = 1.5 µm⁻¹.

(c) A student claims that the 4-µm model exchanges less material in total solely because its SA:V is lower. Evaluate the claim. [3 points]

  • Point 1: The claim is not supported solely by SA:V.

  • Point 2: The larger cube has more absolute surface area (96 µm² versus 6 µm²).

  • Point 3: SA:V describes exchange capacity relative to volume, not total flux without permeability and gradient information.

(d) Predict which model is most favorable for exchange relative to cytoplasmic volume and explain. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The 1-µm cube has the greatest surface-area-to-volume ratio.

  • Point 2: It provides the most exchange surface per unit volume.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 2 | Spherical cell growth

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Calculate the original SA:V. [1 point]

  • Point 1: 3/r = 3/2 = 1.5 µm⁻¹.

(b) Calculate the new SA:V. [1 point]

  • Point 1: 3/4 = 0.75 µm⁻¹.

(c) Determine the factor by which volume increases. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The radius doubles, so volume increases by 2³ = 8-fold.

(d) Explain how the results help account for a size limitation. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Volume-dependent demand grows more rapidly than surface-dependent exchange capacity.

Total for Problem 2: 4 points.

Problem 3 | Agar-cube penetration

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify the side length of the unreacted core in the 6-mm cube. [1 point]

  • Point 1: 6 − 2(1) = 4 mm.

(b) Calculate the reacted volume fraction of the 4-mm cube. [1 point]

  • Point 1: (4³ − 2³)/4³ = 56/64 = 87.5%.

(c) State the percentage reacted in the 2-mm cube. [1 point]

  • Point 1: 100%; penetration from opposite faces reaches the center.

(d) Explain why equal penetration depth produces different reacted fractions. [1 point]

  • Point 1: A smaller cube has a greater surface-area-to-volume ratio and a smaller relative unpenetrated core.

Total for Problem 3: 4 points.

Problem 4 | Microvilli without added volume

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Cell model Area (µm²) Volume (µm³)
Unfolded 150 100
Folded 240 100

(a) Calculate SA:V before and after folding. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Initial SA/V = 150/100 = 1.50 µm⁻¹.

  • Point 2: After folding SA/V = 240/100 = 2.40 µm⁻¹.

(b) Calculate the percentage increase in effective surface area. [2 points]

  • Point 1: (240 − 150)/150 × 100 = 60%.

  • Point 2: The comparison uses the initial 150 µm² as the denominator.

(c) Predict the effect of the folds on potential exchange under otherwise unchanged conditions. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Potential exchange capacity relative to volume increases.

  • Point 2: The folds expose more membrane area for transport.

  • Point 3: The actual exchange rate still depends on gradients and transport protein activity.

(d) Explain why adding folds differs from simply increasing cell radius. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Folding can add exchange surface without a proportional increase in volume.

  • Point 2: Increasing all dimensions normally decreases SA:V.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.

Problem 5 | Same volume, different shapes

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Calculate the volume of either model. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Both models have volume 8 µm³.

(b) Calculate the rectangular model surface area. [1 point]

  • Point 1: 2(1×1 + 1×8 + 1×8) = 34 µm².

(c) Identify which model has the higher SA:V. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The rectangular model: 34/8 = 4.25 µm⁻¹ versus 24/8 = 3 µm⁻¹.

(d) Explain an ecological or physiological advantage of greater exposed surface. [1 point]

  • Point 1: More area per unit volume can facilitate exchange with the environment, provided the surfaces are accessible.

Total for Problem 5: 4 points.

Problem 6 | Heat exchange at equal membrane properties

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Calculate the ratio of their surface-area-to-volume values (small:large). [1 point]

  • Point 1: (3/1):(3/3) = 3:1.

(b) Which cell has greater total outer surface area? [1 point]

  • Point 1: The 3-µm-radius cell.

(c) Which cell has greater potential heat exchange per unit volume under the stated assumptions? [1 point]

  • Point 1: The 1-µm-radius cell.

(d) Justify why total surface area and surface area per volume lead to different comparisons. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Absolute area increases with r², while volume increases with r³, so SA:V falls as size increases.

Total for Problem 6: 4 points.
