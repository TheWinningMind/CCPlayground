FRQ 1 · Spatial separation (9 points)
Criterion Award 1 point for
1(a) Describes that a lipid membrane establishes a distinct local chemical environment or regulates access of reactants to enzymes.
1(b)-1 Remaining S in intact cells at 15 min = 100 − 65 − 15 = 20 nmol; calculation shown.
1(b)-2 Difference in P = 65 − 36 = 29 nmol (intact higher); calculation shown.
1(c)-1 Identifies membrane integrity/leakiness as the independent variable.
1(c)-2 Explains matching initial S/A/B helps attribute yield differences to membrane permeability or compartmentation rather than unequal enzyme/substrate amounts.
1(c)-3 Proposes independent measurement of enzyme A activity under equal in-vitro assay conditions, or another valid assay for direct enzyme effect.
1(d)-1 Predicts lower P formation if A loses its targeting signal and becomes separated from the retained S, conditional on unchanged catalytic activity.
1(d)-2 Justifies with separation of A from the retained S, limiting formation of P despite the presence of catalytically active A.
1(d)-3 Notes the table alone cannot determine whether membrane treatment altered intrinsic A catalytic activity, because A was not independently assayed.

Scoring note: The 100 nmol initial substrate is completely accounted for in each line at 15 minutes (intact: 65+15+20; leaky: 36+44+20). The final clause asks what cannot be inferred—not whether the manipulation necessarily altered catalytic activity.

Criterion Award 1 point for
2(a) Identifies folds (cristae) of the mitochondrial inner membrane as increasing surface area without proportional organelle volume increase.
2(b)-1 Plots correct mean values at 30, 53, and 79 in corresponding categories.
2(b)-2 Y-axis accurately scaled and labeled “ATP synthesis capacity (relative units/min)” or equivalent.
2(b)-3 X-axis accurately identifies each folding category.
2(b)-4 Error bars correctly show ±3, ±4, and ±4 SE at respective means.
2(c)-1 Percentage increase [(79−30)/30]×100 = 163.3% (approximately 163%); calculation shown.
2(c)-2 Identifies controlled variable such as mean mitochondrial volume or protein density per membrane area, or abundant substrate supply in the standardized assay.
2(d)-1 Predicts reduced maximum ATP synthesis capacity if relevant available inner membrane area decreases under controlled conditions.
2(d)-2 Explains less membrane area at constant protein density supports fewer total embedded reaction complexes, reducing maximum reaction capacity under the assay conditions.

FRQ 3 · Acidic compartment (4 points)
Criterion Award 1 point for
3(a) Identifies a lysosome.
3(b) Explains that the surrounding membrane enables the organelle lumen to maintain ion/pH conditions different from cytosol (through regulated transport).
3(c) Predicts decreased digestion of the suitable substrate under the inhibitor treatment.
3(d) Cites activity decline from 100 to 28% as pH rises from 5.0 to 6.5, with equal hydrolase abundance; diminished activity is attributable to changed conditions rather than less hydrolase.
FRQ 4 · Folding model (4 points)
Criterion Award 1 point for
4(a) Describes more folds/greater membrane surface area within approximately the same enclosing region in the folded model.
4(b) Explains greater membrane area can accommodate more membrane-associated enzyme sites when density remains the same.
4(c) Predicts decreased maximum product formation with reduced folding when other named conditions stay constant.
4(d) Identifies a limitation such as finite enzyme supply, substrate limitation, compromised exchange, enzyme density changing, or model not measuring actual rates; therefore folding is not sufficient to prove universal rate increases.

Editorial guardrails: For FRQ 2, the correlation between area and capacity is evidence consistent with the hypothesis, but causation is not inferred from correlation alone. The standardized assay and controls strengthen the proposed explanation. Some mitochondrial steps occur outside the inner membrane; do not assert that all cellular respiration occurs on cristae.
