FRQ 1 · Experimental Evidence for Functional Compartmentalization (9 points)

In a controlled experiment, researchers expose a eukaryotic cell preparation to increasing concentrations of a membrane-disrupting treatment. The same enzyme concentration, temperature, assay duration, and substrate concentration are maintained. Mean product formation (µmol min⁻¹) at treatment levels 0, 1, 2, 3, and 4 is 10.4, 8.8, 7.2, 5.5, and 4.0, respectively. Five independent preparations are measured at each level. The data are fictional and constructed for practice.

Treatment level 0 1 2 3 4
Mean product rate (µmol/min) 10.4 8.8 7.2 5.5 4.0

Graphing workspace for FRQ 1 part (b).

(a) [2 points] Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable.

(b) [2 points] Construct a correctly labeled graph showing the mean product formation rate at each treatment level. Plot all five values and connect adjacent points.

(c) [2 points] Calculate the percent decrease in mean product formation between levels 0 and 4. Show your work.

(d) [2 points] Predict the effect on enzyme localization if the treatment disrupts the membranes that normally enclose the enzyme. Explain how the prediction could contribute to the observed trend.

(e) [1 point] Identify one limitation of using only mean values without reporting variation across replicates.

FRQ 2 · Evaluating the Endosymbiotic Hypothesis (9 points)

A research group compares homologous gene sequences from two organelles and multiple bacterial lineages. For a particular conserved gene, a mitochondrial sequence shares 86% identity with sequences from a sampled alphaproteobacterial lineage and 58% with a cyanobacterial lineage. A chloroplast sequence shares 91% identity with sequences from a sampled cyanobacterial lineage and 54% with the alphaproteobacterial lineage. Investigators also observe that both organelles contain ribosomes and divide separately from nuclear division. These are constructed practice data.

Comparison Sequence identity
Mitochondrial–alphaproteobacterial 86%
Mitochondrial–cyanobacterial 58%
Chloroplast–cyanobacterial 91%
Chloroplast–alphaproteobacterial 54%

(a) [2 points] Describe the endosymbiotic hypothesis for the origin of mitochondria.

(b) [2 points] Identify which bacterial lineage is most closely associated with each organelle in the gene data.

(c) [1 point] Use one quantitative comparison from the data to support the endosymbiotic ancestry of one organelle.

(d) [2 points] Explain why gene sequence similarities provide more direct evidence of common ancestry than organelle size alone.

(e) [2 points] Propose one additional independent source of evidence and explain how its result could strengthen the claim.

FRQ 3 · Specialized Regions in Different Cell Types (4 points)

Cell P is identified as prokaryotic because it lacks a membrane-enclosed nucleus. An enzyme is concentrated in a specialized region near its plasma membrane. Cell E is eukaryotic and contains a membrane-bound compartment enriched in the same enzyme.

(a) [1 point] Describe one similarity in the spatial organization of enzyme activity in Cells P and E.

(b) [1 point] Identify one structural difference relevant to compartmentalization.

(c) [1 point] Predict the effect of dispersing the enzyme in Cell E throughout the cytosol, given that the original compartment has an optimal local pH.

(d) [1 point] Justify the prediction using the role of local chemical conditions in enzyme function.

FRQ 4 · Nuclear Control of a Mitochondrial Protein (4 points)

A nuclear gene encodes a protein used in a mitochondrial metabolic pathway. In an experimental mutant, the protein is translated at a normal rate but does not reach mitochondria; total mitochondrial DNA content remains unchanged.

(a) [1 point] Identify the cellular location where this nuclear-encoded protein is most directly synthesized.

(b) [1 point] Explain why the observation supports functional integration between mitochondria and the host cell.

(c) [1 point] Predict the likely effect on the mitochondrial pathway if the protein is essential to it.

(d) [1 point] Propose a measurement that would distinguish a targeting defect from a change in the amount of protein synthesized.

FRQ 5 · Ancestral Relationships and Alternative Explanations (4 points)

A student claims that because mitochondria have double membranes, every double-membrane structure must have evolved by endosymbiosis. Another student argues that molecular phylogenies, patterns of organelle division, and dependence on nuclear-encoded proteins should also be considered.

(a) [1 point] Identify the limitation in the first student’s claim.

(b) [1 point] Provide one additional observation that supports bacterial ancestry of mitochondria.

(c) [1 point] Explain how the observation in part (b) supports an evolutionary relationship.

(d) [1 point] Describe one reason why the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria does not demonstrate that the entire endomembrane system has the same origin.
