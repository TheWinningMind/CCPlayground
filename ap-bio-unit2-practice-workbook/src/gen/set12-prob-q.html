Cumulative Quantitative and Visual Challenges

Problem 1 | Equal SA:V but unequal total area

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 2.D, 5.A, 6.C

A spherical model has radius 1 µm. A cubic model has side length 2 µm. Use the standard surface-area and volume equations; treat both as ideal geometrical objects.

Object Dimension
Sphere radius = 1 µm
Cube side = 2 µm

(a) Calculate the sphere’s SA:V. [2 points]

(b) Calculate the cube’s SA:V. [2 points]

(c) Evaluate the statement “the equal ratios prove equal total exchange under all conditions.” [3 points]

(d) Explain why these models can nevertheless illustrate a common scaling principle. [2 points]

Problem 2 | Quick mass-change calculation

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 5.A, 6.B

A sample of plant tissue increases from 8.00 g to 8.56 g during an osmosis trial.

(a) Calculate the mass increase. [1 point]

(b) Calculate the percentage change. [1 point]

(c) Identify the initial direction of net water movement consistent with the change. [1 point]

(d) Explain why mass alone cannot quantify pressure potential directly. [1 point]

Problem 3 | Changes in membrane-transport rate

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 3.C, 5.A

A carrier-mediated transport rate falls from 32 to 20 arbitrary units after an inhibitor is added.

(a) Calculate the absolute decrease. [1 point]

(b) Calculate the percentage decrease relative to control. [1 point]

(c) Identify a control for interpreting the inhibitor effect. [1 point]

(d) Explain why the rate change does not establish direct ATP dependence. [1 point]

An experiment measures a membrane-dependent response at four treatment intensities. Values are means ± SEM, based on independent replicates. A model graph is provided as a reference for the answer guide.

Problem 5 | Water potential and numerical direction

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 5.A, 6.E

A solution contains 0.20 M sucrose at 25°C. Assume i = 1, T = 298 K, R = 0.0831 L·bar·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹, and Ψp = 0.

(a) State the relevant solute-potential equation. [1 point]

(b) Calculate the solute potential. [1 point]

(c) If a cell has Ψ = −6.00 bar, predict net water movement. [1 point]

(d) Justify the direction with the two potentials. [1 point]

Problem 6 | Integrating membrane and evolution concepts

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.B

A hypothetical organelle has a double membrane, its own small DNA genome, and ribosomes. Its inner membrane is highly folded.

(a) Identify a plausible organelle. [1 point]

(b) Explain what the inner folds contribute to current function. [1 point]

(c) Identify evidence from the description that is relevant to evolutionary origin. [1 point]

(d) State one additional result that would strengthen endosymbiotic inference. [1 point]
