Mechanisms of Transport: ATP and Electrochemical Gradients

Problem 1 | Stoichiometry of an ATP-driven ion pump

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 2.D, 5.A, 6.E

A sodium–potassium ATPase completes 250 transport cycles. In each idealized cycle, it hydrolyzes one ATP, exports three Na⁺, and imports two K⁺.

Figure M08.1: Original instructional model. Labels are intentional, not to physical scale.

Ion Per cycle Direction
Na⁺ 3 Out of cell
K⁺ 2 Into cell

(a) Calculate the number of Na⁺ moved outward and K⁺ moved inward. [2 points]

(b) Calculate ATP consumed and net positive charges transferred outward. [2 points]

(c) Explain why the pump is electrogenic and requires energy. [3 points]

(d) Predict the immediate effect of blocking ATP hydrolysis on pump-mediated transport. [2 points]

Problem 2 | Identifying an electrochemical gradient

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.E

An ion has a greater concentration outside a cell than inside. The cell interior is electrically negative relative to the exterior.

(a) For a positive ion, identify the chemical-gradient direction. [1 point]

(b) For the same positive ion, identify the electrical-force direction. [1 point]

(c) Predict net passive movement through an open selective channel. [1 point]

(d) Explain why an ion pump moving the ion outward would require energy. [1 point]

Problem 3 | ATP depletion versus passive ion leak

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.E

An inhibitor reduces ATP generation in animal cells without immediately disrupting the plasma membrane or ion channels.

(a) Predict the direct effect on Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase activity. [1 point]

(b) State whether existing ion concentration gradients vanish instantly. [1 point]

(c) Explain why the gradients change gradually. [1 point]

(d) Predict the eventual tendency of Na⁺ and K⁺ distributions if leak persists and no pumping occurs. [1 point]

Problem 5 | Conformational cycling of a carrier
