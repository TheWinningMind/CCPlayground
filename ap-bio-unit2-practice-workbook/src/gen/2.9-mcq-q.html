AP BIOLOGY • UNIT 2 • TOPIC 2.9

35 AP-Style Multiple-Choice Questions

STUDENT BOOKLET • 15 individual questions + 4 five-question stimulus sets • One best answer per question | Original, unofficial AP-style educational material.

Directions: Choose the single best answer for each question. All experimental datasets are original hypothetical teaching models. Unless stated otherwise, investigators control all named variables and the observations refer only to the defined experimental conditions.

Skills focus: 2.9.A.1, 2.9.B.1; especially science practice 6.E (prediction), alongside data interpretation, experimental design, and quantitative reasoning.

Stimulus Set 1 | Competing enzyme pathways

Researchers develop an artificial compartment inside cultured eukaryotic cells. Enzyme A converts substrate S to desired product P, whereas enzyme B converts the same substrate S to by-product W. All groups contain the same amounts of S, enzyme A, and enzyme B and are measured after the same incubation period. The compartment retains S with enzyme A while restricting access by enzyme B. The data are hypothetical and illustrate the experiment design.

Condition P formed (nmol) W formed (nmol) Unreacted S (nmol)
No compartment 40 40 20
Intact compartment 72 8 20
Membrane selectively disrupted 45 35 20

All rows begin with 100 nmol S; mass balance is conserved.

Answer questions 1–5 using this stimulus.

1
Which conclusion is most directly supported by the table?
  1. The intact membrane increases the intrinsic turnover rate of enzyme A by the same proportion as product P.
  2. Higher P formation demonstrates that enzyme B has been irreversibly inactivated.
  3. The membrane allows P to form independently of the localization of its substrate.
  4. Isolating S with enzyme A shifts more substrate toward P without changing the initial substrate amount.
2
Relative to the no-compartment group, what is the percentage increase in the amount of P formed by the intact-compartment group?
  1. 32%
  2. 44%
  3. 180%
  4. 80%
3
If the selectively disrupted group has partially restored access of S to enzyme B, which result is consistent with this mechanism?
  1. The selectively disrupted group should retain the intact-compartment value of 72 nmol P.
  2. A leaky boundary should cause production of W to fall below the 8 nmol intact value.
  3. Product P is intermediate between the no-compartment and intact-compartment values.
  4. Increasing access of B to S should prevent enzyme A from forming any P.
4
Which aspect of the design most directly helps isolate compartmentalization as the explanatory variable?
  1. Use the same incubation time but allow enzyme concentrations to vary between groups.
  2. The total amounts of both enzymes and the initial substrate are held constant.
  3. Use the same enzyme amounts but change the starting substrate concentration in each condition.
  4. Use identical starting substrate but compare cells from two species without checking enzyme expression.
5
Which additional observation would most strongly support the claim that product differences result from spatial separation rather than a change in enzyme A activity?
  1. Purified enzyme A has the same catalytic activity in samples from all three groups when tested under identical conditions.
  2. Total enzyme A protein is equal even when its isolated catalytic activity differs across groups.
  3. Enzyme B protein is present in equal amounts but is localized differently across groups.
  4. The experimental groups have similar cell volumes but have not been tested for enzyme function.

Stimulus Set 2 | A specialized reaction environment

Scientists examine a lysosomal hydrolase whose activity depends on pH. Equal enzyme samples are assayed under otherwise identical conditions. They also measure lysosomal lumen pH in untreated cells and cells exposed to a treatment that reduces lumen acidification. The numbers are hypothetical and for practice.

Condition pH Relative hydrolase activity
Untreated lysosomal lumen 5.0 100
Less acidic lysosomal lumen 6.5 28
Cytosol-like assay solution 7.2 12

Activities are normalized to 100 for the first condition; the assay controls enzyme quantity.

6

  3. The acidic environment must be created by two surrounding lipid bilayers.
  4. A membrane-bounded lumen can maintain a chemical environment different from the cytosol.
7

  1. Hydrolase activity must remain at 100% as long as enzyme abundance does not decrease.
  2. The higher pH should raise hydrolase activity because the cytosol is near neutral.
  3. The treatment changes only the lysosomal surface area and cannot alter activity.
  4. The measured hydrolase activity will decrease under the specified assay conditions.
8
How many times as high is the activity at pH 5.0 as at pH 6.5 in this experiment?
  1. About 1.3 times

  3. About 7.2 times
  4. About 3.6 times

  1. Increasing lysosomal pH further toward 7.2 without changing protein quantity.

Stimulus Set 3 | Internal membrane area and capacity

Researchers compare three synthetic membrane systems. Each has the same enclosed volume, the same enzyme density per unit of membrane area, and equal substrate supply. The enzyme is active only when associated with a membrane. The data are hypothetical.

Membrane system Available membrane area (μm²) Product formed per minute (nmol)
A 8 20
B 12 30
C 16 40

The data compare total reaction capacity under controlled conditions, not the intrinsic rate of a single enzyme molecule.

Answer questions 11–15 using this stimulus.

11
Which feature most directly accounts for the observed difference in total product formation?
  1. The amount of product rises because every enzyme molecule has a higher intrinsic catalytic rate at greater area.
  2. Larger area changes the initial substrate concentration although substrate supply was controlled.
  3. Total product depends on enclosed volume rather than membrane-associated sites in this setup.
  4. A larger membrane area can accommodate more membrane-associated enzyme molecules at the same density.
12
What is the percentage increase in product formed per minute from system A to system C?
  1. 50%
  2. 100%
  3. 75%
  4. 200%
13
Which modification best tests whether surface area, rather than enclosed volume, influences the outcome?
  1. Change area and total enzyme density simultaneously, then attribute the result only to surface area.
  2. Increase substrate concentration with membrane folding so both variables affect the response.
  3. Change membrane folding while maintaining enclosed volume and enzyme density per membrane area.
  4. Compare systems with different volumes but hold membrane area fixed.
14
A mutation decreases folding of an internal membrane but leaves organelle volume approximately unchanged. Which outcome is most consistent with the table, assuming enzyme density and substrate supply remain unchanged?
  1. Higher product formation because a smaller area necessarily raises turnover per enzyme.
  2. Unchanged product formation because inner surface area is irrelevant to membrane-associated enzymes.
  3. Lower total product formation because fewer membrane-associated enzymes can operate.
  4. Lower product formation only if the enclosed volume also becomes smaller.
15
Which statement would NOT be justified solely by the data?
  1. Increasing membrane surface area always increases every cellular reaction rate, regardless of enzyme abundance or substrate supply.
  2. In these systems, product formed rises as membrane area rises.
  3. In the controlled comparison, system C produces twice as much product per minute as system A.
  4. The systems were designed to have equal enclosed volumes.

Stimulus Set 4 | Sequential reactions near a membrane

In an artificial eukaryotic-cell model, enzyme X converts a substrate to an intermediate, and enzyme Y converts the intermediate to the final product. Investigators place X and Y in different spatial arrangements, while holding total enzyme amounts and initial substrate constant. Each reaction occurs for 12 minutes; data are hypothetical.

Arrangement Final product (nmol) Remaining intermediates (nmol)
X and Y localized to same membrane region 84 11
X and Y dispersed through same volume 48 37
Only X membrane-localized; Y dispersed 59 28

The remaining material may also include unreacted substrate; the table does not purport to show complete mass balance.

Answer questions 16–20 using this stimulus.

16
Which hypothesis is most consistent with the results?
  1. Localizing successive enzymes in the same membrane region can increase final-product formation.
  2. Co-localization proves that the two enzymes form a permanent multimeric complex.
  3. The dispersed arrangement contains less total enzyme Y than the co-localized arrangement.
  4. Membrane-bound enzymes synthesize final product even when their substrate is absent.
17
Compared with the dispersed arrangement, how much additional final product is formed when X and Y are localized together?
  1. 11 nmol
  2. 25 nmol
  3. 36 nmol
  4. 84 nmol
18
If a targeting defect prevents Y from associating with the membrane while X remains membrane-localized, which observed condition best predicts the result?
  1. The third arrangement must produce 84 nmol because X remains on a membrane.
  2. The defect must stop all reaction steps and prevent the intermediate from accumulating.
  3. The defect should have no effect because enzyme amounts are unchanged.
  4. The third arrangement, with 59 nmol of final product.
19
Which control most directly excludes a difference in enzyme abundance as the reason for the observed product yields?
  1. Measure and match total amounts of enzyme X and enzyme Y across all arrangements.
  2. Measure only the rates of product loss, with no assay of X or Y amounts.
  3. Keep the total enzyme amount constant but allow the X:Y ratio to change in each group.
  4. Match the initial substrate but supply different quantities of enzyme X across groups.
20
Which conclusion is not established by the results?
  1. The co-localized arrangement yields more final product than the dispersed one.
  2. The data show differences in pathway yield under the three conditions.
  3. The two enzymes must form a stable protein-protein complex when localized together.
  4. The third arrangement has an intermediate final-product yield.
