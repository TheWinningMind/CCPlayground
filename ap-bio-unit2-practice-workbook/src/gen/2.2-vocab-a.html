Learning objective(s): 2.2.A • 25 retrieval prompts • Answers with explanations

01. Surface area — For a cube, surface area = 6 × side length².

02. Volume — For a cube, volume = side length³.

03. Surface area-to-volume ratio (SA:V) — A greater SA:V generally offers more exchange area per unit volume.

04. Linear dimension (edge length) — Geometrical scaling begins with the object's linear dimensions.

05. Square-law scaling — Surface area scales with the square of linear size.

06. Cube-law scaling — Volume scales with the cube of linear size.

07. Diffusion distance — Longer diffusion pathways generally increase the time needed for material to move.

08. Metabolic demand — Larger cellular volumes generally require more total resources for maintenance.

09. Membrane exchange capacity — Exchange depends on available membrane surface and the transport conditions.

10. Exchange surface — A large exchange surface can improve transport capacity.

11. Microvilli — Microvilli increase the surface available for absorption.

12. Root hair — A root hair increases the surface area available for water and mineral uptake.

13. Membrane infolding — Membrane folds increase surface area relative to the enclosed volume.

14. Flattened cell shape — Flattening can decrease diffusion distance and improve SA:V.

15. Cell elongation — Elongation can modify SA:V without simply enlarging all dimensions.

16. Sphere — A sphere minimizes surface area for a fixed volume.

17. Agar-cube diffusion model — Gel cubes make diffusion distance and fraction penetrated observable.

18. Diffusion front — The front's position can be used to estimate penetration.

19. Penetration depth — Depth of penetration differs from the percentage of the cube's total volume penetrated.

20. Fraction (or percentage) of volume penetrated — The same penetration depth can affect different proportions of differently sized cubes.

21. Heat loss (heat exchange) — Greater surface area per unit volume can increase potential heat exchange.

22. Cell division — Separating a large cell into smaller cells can improve their exchange area relative to volume.

23. 54 μm² — The six faces each have area 9 μm²: 6 × 9 = 54 μm².

24. 27 μm³ — Volume of a cube = side³ = 27 μm³.

25. 2 μm⁻¹ (equivalently, 2:1 with consistent length units) — SA:V = 54/27 = 2 μm⁻¹; an SA:V ratio has inverse-length units when expressed as a quotient.
