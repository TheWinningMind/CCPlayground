Item-by-item explanations

1. Correct answer: B | 2.3.A.1

The phosphate-containing head is polar and faces aqueous surroundings.

Distractor review: Choice A identifies hydrophobic chains, not the water-contacting polar head; C describes the nonpolar membrane center; D is a protein region, not part of a phospholipid.

2. Correct answer: C | 2.3.A.1

Hydrophilic heads face cytosol and extracellular fluid; hydrophobic tails face each other.

Distractor review: A, B, and D expose nonpolar hydrocarbon chains or place polar heads away from water, which contradicts amphipathic self-organization.

3. Correct answer: A | 2.3.A.1

Phospholipids contain polar hydrophilic heads and nonpolar hydrophobic tails.

Distractor review: B, C, and D do not define molecules containing both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions; amphipathicity concerns compatibility with water, not merely charge.

4. Correct answer: D | 2.3.B.1

The phospholipid bilayer forms the fundamental framework into which other membrane components are embedded.

Distractor review: Carbohydrates, proteins, and cholesterol contribute to the mosaic, but none replaces the phospholipid structural bilayer.

5. Correct answer: B | 2.3.A.2

A protein surface in contact with the fatty acid interior is typically hydrophobic.

Distractor review: Water-exposed surfaces in A/C favor polar or charged residues. An extracellular carbohydrate chain is not the lipid-contacting portion of a spanning protein.

6. Correct answer: C | 2.3.A.2

Cytosol-exposed protein surfaces can interact with water through polar/charged regions.

Distractor review: A and B would make the protein poorly compatible with cytosolic water; D confuses extracellular carbohydrate decoration with core protein chemistry.

7. Correct answer: A | 2.3.B.1

Cholesterol is a steroid present in vertebrate animal plasma membranes.

Distractor review: A glycolipid and D glycoprotein include carbohydrates, whereas C is the principal lipid framework rather than a steroid.

8. Correct answer: D | 2.3.B.1

A glycoprotein is a protein with covalently attached carbohydrate groups.

Distractor review: A describes a phospholipid; B a steroid; C a lipid with no attached carbohydrate; only D has a carbohydrate linked to protein.

9. Correct answer: B | 2.3.B.1

The carbohydrate of a glycolipid is attached to a lipid, not a protein.

Distractor review: A is a protein conjugate. C and D are neither the lipid-linked carbohydrate group defined by the question nor glycosylated membrane lipids.

10. Correct answer: C | 2.3.B.1

The membrane has a dynamic arrangement of lipids and embedded molecules that often diffuse laterally.

Distractor review: A and B describe incorrect immobility, while D incorrectly replaces the lipid framework with only proteins.

11. Correct answer: A | 2.3.A.1

The nonpolar fatty acid tails create a hydrophobic bilayer interior.

Distractor review: B and D refer to aqueous environments; C refers to polar heads. The hydrocarbon tail region is the hydrophobic environment.

12. Correct answer: D | 2.3.A.2

Protein regions exposed to aqueous fluid often have polar and charged residues.

Distractor review: A and B assume a membrane protein surface contacting water must be nonpolar, contrary to hydrophilic interactions; C confuses protein and carbohydrate.

13. Correct answer: B | 2.3.A.1

The arrangement reverses the hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails.

Distractor review: A, C, and D discuss other properties rather than the reversed placement of phospholipid head/tail regions.

14. Correct answer: A | 2.3.B.1

Lateral relocation provides direct support for component mobility in a membrane.

Distractor review: B describes membrane boundary function; C describes composition; D chemical heterogeneity. None directly tests lateral motion.

15. Correct answer: C | 2.3.B.1

Mosaic refers to varied components associated with the phospholipid framework.

Distractor review: A ignores molecular diversity; B mistakes the membrane for an organelle; D reverses the location of the carbohydrate and bilayer molecules.

16. Correct answer: D | 2.3.B.1

The model includes steroids such as cholesterol within vertebrate animal membranes.

Distractor review: A, B, and C are not standard components of a fluid mosaic depiction; the membrane remains a lipid-based structure.

17. Correct answer: B | 2.3.A.1

The inner leaflet faces the aqueous cytosol.

Distractor review: The inner leaflet faces cytosol, not outer leaflet tails, bilayer center, or extracellular glycoconjugates.

18. Correct answer: A | 2.3.A.2

Nonpolar groups favor interactions with the fatty acid tail environment.

Distractor review: B is chemically implausible for nonpolar surfaces; C wrongly universalizes nonpolarity on aqueous faces; D assumes carbohydrates are required.

19. Correct answer: C | 2.3.B.1

Carbohydrate attached to lipid is a glycolipid; attached to protein is a glycoprotein.

Distractor review: A indicates protein-linked carbohydrate; B steroid; D a nucleic acid bond. None is a carbohydrate-bearing lipid.

20. Correct answer: D | 2.3.A.1

Both sides of the phospholipid bilayer face aqueous environments and display hydrophilic head groups.

Distractor review: A and C expose water-avoiding tails or misplace polar heads; B describes a single layer rather than a bilayer.

21. Correct answer: A | 2.3.A.2

Region B contacts the fatty acid tails, which favor a nonpolar protein surface; adding charge there reduces compatibility.

Distractor review: B/C are hydrophilic-for-hydrophilic substitutions in water-exposed regions; D concerns an external carbohydrate and not the lipid-contacting protein surface.

22. Correct answer: B | 2.3.A.2

Detection falls from 88% to 31% despite comparable synthesis; substitutions are associated with reduced membrane localization.

Distractor review: A misstates protein composition, C was not measured, and D contradicts the stated comparable synthesis.

23. Correct answer: C | 2.3.A.2

A substitution preserving polarity at a water-exposed surface need not disrupt insertion/localization.

Distractor review: A assumes every polar substitution disrupts localization; B moves an unchanged membrane region without cause; D confuses protein with carbohydrate-bearing lipid.

24. Correct answer: D | 2.3.A.2

Matched total protein abundance plus selective depletion from the membrane makes altered localization the stronger explanation.

Distractor review: Cell volume and ribosome number are too indirect, while the mere presence of phospholipids does not isolate the mechanism.

25. Correct answer: A | 2.3.A.2

C faces the aqueous cytosol and therefore can display hydrophilic amino acid side chains.

Distractor review: B incorrectly makes a protein segment into lipid, C switches cytosol and extracellular fluid, and D confuses protein with a steroid.

26. Correct answer: B | 2.3.B.1

Recovery of signal after localized photobleaching is consistent with lateral movement of unbleached labeled proteins.

Distractor review: New synthesis is explicitly negligible; fluorescence recovery means bleached fluorophores remain bleached, and it does not establish organismal identity.

27. Correct answer: C | 2.3.B.1

A rises from 39% to 65%, a 26-point increase; B rises from 18% to 24%, a 6-point increase.

Distractor review: A and B give the wrong comparison; D confuses the final value (65) with change over the specified interval.

28. Correct answer: D | 2.3.B.1

The data cover only 60 seconds, so permanent immobility is not established; some recovery does occur.

Distractor review: A, B, and C are supported numerically; D overextends the limited observation window to a permanent molecular state.

29. Correct answer: A | 2.3.B.1

Average rate=(75−15)/60 = 1.00 percentage point/s.

Distractor review: B uses the initial percentage as a rate; C reverses the division; D treats the 60-second interval as if it were itself the recovery in percentage points.

30. Correct answer: B | 2.3.B.1

The controlled intervention directly tests whether restricting mobility reduces recovery.

Distractor review: Changing microscopes, measuring prebleach only, or counting chromosomes does not directly manipulate lateral protein mobility.

31. Correct answer: A | 2.3.B.1

The mobile fraction rises from 61% to 76%, an increase of 15 percentage points.

Distractor review: B reverses the sign; C divides by a different reference and misstates percentage points; D ignores the measured change.

32. Correct answer: C | 2.3.B.1

Treatment and control differ in cholesterol content and measured mobility; generalization to all conditions is unwarranted.

Distractor review: A, B, and D overclaim universal membrane effects or invent changes in unmeasured components.

33. Correct answer: D | 2.3.B.1

Protein expression is a potential confound; confirming it is constant helps isolate the intended variable.

Distractor review: Water temperature and mitochondrial DNA are unrelated controls; a carbohydrate chain on another cell does not address protein abundance.

34. Correct answer: B | 2.3.B.1

A measurable mobile fraction in both conditions contradicts the absolute claim that every component is fixed.

Distractor review: A assumes cholesterol proves rigidity; C invents missing phospholipids; D assumes identical diffusion of all proteins.

35. Correct answer: A | 2.3.B.1

A context-dependent response is a reasonable interpretation, not a universal conclusion.

Distractor review: B ignores the contradictory second condition; C and D infer unsupported changes to membrane identities and bilayer existence.

36. Correct answer: C | 2.3.B.1

Probe P binds carbohydrate attached to proteins, the identifying property of glycoproteins.

Distractor review: Phospholipids without sugar and cholesterol lack the targeted protein-linked carbohydrate; nonglycosylated proteins lack Probe P targets.

37. Correct answer: D | 2.3.B.1

(80−24)/80×100 = 70%.

Distractor review: The absolute change of 56 fluorescence units is not 56%; values of 24 or 60% do not equal the relative decrease from 80.

38. Correct answer: A | 2.3.B.1

The P signal declines sharply while the L signal changes from 52 to 50 units, consistent with relative specificity for protein-linked carbohydrates.

Distractor review: B/C predict different probes changes than observed; D asserts a structural consequence not measured.

39. Correct answer: B | 2.3.B.1

The glyco- prefix indicates a carbohydrate; the type of attached molecule distinguishes glycolipid from glycoprotein.

Distractor review: A confuses the structure with a steroid, C with a ribosome, and D with a protein despite the specified lipid linkage.

40. Correct answer: C | 2.3.B.1

A binding-competition control helps test whether the fluorescent probe signal depends on its intended carbohydrate recognition.

Distractor review: A and B fail to test specific binding, whereas D introduces uncontrolled environmental differences rather than a probe-specific comparison.
