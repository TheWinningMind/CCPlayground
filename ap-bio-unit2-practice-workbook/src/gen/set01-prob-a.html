Problem 1 | Pulse–chase tracing of a secreted protein

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Time (min) Rough ER (%) Golgi (%) Secretory vesicles (%) Outside cell (%)
0 72 18 10 0
5 42 35 18 5
15 10 18 25 47

(a) Describe TWO time-dependent changes in label distribution. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The rough-ER fraction decreases from 72% to 10% between 0 and 15 min.

  • Point 2: The extracellular fraction increases from 0% to 47% over the same interval.

(b) Explain the roles of rough ER and Golgi apparatus in the proposed route. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Ribosomes associated with rough ER synthesize proteins directed into the secretory pathway, and the ER supports their processing.

  • Point 2: The Golgi modifies/sorts these proteins before they are sent onward.

(c) Predict the pattern at 15 min if vesicle export from the Golgi is blocked, and justify the prediction. [3 points]

  • Point 1: A greater fraction of labeled protein accumulates in the Golgi than in the untreated cells.

  • Point 2: A smaller fraction reaches secretory vesicles and/or extracellular medium.

  • Point 3: Blocking export prevents labeled cargo from progressing from the Golgi to downstream destinations.

(d) Identify a suitable negative control and explain its value. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Use cells in which the labeling step was omitted (or an equivalent unlabeled sample) as a negative control.

  • Point 2: This estimates fluorescence/background signal not attributable to newly labeled proteins.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 4 | Lysosomal acidity experiment

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Condition Mean lysosomal pH Particles degraded (%)
Control 4.8 70
Inhibited pump 6.2 25

(a) Calculate the percentage-point change in the fraction degraded between groups. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The difference is 70 − 25 = 45 percentage points.

  • Point 2: This is an absolute percentage-point difference, not a 45% relative change.

Worked calculation: 70% − 25% = 45 percentage points.

(b) Explain why changing lysosomal pH can affect digestion. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Lysosomal digestive enzymes function efficiently in an acidic compartment.

  • Point 2: Increasing pH can reduce enzyme activity and slow breakdown of engulfed materials.

(c) Propose one additional measurement to test whether the reduced degradation is due to acidification rather than fewer particles entering cells. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Measure the initial number of particles taken up per cell in both conditions.

  • Point 2: Keep the exposure duration and particle concentration equivalent.

  • Point 3: Compare degradation after normalizing for the amount engulfed; equal uptake but lower degradation implicates lysosomal function.

(d) Predict how intracellular undegraded material changes and justify. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Undegraded material will accumulate to a greater extent in the inhibited group.

  • Point 2: Less efficient hydrolysis leaves more engulfed contents within compartments.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.
