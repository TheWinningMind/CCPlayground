Cell Compartmentalization: Reaction Environments

Problem 1 | Localized enzyme activity and pH

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Assay pH Enzyme activity (units/min)
5.0 82
7.0 21

(a) Calculate the difference in reaction rate between the two buffers. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The rate is 82 − 21 = 61 units/min higher at pH 5.

  • Point 2: The absolute difference is expressed in the supplied units.

(b) Explain why maintaining a distinct organelle environment can be advantageous. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The compartment maintains conditions suitable for its specialized enzymes.

  • Point 2: The cytosol can simultaneously maintain a different pH and support other processes.

(c) Propose a way to test whether an intact organelle membrane is necessary to maintain the low internal pH. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Measure lumenal pH using a probe targeted to the compartment.

  • Point 2: Compare intact organelles with organelles whose membranes are selectively disrupted.

  • Point 3: Keep external buffer conditions and measurement timing consistent.

(d) Predict the enzyme activity after membrane disruption if lumenal pH approaches 7. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The enzyme activity would be expected to decline.

  • Point 2: The pH 7 assay showed a much lower rate for this enzyme.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 2 | Separating two incompatible reactions

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify one organizational feature that can allow both reactions. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Membrane-bound compartments with distinct internal conditions.

(b) Explain the role of the separating membrane. [1 point]

  • Point 1: It limits mixing and helps maintain different pH environments.

(c) Predict a consequence of eliminating the separation. [1 point]

  • Point 1: At least one reaction may operate less efficiently.

(d) State why both reactions need not be in different cells. [1 point]

  • Point 1: A single cell can localize reactions in different intracellular compartments.

Total for Problem 2: 4 points.

Problem 4 | The consequences of breaking a compartment barrier

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Condition Compartment pH Conversion rate (units/min)
Intact membrane 5.0 75
Proton-permeable membrane 6.5 28

(a) Describe the treatment-associated changes. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Internal pH rises from 5.0 to 6.5.

  • Point 2: Conversion rate falls from 75 to 28 units/min.

(b) Explain a mechanism connecting the two observations. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The treatment reduces the ability to maintain a distinct proton concentration.

  • Point 2: The resulting pH shift moves the enzyme away from its optimal conditions.

(c) Propose a test to distinguish loss of enzyme protein from loss of a favorable environment. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Recover equal amounts of enzyme from treated and untreated compartments.

  • Point 2: Assay both preparations at the same optimal pH.

  • Point 3: Similar enzyme activity under common assay conditions supports environmental rather than enzyme-loss effects.

(d) Predict whether selectively restoring acidic lumenal conditions should improve activity. [2 points]

  • Point 1: It should improve activity if pH disruption is the limiting cause.

  • Point 2: This prediction assumes the enzyme and other required components remain intact.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.

Problem 5 | Preventing pathway interference

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify the advantage of separating the enzymes. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The separation can prevent immediate degradation of P.

(b) Predict an effect if the enzymes are mixed and simultaneously active. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Less P may accumulate.

(c) Explain how membrane separation controls the interaction. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Compartment membranes limit unintended contact between the enzymes and their substrates.

(d) State a limitation of assuming compartmentalization always increases reaction rate. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Movement of reactants between compartments and other costs can also limit throughput.

Total for Problem 5: 4 points.

(c) Explain a potential functional benefit. [1 point]

(d) State one empirical measurement needed to support an increase in actual cellular performance. [1 point]
