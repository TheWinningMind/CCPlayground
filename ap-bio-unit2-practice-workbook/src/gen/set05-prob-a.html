Membrane Transport: Gradients and Vesicles

Problem 1 | Energy-dependent accumulation in cultured cells

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Treatment Internal solute at 20 min (units)
Normal ATP supply 64
ATP-depleted 19

(a) Describe the treatment effect on intracellular solute abundance. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Control cells reach 64 relative units after 20 min.

  • Point 2: ATP-depleted cells reach only 19 units, a decrease of 45 units.

(b) Explain why net uptake under the specified concentration gradient is evidence for an energy-dependent process. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Net solute uptake occurs from lower outside concentration to higher inside concentration.

  • Point 2: Movement against the solute concentration gradient requires an energy-coupled transport mechanism.

(c) Propose an experiment to distinguish ATP limitation from direct toxic destruction of the membrane. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Measure membrane integrity using an impermeant fluorescent dye.

  • Point 2: Include an ATP-depleted group whose ATP supply is subsequently restored.

  • Point 3: Recovery of uphill uptake with intact membranes supports dependence on cellular energy availability.

(d) Predict the effect of complete loss of the required transport protein. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Uphill uptake should decline markedly or cease under the stated conditions.

  • Point 2: The specific transport protein is needed to couple energy expenditure to accumulation.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 2 | A concentration gradient without ATP input

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Predict the initial net direction of movement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Into the cell.

(b) Identify the energy source for the net movement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The preexisting concentration gradient; no direct ATP input is required.

(c) Predict the direction when the concentrations become equal. [1 point]

  • Point 1: There is no net direction; molecules still move in both directions.

(d) Explain why equal concentrations do not mean all movement stops. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Molecules continue random thermal motion, but opposing fluxes balance.

Total for Problem 2: 4 points.

Problem 3 | Engulfing a particulate nutrient

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify the type of bulk transport. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Phagocytosis, a form of endocytosis.

(b) Describe the change in plasma membrane shape. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The plasma membrane surrounds and pinches off around the particle.

(c) Identify the destination of the particle immediately after engulfment. [1 point]

  • Point 1: An intracellular vesicle/phagosome.

(d) Explain why direct diffusion is not a suitable mechanism for engulfing the particle. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The particle is too large to traverse the phospholipid bilayer as a dissolved molecule.

Total for Problem 3: 4 points.

Problem 4 | Vesicle-fusion inhibitor and secreted protein

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Condition Protein released (relative units)
Control 80
Fusion inhibitor 20

(a) Calculate the relative percentage decrease in secretion under treatment. [2 points]

  • Point 1: (80 − 20)/80 × 100 = 75%.

  • Point 2: The untreated value is the reference for the relative change.

(b) Explain what stage of secretion is most directly affected. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Vesicles fail to fuse effectively with the plasma membrane.

  • Point 2: Cargo therefore remains within the cell rather than being released outside.

(c) Describe a second measurement that would strengthen the mechanism inference. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Quantify the number or volume of intracellular secretory vesicles.

  • Point 2: Compare treated and untreated cells at the same time.

  • Point 3: Accumulated cargo-filled vesicles would be consistent with a fusion block.

(d) Predict the outcome if the compound were removed and vesicle fusion recovered. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Extracellular secretion should increase relative to continued inhibition.

  • Point 2: Stored cargo can reach the cell exterior when fusion resumes.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.

Problem 5 | Solute movement and steady state

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) State what a dynamic steady state implies about molecular motion. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Individual ions continue moving across the membrane.

(b) Describe what is equal at a state of no net ion flux. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The opposing directional ion fluxes are equal.

(c) Identify a factor besides concentration that may affect ion movement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Electrical potential difference across the membrane.

(d) Explain why membrane-protein selectivity matters. [1 point]

  • Point 1: A channel must provide a suitable pathway for the ion to cross the nonpolar bilayer core.

Total for Problem 5: 4 points.

Problem 6 | Competing proposed mechanisms

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Name the likely type of internalization. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Receptor-mediated endocytosis.

(b) Explain the role of receptor binding. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Receptor binding selectively concentrates target cargo for uptake.

(c) Identify the membrane structure formed during uptake. [1 point]

  • Point 1: An endocytic vesicle.

(d) Predict an effect of reducing surface receptor abundance. [1 point]

  • Point 1: At the same extracellular concentration, receptor-dependent uptake is expected to decrease.

Total for Problem 6: 4 points.
