Membrane Permeability and Cell-Wall Protection

Problem 1 | Analyzing an artificial bilayer

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Solute Net inward flux (relative units)
O₂ 94
CO₂ 90
Urea 18
Glucose 3
Na⁺ 0.1

(a) Identify the two substances showing the greatest flux. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Oxygen has the highest flux (94 units).

  • Point 2: Carbon dioxide has the second highest (90 units).

(b) Explain why sodium ions cross this bilayer poorly. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Na⁺ is charged and surrounded by water.

  • Point 2: The nonpolar bilayer core is an unfavorable barrier to direct passage.

(c) Predict what will happen to glucose flux if a correctly oriented glucose transport protein is inserted. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Glucose flux is expected to increase.

  • Point 2: The protein provides a pathway through the membrane.

  • Point 3: Any increase depends on protein functionality and the glucose gradient.

(d) Justify why molecular mass alone cannot fully explain the flux ordering. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Charge and polarity influence bilayer partitioning in addition to size.

  • Point 2: A small ion can be less permeable than a larger nonpolar molecule.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 2 | The function of a cell wall

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify which cell has a rigid wall. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Plant cell.

(b) Predict the initial net water movement in both cells. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Into both cells.

(c) Identify the structure resisting unlimited expansion in the plant cell. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Cell wall.

(d) Explain why the wall does not replace selective permeability of the membrane. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The wall provides mechanical support, whereas the plasma membrane remains the selective permeability barrier.

Total for Problem 2: 4 points.

Problem 4 | Separating passage through the lipid phase from a protein pathway

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Membrane Relative permeability
No added protein 5
Candidate channel added 45

(a) Calculate the fold increase in permeability after adding the channel. [2 points]

  • Point 1: 45 ÷ 5 = 9-fold.

  • Point 2: The comparison uses the protein-free permeability as the baseline.

(b) Interpret the result in terms of membrane structure. [2 points]

  • Point 1: A protein pathway increases movement through the membrane.

  • Point 2: The bilayer alone has limited permeability to this solute.

(c) Design a test of whether the channel itself causes the increase. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Include a membrane reconstituted with an inactive mutant channel as a control.

  • Point 2: Keep channel abundance, bilayer composition, and gradient constant.

  • Point 3: A low permeability with the inactive mutant supports a channel-dependent effect.

(d) Explain why the experiment does not by itself establish that transport is active. [2 points]

  • Point 1: No ATP-driven or uphill transport has been demonstrated.

  • Point 2: The observed increase may represent passive facilitated movement down a gradient.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.

Problem 5 | Wall composition and function

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Name a common structural component of plant cell walls. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Cellulose.

(b) Name a common structural component of fungal cell walls. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Chitin.

(c) State a shared function of the walls. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Mechanical support and protection against osmotic lysis.

(d) Explain why the different chemical compositions do not imply identical permeability properties. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Composition affects wall architecture and porosity; both remain distinct from the selective plasma membrane.

Total for Problem 5: 4 points.

Problem 6 | Relative permeability predictions

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Predict which solute shows greater direct bilayer permeation. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Oxygen.

(b) Explain the prediction using molecular properties. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Oxygen is small and nonpolar; the charged dye is disfavored in the hydrophobic core.

(c) Identify why volume equality alone does not guarantee equal flux. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Permeability differs by solute properties.

(d) State a measurement needed to calculate a true flux per unit membrane area. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Quantity crossing per unit time divided by membrane area, with consistent units.
