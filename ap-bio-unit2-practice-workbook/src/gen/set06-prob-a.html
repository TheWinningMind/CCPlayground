Facilitated Diffusion: Channel and Carrier Experiments

Problem 1 | Transport saturation and concentration

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

External glucose (mM) Line A uptake Line B uptake
0 0 0
2 8 4
8 15 8
16 16 9

(a) Calculate the rate increase for Line A as concentration rises from 2 to 8 mM. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The rate increases from 8 to 15 units.

  • Point 2: The increase equals 7 units or (7/8)×100 = 87.5%.

(b) Describe evidence for saturation in Line A. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Its rate rises only from 15 to 16 units as concentration doubles from 8 to 16 mM.

  • Point 2: This leveling is consistent with a finite number of carrier proteins.

(c) Explain why Line B has lower rates at every tested concentration if it expresses fewer functional carriers. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Fewer carriers provide fewer binding/translocation sites.

  • Point 2: With otherwise similar conditions, transport capacity is reduced.

  • Point 3: The rate difference does not demonstrate direct ATP consumption by the carriers.

(d) Predict whether an ATP-synthesis inhibitor must stop all uptake immediately, and justify. [2 points]

  • Point 1: No; facilitated diffusion itself does not directly require ATP hydrolysis.

  • Point 2: Uptake can continue while the solute gradient and functional carriers persist.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 2 | Aquaporin abundance

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify the transported substance. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Water.

(b) Predict the effect on initial water movement rate. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The aquaporin-rich line may have a higher initial water movement rate.

(c) Explain whether increased aquaporins necessarily change the direction of net water movement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: No; direction is set by the water-potential difference, not channel number.

(d) State one variable to match across groups. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Water-potential difference or external solute concentration, and temperature.

Total for Problem 2: 4 points.

Problem 4 | A competing substrate reduces uptake

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Y concentration (mM) X uptake rate (units)
0 20
2 17
6 12
12 8

(a) Calculate the percentage change when Y is added at 6 mM. [2 points]

  • Point 1: (20 − 12)/20 × 100 = 40% decrease.

  • Point 2: Rate is compared with the no-Y control.

(b) Explain why the data support competitive binding. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Increasing Y concentration reduces X transport because Y may occupy sites used by X.

  • Point 2: The data alone are also compatible with some alternative inhibitory mechanisms.

(c) Design a test to distinguish competition from irreversible carrier destruction. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Remove Y and remeasure X uptake using the same external X concentration.

  • Point 2: Include matched untreated carriers.

  • Point 3: Recovery after Y removal supports a reversible effect rather than permanent damage.

(d) Explain why uptake remains passive under the scenario. [2 points]

  • Point 1: X travels down its concentration gradient.

  • Point 2: No direct energy input is needed for carrier-mediated movement in the stated experiment.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.

Problem 5 | Reversal of a permeant-solute gradient

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Predict the new direction of net carrier-mediated movement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Down the reversed concentration gradient.

(b) State whether the carrier necessarily becomes an ATP-driven pump. [1 point]

  • Point 1: No.

(c) Explain the direction change. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The net direction is determined by the solute gradient for passive carrier transport.

(d) Identify a condition that yields no net solute movement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Equal effective concentrations on both sides, in the absence of other driving forces.

Total for Problem 5: 4 points.

Problem 6 | Channel closure and membrane flux

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Predict the initial ion flux with the channel blocked. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Lower than in the open-channel condition.

(b) Identify what must be measured to distinguish permeability from gradient change. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The ion concentrations on both sides or the membrane potential, as relevant.

(c) State a control for nonspecific effects of the blocking agent. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Use an inactive chemical analog or an untreated matched sample.

(d) Explain why ion movement through an open channel does not directly require ATP. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Channel-mediated flux uses the existing electrochemical gradient.

Total for Problem 6: 4 points.
