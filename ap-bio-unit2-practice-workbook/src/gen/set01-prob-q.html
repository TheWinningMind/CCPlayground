Problem 1 | Pulse–chase tracing of a secreted protein

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 3.B, 4.B, 6.E

Researchers pulse-label newly synthesized secretory proteins in cultured animal cells, then follow the label during a chase with unlabeled amino acids. The entries are percentages of total detectable label at each time; measurements are illustrative.

Figure M01.1: Original instructional model. Labels are intentional, not to physical scale.

Time (min) Rough ER (%) Golgi (%) Secretory vesicles (%) Outside cell (%)
0 72 18 10 0
5 42 35 18 5
15 10 18 25 47

(a) Describe TWO time-dependent changes in label distribution. [2 points]

(b) Explain the roles of rough ER and Golgi apparatus in the proposed route. [2 points]

(c) Predict the pattern at 15 min if vesicle export from the Golgi is blocked, and justify the prediction. [3 points]

(d) Identify a suitable negative control and explain its value. [2 points]

Problem 2 | A cell with abundant smooth ER

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.E

Electron micrographs show one cell type with extensive smooth ER and relatively little rough ER. A second cell type has extensive rough ER.

(a) Identify the organelle enriched in the first cell. [1 point]

(b) State a principal function of the smooth ER. [1 point]

(c) Predict which cell is better equipped to export many soluble proteins. [1 point]

(d) Justify the prediction from structure. [1 point]

Problem 3 | Inner membrane folding and ATP demand

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 2.A, 6.C

Mitochondria from two otherwise comparable cells have equal outer dimensions. Type X has much more highly folded inner membranes than type Y.

(a) Identify the folds of the mitochondrial inner membrane. [1 point]

(b) Predict which type has greater membrane area available for membrane-associated ATP production. [1 point]

(c) Explain why the fold pattern could be advantageous. [1 point]

(d) State one limitation of inferring actual ATP production from fold area alone. [1 point]

Problem 4 | Lysosomal acidity experiment

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 3.C, 5.A, 6.E

Scientists compare cells with functional lysosomal acidification and cells with an inhibited proton pump. A fluorescent substrate reports the fraction of engulfed particles degraded after 30 minutes.

Condition Mean lysosomal pH Particles degraded (%)
Control 4.8 70
Inhibited pump 6.2 25

(a) Calculate the percentage-point change in the fraction degraded between groups. [2 points]

(b) Explain why changing lysosomal pH can affect digestion. [2 points]

(c) Propose one additional measurement to test whether the reduced degradation is due to acidification rather than fewer particles entering cells. [3 points]

(d) Predict how intracellular undegraded material changes and justify. [2 points]

Problem 5 | Central vacuoles and plant cell support

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.E

Two plant cell preparations have similar cell walls, but one contains a large water-filled central vacuole and the other has lost substantial vacuolar water.

(a) Name the organelle in which most of the described water is stored. [1 point]

(b) Identify the membrane surrounding this organelle. [1 point]

(c) Predict the relative turgor pressure in the water-depleted preparation. [1 point]

(d) Explain how the vacuole supports plant structure. [1 point]

Problem 6 | Common cellular structures

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.B

Investigators detect ribosomes in bacterial cells, animal cells, and plant cells. They also observe membrane-bound chloroplasts only in the sampled plant cells.

(a) Identify the nonmembrane-bound structure detected in all three cell types. [1 point]

(b) State the principal cellular process carried out by this structure. [1 point]

(c) Describe a chloroplast feature connected to light capture. [1 point]

(d) Explain why the presence of ribosomes across all three samples is consistent with common ancestry. [1 point]
