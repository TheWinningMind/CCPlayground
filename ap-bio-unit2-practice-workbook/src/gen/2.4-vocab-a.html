Learning objective(s): 2.4.A–2.4.B • 25 retrieval prompts • Answers with explanations

01. Selective permeability — Selective permeability depends strongly on the membrane's hydrophobic interior and transport proteins.

02. Hydrophobic membrane interior — Ions cannot readily move through the nonpolar membrane core.

03. Simple diffusion — Small nonpolar molecules can diffuse through the bilayer directly.

04. Oxygen (O₂) — Oxygen is small and nonpolar, so it crosses the membrane readily.

05. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — Carbon dioxide can cross the hydrophobic core of membranes without a transporter.

06. Nitrogen gas (N₂) — Nitrogen gas is a small nonpolar molecule.

07. Small nonpolar molecule — Size and nonpolarity favor diffusion through the hydrophobic interior.

08. Large polar molecule — Large polar solutes usually require transport proteins to move efficiently across membranes.

09. Ion — Charge makes direct passage through nonpolar lipid tails unfavorable.

10. Small uncharged polar molecule — Small uncharged polar molecules show limited passage through lipids.

11. Water — Water can permeate lipids to a limited extent; aquaporins support rapid movement.

12. Ammonia — The uncharged form of ammonia may permeate the bilayer.

13. Channel protein — Channels provide a selective route through the membrane.

14. Transport protein — Transport proteins enable selective passage of certain solutes.

15. Membrane permeability — Different solutes can have different membrane permeabilities.

16. Hydrocarbon (fatty-acid) tails — Nonpolar tails create the hydrophobic barrier.

17. Cell wall — Cell walls support cells and can protect them from bursting.

18. Cellulose — Cellulose microfibrils provide tensile strength to plant walls.

19. Chitin — Fungal walls commonly contain chitin.

20. Peptidoglycan — Peptidoglycan provides structural strength in bacteria.

21. Archaeal cell wall — Archaeal cell walls can contain proteins or other polymers but lack bacterial peptidoglycan.

22. Osmotic lysis — A supportive cell wall can help prevent osmotic lysis.

23. Structural support — A wall provides a load-bearing boundary outside the plasma membrane.

24. Protein-dependent permeability — Enhanced movement with the appropriate protein indicates a protein-mediated pathway.

25. Glucose — Glucose is hydrophilic and passes poorly through the lipid core unaided.

Teacher note: Award one point for each conceptually correct answer. Accept standard biological synonyms and appropriate notation, but use the clue’s defining detail to distinguish closely related structures and mechanisms.
