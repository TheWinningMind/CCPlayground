Learning objective(s): 2.5.A–2.5.B • 25 retrieval prompts • Answers with explanations

01. Concentration gradient — A concentration gradient creates a tendency for net passive movement down the gradient.

02. Passive transport — Passive transport includes simple and facilitated diffusion.

03. Simple diffusion — This form of passive transport does not require a membrane protein.

04. Active transport — Active transport requires energy and can establish or maintain gradients.

05. ATP (adenosine triphosphate) — ATP hydrolysis can directly power membrane transport proteins.

06. Net movement (net flux) — Net flux can be zero even while individual molecules continue to move.

07. Dynamic equilibrium — Random molecular motion continues despite zero net transfer.

08. Down the concentration gradient — Down-gradient movement does not directly require metabolic energy.

09. Against the concentration gradient — Moving against the gradient cannot occur by simple passive diffusion alone.

10. Endocytosis — Endocytosis is an energy-requiring form of vesicular uptake.

11. Exocytosis — Exocytosis expels cargo through vesicle fusion with the cell surface.

12. Membrane invagination — Invagination can enclose extracellular material during endocytosis.

13. Vesicle — Vesicular transport handles cargo beyond the capacity of simple lipid diffusion.

14. Vesicle fusion — Fusion is central to exocytosis.

15. Phagocytosis — Phagocytosis is a specialized type of endocytosis.

16. Pinocytosis — Pinocytosis is a specialized type of endocytosis.

17. Bulk (vesicular) transport — Endocytosis and exocytosis are major examples of bulk transport.

18. Facilitated diffusion — Facilitated diffusion is passive but requires a suitable membrane protein.

19. Selective permeability — Selective membranes can maintain differences between interior and exterior solute concentrations.

20. Metabolic energy input — Active transport requires an energy source.

21. Solute balance — Solute balance helps cells maintain stable internal conditions.

22. Secretion — Many secreted macromolecules leave cells through exocytosis.

23. Engulfment — Engulfment occurs in the formation of endocytic vesicles.

24. Vesicle-mediated export — Vesicle-mediated export is a way to deliver macromolecular cargo outside the cell.

25. Vesicle budding — Budding creates a discrete cargo-containing vesicle.

Teacher note: Award one point for each conceptually correct answer. Accept standard biological synonyms and appropriate notation, but use the clue’s defining detail to distinguish closely related structures and mechanisms.
