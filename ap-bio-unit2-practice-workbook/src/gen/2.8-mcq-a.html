Section A — Independent Questions

1. Correct answer: A — Primary active transport couples ion movement to a source of metabolic energy; facilitated diffusion does not.

Both processes can use transport proteins. Primary active transport couples transport to energy input and can move a solute against its electrochemical gradient.

2. Correct answer: C — Three Na⁺ out and two K⁺ in.

The sodium–potassium pump exports three Na⁺ and imports two K⁺ per ATP-hydrolyzing cycle.

3. Correct answer: B — The ion flux declines after ATP is depleted and is restored when ATP availability is restored.

A reversible ATP-dependence experiment supports a requirement for metabolic energy; the other observations do not distinguish active from passive transport.

4. Correct answer: D — The proteins provide a selective pathway and couple ion movement to the energy source.

The hydrophobic interior of the bilayer restricts passage of ions; an ATP-driven protein permits selective and directional ion movement.

5. Correct answer: B — An electrical potential difference between the two sides of a membrane.

Membrane potential is a voltage across the membrane caused by separation of electric charge.

6. Correct answer: A — It reflects both the ion concentration difference and the electrical potential difference across the membrane.

The ion’s net driving force depends on its chemical gradient and on the effect of voltage on the charged ion.

7. Correct answer: D — It moves a net one positive charge outward.

Export of 3 Na⁺ and import of 2 K⁺ moves one more positive charge out than in; the pump is electrogenic.

8. Correct answer: C — Passive movement of ions through an open channel.

Channel-mediated diffusion is passive. A pre-existing gradient can drive it transiently even if ATP-dependent pumps are inhibited.

9. Correct answer: C — The chemical gradient favors outward K⁺ movement, but the net electrochemical driving force cannot be determined without voltage information.

Concentration alone predicts the chemical component; electrical forces may reinforce or oppose it.

10. Correct answer: B — The pump will fail to carry out normal ATP-coupled ion transport despite remaining in the membrane.

ATP binding and energy coupling are necessary for normal pump cycling; mere membrane localization is insufficient.

Stimulus Set A

11. Correct answer: D — The ATP-depleted and ATP-restored treatments differ in both ATP availability and Na⁺ flux.

Restoration of ATP together with recovery of outward flux is evidence consistent with ATP-dependent transport.

12. Correct answer: A — 75%

[(12.0 − 3.0) / 12.0] × 100 = 75%.

13. Correct answer: B — The measured flux may include other transport pathways or residual transport activity.

A nonzero measured flux does not establish which alternative pathway accounts for it; additional experiments would be needed.

14. Correct answer: C — Measure cell viability in pump-inhibitor and vehicle-only groups during the same interval.

Paired viability measures help separate specific transport effects from loss of living cells.

15. Correct answer: A — ATP availability and pump activity both contribute to outward Na⁺ transport in these cells.

The interventions show associations consistent with ATP and pump involvement but cannot establish absolute exclusivity or rule out indirect effects.

Stimulus Set B

16. Correct answer: D — 24,000

Each completed cycle transports 3 Na⁺ outward, giving 3 × 8,000 = 24,000.

17. Correct answer: A — 16,000

Two K⁺ are imported per completed pump cycle: 2 × 8,000 = 16,000.

18. Correct answer: B — Normal vesicles show approximately one ATP hydrolyzed per completed transport cycle.

The one-to-one ATP-hydrolysis-to-cycle correspondence supports direct energy coupling in functional vesicles.

19. Correct answer: D — 40,000 elementary positive charges

The pump is electrogenic by one net positive charge outward per cycle. 8,000 cycles/min × 5 min = 40,000 cycles.

20. Correct answer: C — Compare vesicles with functional pumps to otherwise matched vesicles lacking the pump, both supplied with ATP.

Removing only the protein while controlling ATP and other conditions isolates the role of the transport protein.

Stimulus Set C

21. Correct answer: B — Into Cell R

Na⁺ concentration is 145 mM outside and 15 mM inside, so chemical diffusion is inward.

22. Correct answer: D — Out of Cell R

K⁺ concentration is higher inside (140 mM) than outside (5 mM), favoring outward movement.

23. Correct answer: C — The magnitude of the electrical force opposing or reinforcing its chemical gradient is not given.

The inside-negative voltage attracts positive K⁺ inward; without quantitative voltage information the opposing chemical and electrical forces cannot be summed.

24. Correct answer: A — Cell R

For Cell R, 145/15 ≈ 9.7; for Cell S, 95/65 ≈ 1.46.

25. Correct answer: C — The active maintenance of Na⁺ and K⁺ gradients will weaken as passive fluxes continue.

Blocking ATP-coupled pumping does not instantaneously erase gradients, but persistent leakage tends to dissipate gradients if they are not maintained.

Stimulus Set D

26. Correct answer: D — Intracellular Na⁺ rises as intracellular K⁺ falls.

Without sustained Na⁺ export/K⁺ import, pre-existing gradients can weaken through passive movement.

27. Correct answer: B — 1.0 mM/min

(42 − 12) mM ÷ 30 min = 1.0 mM/min.

28. Correct answer: A — Record the electrical potential difference between the cytosol and extracellular fluid.

Membrane potential is an electrical potential difference across the membrane, measured directly with suitable voltage-sensitive techniques.

29. Correct answer: C — The Na⁺/K⁺ pump is solely responsible for every change in intracellular ion concentration.

The data show correlated changes after inhibition but do not isolate all possible mechanisms or prove exclusive pump causation.

30. Correct answer: D — Track Na⁺ and K⁺ concentrations in vehicle-treated controls measured at matching times.

A parallel vehicle-treated time course controls for changes that occur independently of the intervention.
