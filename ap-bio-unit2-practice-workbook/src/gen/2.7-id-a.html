Learning objective(s): 2.7.A and 2.7.B • 25 answers

01. Osmosis — Water moves from higher to lower water potential.

02. Hypotonic solution — Water enters when the external effective solute concentration is lower.

03. Hypertonic solution — Net water moves out of the cell.

04. Isotonic solution — Equal effective tonicity gives no net volume change.

05. Osmotic lysis — An animal cell without a wall may rupture in a very hypotonic medium.

06. Crenation — Animal cells can become shrunken in hypertonic media.

07. Turgid plant cell — Water uptake creates substantial turgor pressure.

08. Flaccid plant cell — Reduced turgor occurs when water support declines.

09. Plasmolysis — The protoplast shrinks away from the cell wall in strongly hypertonic conditions.

10. Turgor pressure — A rigid wall resists the inward cell's expansion.

11. Contractile vacuole — Pumping out water supports osmoregulation in freshwater protists.

12. Osmoregulation — Osmoregulation stabilizes cell volume and water potential.

13. Water potential (Ψ) — Water moves down its water-potential gradient.

14. Pressure potential (Ψp) — Positive pressure potential is relevant in turgid cells.

15. Solute potential (Ψs) — Adding solute typically lowers water potential.

16. Ψ = Ψp + Ψs — Total water potential is the sum of its pressure and solute components.

17. Ψs = −iCRT — The negative sign reflects a solute-induced reduction in water potential.

18. Ionization factor (i) — The factor describes effective dissolved particles formed per formula unit.

19. Kelvin (K) — Absolute temperature is required, not degrees Celsius.

20. Isotonic concentration — At this concentration, there is no net mass change from water movement.

21. Swollen cell in hypotonic solution — Water enters an animal cell in a hypotonic environment.

22. Cell in isotonic solution — Water influx and efflux balance.

23. Crenation in a hypertonic solution — Water leaves a cell when the external solution is hypertonic.

24. Turgid plant cell — Turgor increases after water uptake.

25. Plasmolysis — Water loss causes the protoplast to shrink away from the wall.
