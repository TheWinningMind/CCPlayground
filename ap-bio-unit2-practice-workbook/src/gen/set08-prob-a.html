Mechanisms of Transport: ATP and Electrochemical Gradients

Problem 1 | Stoichiometry of an ATP-driven ion pump

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Ion Per cycle Direction
Na⁺ 3 Out of cell
K⁺ 2 Into cell

(a) Calculate the number of Na⁺ moved outward and K⁺ moved inward. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Na⁺ exported = 3(250) = 750.

  • Point 2: K⁺ imported = 2(250) = 500.

(b) Calculate ATP consumed and net positive charges transferred outward. [2 points]

  • Point 1: ATP used = 250 molecules.

  • Point 2: Net outward positive charge = 750 − 500 = 250 elementary charges.

(c) Explain why the pump is electrogenic and requires energy. [3 points]

  • Point 1: More positive charge is exported than imported per cycle.

  • Point 2: Na⁺ and K⁺ are transported against their relevant electrochemical gradients.

  • Point 3: ATP hydrolysis supplies energy for conformational changes associated with transport.

(d) Predict the immediate effect of blocking ATP hydrolysis on pump-mediated transport. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Active pump cycling is greatly reduced or stops.

  • Point 2: Passive ion movement through other pathways may still continue.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 2 | Identifying an electrochemical gradient

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) For a positive ion, identify the chemical-gradient direction. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Into the cell.

(b) For the same positive ion, identify the electrical-force direction. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Into the cell.

(c) Predict net passive movement through an open selective channel. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Into the cell.

(d) Explain why an ion pump moving the ion outward would require energy. [1 point]

  • Point 1: It would move the cation against the combined inward electrochemical driving forces.

Total for Problem 2: 4 points.

Problem 3 | ATP depletion versus passive ion leak

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Predict the direct effect on Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase activity. [1 point]

  • Point 1: It decreases as available ATP falls.

(b) State whether existing ion concentration gradients vanish instantly. [1 point]

  • Point 1: No.

(c) Explain why the gradients change gradually. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Ongoing passive leak can dissipate gradients when active restoration diminishes.

(d) Predict the eventual tendency of Na⁺ and K⁺ distributions if leak persists and no pumping occurs. [1 point]

  • Point 1: They move toward electrochemical equilibration, subject to other cellular constraints.

Total for Problem 3: 4 points.

Problem 4 | Ion transport under a selective inhibitor

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Inhibitor (µM) ATP-dependent outward Na⁺ flux (units/min)
0 48
1 39
5 23
10 12

(a) Calculate the percentage reduction in ATP-dependent transport at the highest inhibitor dose. [2 points]

  • Point 1: (48 − 12)/48 × 100 = 75% reduction.

  • Point 2: The zero-inhibitor rate is the baseline.

(b) Describe the relationship between inhibitor concentration and active transport. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Transport declines as inhibitor concentration increases.

  • Point 2: The change is monotonic across the measured doses.

(c) Propose a control to test whether inhibitor treatment affects viability rather than the target pump specifically. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Measure short-term cell viability or membrane integrity.

  • Point 2: Use a matched vehicle-only treatment.

  • Point 3: Compare target transport at doses where viability remains similar.

(d) Evaluate whether these data prove the inhibitor binds directly to the pump. [2 points]

  • Point 1: No; the assay shows an association between treatment and reduced transport.

  • Point 2: Direct binding would require an additional biochemical experiment.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.
