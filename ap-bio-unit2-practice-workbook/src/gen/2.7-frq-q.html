FRQ 1 · Potato osmosis investigation (9 points)

Five groups of identical potato cores (n = 6 per group) were submerged in different sucrose solutions for 50 min. Mean percentage changes were +16.0, +8.0, +1.5, −5.5, and −12.5 at 0.00, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, and 0.40 M, respectively. Assume sucrose does not enter the cores appreciably.

Sucrose (M) Mean mass change (%)
0.00 +16.0
0.10 +8.0
0.20 +1.5
0.30 −5.5
0.40 −12.5

(a) Identify the independent and dependent variables (2 points).

(b) Calculate the percentage mass change if one sample changed from 2.00 g to 2.16 g; show your work (2 points).

(c) Construct an appropriately labeled graph of mean percentage mass change versus molarity; plot all five data points (2 points).

(d) Estimate the isotonic concentration and explain your reasoning (2 points).

(e) Propose one improvement that would reduce measurement variation (1 point).

FRQ 2 · Water potential across neighboring cells (9 points)

Two living plant cells are separated by a water-permeable interface. Cell A: Ψs = −8.0 bars, Ψp = +2.0 bars. Cell B: Ψs = −5.0 bars, Ψp = +1.0 bar. Values are instantaneous; assume solute values initially remain unchanged.

(a) Calculate the water potential of each cell and state the appropriate units (3 points).

(b) Predict the initial net direction of water movement and justify the prediction (2 points).

(c) Predict how the pressure potential of cell A would initially change as water enters and explain why (2 points).

(d) If cell A pressure potential increases to +5 bars with Ψs constant, calculate its new Ψ and predict whether the original direction remains the same (2 points).

FRQ 3 · Contractile vacuoles (4 points)

Researchers count vacuole discharges in freshwater protists. Control cells show 10 discharges/min. After a pharmacological treatment, cells show 2 discharges/min and begin swelling. Temperature and external solution composition remain unchanged.

(a) Describe the role of the contractile vacuole in freshwater protists (1 point).

(b) Calculate percentage reduction in discharge frequency (1 point).

(c) Explain why treated cells swell (1 point).

(d) Propose an appropriate control to determine whether the treatment itself affects cell health (1 point).

FRQ 4 · Comparing extracellular solutions (4 points)

At 27°C (300 K), an open beaker contains 0.15 M sucrose and another contains 0.10 M ideal, fully dissociated NaCl. Assume both have zero pressure potential and R = 0.0831 L·bar/(mol·K).

(a) Calculate the solute potential of the sucrose solution (1 point).

(b) Calculate the solute potential of NaCl (1 point).

(c) Predict initial net water movement across a water-only membrane separating the beakers (1 point).

(d) Justify the prediction using water potential (1 point).

FRQ 5 · Animal cells and tonicity (4 points)

Equal volumes of an erythrocyte suspension were exposed for 10 minutes to isotonic saline, diluted saline, or concentrated saline. The cells remained viable for observation.

(a) Predict the volume change in diluted saline (1 point).

(b) Predict the volume change in concentrated saline (1 point).

(c) Explain why diluted saline could cause hemolysis (1 point).

(d) Identify the purpose of isotonic saline in this design (1 point).

FRQ 6 · Plasmolysis model (4 points)

Students observe onion epidermal cells in a hypotonic solution and then in a hypertonic solution. The cell wall remains visibly intact, but the membrane retracts away from the wall in the second condition.

(a) Identify the observed process in the second condition (1 point).

(b) Explain the direction of osmosis (1 point).

(c) Predict the initial change in turgor pressure (1 point).

(d) Describe an outcome if cells are returned to suitably hypotonic medium while still viable (1 point).

FRQ 7 · Evaluating a graph (4 points)

A student constructs a graph of mean percentage change in potato mass against sucrose molarity, but omits numerical units and plots a single sample at each concentration.

(a) Identify one necessary addition to the graph’s axes (1 point).

(b) Explain why replication improves the investigation (1 point).

(c) Describe where the isotonic point is located on the graph (1 point).

(d) Propose how to communicate uncertainty around each plotted mean (1 point).

FRQ 8 · Osmoregulation across environments (4 points)

A freshwater protist population is transferred to a moderately hypertonic medium for a brief observation period. Investigators monitor cell volume and contractile-vacuole frequency.

(a) Predict the initial net direction of water movement (1 point).

(b) Predict how contractile-vacuole discharge frequency could change (1 point).

(c) Explain why continuous water regulation contributes to homeostasis (1 point).

(d) Identify one variable to hold constant across experimental groups (1 point).
