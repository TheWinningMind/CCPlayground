Learning objective(s): 2.6.A • 25 retrieval prompts • Answers with explanations

01. Facilitated diffusion — Facilitated diffusion is a passive, protein-mediated transport mechanism.

02. Channel protein — Channel proteins create pathways for selected ions or molecules.

03. Carrier protein — Many carrier proteins use conformational changes during transport.

04. Aquaporin — Aquaporins enable water to move rapidly through membranes.

05. Sodium ion — The charge of Na⁺ prevents rapid direct passage through lipids.

06. Potassium ion — Potassium can move through selective K⁺ channels.

07. Charged ion — Ions generally need membrane proteins to pass through the hydrophobic core.

08. Large polar molecule — Large polar substances do not diffuse efficiently through the lipid bilayer.

09. Concentration gradient — Passive movement occurs down the relevant concentration gradient.

10. ATP (direct ATP input) — Facilitated diffusion does not directly consume ATP to move a solute.

11. Transport specificity (selectivity) — Transport proteins recognize or permit particular substances.

12. Selective potassium ion channel — Some ion channels are selective for K⁺ over other ions.

13. Hydrophilic pore (channel) — A hydrophilic channel provides an alternative path through a lipid bilayer.

14. Carrier conformational change — Alternating carrier conformations expose a binding site to opposite sides.

15. Transport saturation — Finite carrier numbers can limit the maximum facilitated-diffusion rate.

16. Transporter abundance (density) — More functional carriers or channels may increase total transport capacity.

17. Channel blockade — Channel blockade can reduce ion permeability through that channel.

18. Membrane polarization — Unequal ion distribution can create an electrical potential difference.

19. Membrane potential — Membrane potential reflects an electrical gradient across the membrane.

20. Down-gradient transport — For an uncharged solute, net facilitated diffusion follows its concentration gradient.

21. Net transport rate (net flux) — Net flux measures the overall direction and rate of transfer.

22. Diffusive equilibrium — At equilibrium, opposing movements continue but cancel in net.

23. Ion-channel selectivity — Channel architecture determines which ions can permeate.

24. Aquaporin loss-of-function — Impaired aquaporins generally reduce the membrane's capacity for rapid water movement.

25. Competitive binding (carrier competition) — Competition for a finite binding site can limit transport of a substrate.

Teacher note: Award one point for each conceptually correct answer. Accept standard biological synonyms and appropriate notation, but use the clue’s defining detail to distinguish closely related structures and mechanisms.
