FRQ 1 · Energy dependence of uphill solute uptake (9 points)

Curriculum: 2.5.A.1–2.5.A.3 | total criteria: 9

Criterion Award 1 point for a response that...
a1 Outside Y is 5 mM and initial inside Y is 25 mM, so Y is more concentrated inside the cell.
a2 Untreated cells show net Y movement into the cell because internal Y rises from 25 to 35 mM.
b 35 − 25 = 10 mM increase (correct calculation and units).
c1 Energy restriction strongly reduces the increase in internal Y (25→26 mM versus 25→35 mM).
c2 Energy restoration largely rescues the increase (25→34 mM), supporting a reversible energy-dependent component.
d1 Propose paired groups with and without energy restriction and a suitable integrity/viability assay, while holding starting gradients or duration constant.
d2 Specify quantitative uptake of Y and membrane integrity as outcomes, with how the comparison separates energy limitation from damage.
e Predict that net Y uptake will increase toward untreated levels after energy supply is restored, if inhibition is reversible.
f Continued Z uptake suggests the membrane retains a route for passive entry and that general transport failure is less likely.

Scoring interpretation: Do not award a claim identifying a specific ATPase; no transporter identity is given. The Z comparison does not prove that all passive pathways remain unchanged.

FRQ 2 · Quantifying vesicle-mediated uptake and secretion (9 points)

Curriculum: 2.5.B.1 | total criteria: 9

Criterion Award 1 point for a response that...
a1 Bead uptake by membrane invagination and vesicle formation is endocytosis.
a2 Release of vesicular protein via fusion with plasma membrane is exocytosis.
b 12/1 = 12-fold relative to the mean count at 0 min.
c1 Endocytic vesicles are lower with treatment: 3 versus 12 per cell at 10 min.
c2 Secreted protein is lower with treatment: 12 versus 42 relative units.
d1 Preload or label a fixed amount of protein before applying the inhibitor, then compare intracellular cargo and extracellular secretion between groups.
d2 A buildup of correctly packaged intracellular cargo with reduced external release supports a fusion defect; little or no intracellular packaged cargo suggests an upstream defect.
e Intracellular cargo-bearing vesicles accumulate when packaging continues but fusion and release are blocked.
f Cell damage or lysis could release fluorescent protein nonspecifically; a survival or integrity assay helps assess this alternative.

Scoring interpretation: An observed reduction in both processes does not establish that one molecular component controls both. Award investigation points for testable comparisons, not unsupported certainty.

FRQ 6 · Separating exocytosis from membrane rupture (4 points)

Curriculum: 2.5.B.1 | total criteria: 4

Criterion Award 1 point for a response that...
a Extracellular cargo rises from 6 to 34 relative units with stimulation, an increase of 28 relative units.
b 34 − 9 = 25 relative units lower with fusion inhibitor.
c Low, similar damage-dye positivity (2–3%) makes widespread membrane rupture an unlikely explanation for the large release difference.
d More preloaded cargo remains inside fusion-inhibited cells than in stimulated cells because cargo-containing vesicles cannot discharge normally.

Scoring interpretation: Damage-dye results support, but do not definitively prove, vesicle fusion. Direct imaging would provide stronger mechanism-specific evidence.
