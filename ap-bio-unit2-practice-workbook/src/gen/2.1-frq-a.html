FRQ 1 · Protein Secretion and the Endomembrane System (9 points)

Scoring criteria (9 points)

Part Point awarded for Pts
a(i) Ribosomes translate mRNA sequences by assembling amino acids into polypeptides/proteins. 1
a(ii) Ribosomes attached to rough ER synthesize proteins destined for the secretory/endomembrane pathway. 1
b(i) 20 minutes (34% of labeled protein in Golgi, the maximum). 1
b(ii) 55% − 5% = 50 percentage points. 1
c(i) Labeled protein in rough ER decreases from 78% to 8% while the proportion outside increases from 2% to 55%. 1
c(ii) An increase/peak in Golgi labeling before the high extracellular fraction is consistent with movement via Golgi during secretion. 1
d(i) At 30 minutes, 61% of the recovered label is in the Golgi with Drug X versus 15% without the drug (a 46-percentage-point increase). 1
d(ii) Retention of more labeled reporter in the Golgi together with a smaller extracellular fraction is consistent with impaired onward export from the Golgi; the result supports, but does not by itself prove, that mechanism. 1
d(iii) Compare the amount of newly synthesized labeled protein immediately after equal-length pulses in matched treated and control cells; similar initial labeling supports unchanged synthesis. 1

Scientific explanation / examiner guidance

Secretory proteins are synthesized by ribosomes, often associated with rough ER. As these proteins move through the endomembrane system, transient labeling shifts toward Golgi compartments and eventually the extracellular fraction. Drug X leaves far more labeled protein in the Golgi and less outside the cell at 30 minutes, consistent with impaired Golgi export. The time course supports—but does not directly visualize—the suggested route.

Exemplar reasoning: Across 30 minutes, labeled protein in rough ER decreases from 78% to 8%, while labeling outside the cell rises from 2% to 55%. Golgi labeling rises to a peak of 34% at 20 minutes before declining. This time sequence is consistent with secretory trafficking through Golgi. With Drug X at 30 minutes, 61% remains in the Golgi, compared with 15% in controls, and only 13% is outside the cell versus 55% in controls. These observations support reduced Golgi export but do not establish the exact drug target.

Do not award: Subtracting 5 from 55 and reporting a 50% relative increase; the question asks for percentage points. Do not credit the claim that Golgi ribosomes synthesize secreted proteins.

Alignment: 2.1.A.1–2.1.A.4; interpretation of an experimental data set, calculation, causal inference and control design.

FRQ 2 · Mitochondrial Structure and ATP Production (9 points)

Scoring criteria (9 points)

Part Point awarded for Pts
a(i) The outer membrane is relatively smooth; the inner membrane forms numerous folds (cristae). 1
a(ii) Inner-membrane folds provide greater membrane area for ATP-producing processes and support efficient ATP synthesis. 1
b(i) Axes correctly show relative inner-membrane area (unitless) and ATP production rate (nmol min⁻¹ mg⁻¹ mitochondrial protein). 1
b(ii) All four means are correctly plotted at (1.0,10), (1.4,15), (1.8,19), and (2.2,23) and connected. 1
b(iii) Four error bars extend ±1, ±1, ±2, and ±2 units from the respective means. 1
c(i) Mean ATP production rate increases as relative inner-membrane area increases (positive association). 1
c(ii) [(23 − 10) ÷ 10] × 100 = 130% increase. 1
d(i) The results show association, not proof that membrane area alone causes increased ATP production; other properties may differ. 1
d(ii) Proposes a controlled manipulation of inner-membrane area or folding in otherwise matched mitochondria, measures ATP rate after the manipulation, and controls mitochondrial amount, oxygen, and substrates. Observing a correlation or measuring membrane proteins alone is insufficient as a causal test. 1

MODEL GRAPH. Mean rates plotted against relative inner-membrane area; error bars indicate ±1 SE.

Scientific explanation / examiner guidance

Mitochondrial double membranes provide distinct cellular compartments. Extensive folding of the inner membrane increases the available membrane surface and can facilitate ATP production. The table shows a monotonic rise in ATP rate across preparations (10, 15, 19 and 23 units). However, preparations may also differ in composition or physiological state. Correlation by itself is not sufficient to establish that area is the sole cause.

Worked data interpretation: The x-axis reports relative membrane area, not the number of mitochondria. The y-axis reports ATP produced per minute per milligram of mitochondrial protein. In preparation A, the mean rate is 10 with a standard error of 1; in preparation D, the mean is 23 with a standard error of 2. The percent increase from A to D is ((23 − 10) / 10) × 100 = 130%. Four error bars must show the corresponding plus-or-minus one-SE intervals. Equal oxygen availability, nutrients, temperature, and protein mass improve comparability, but other membrane-associated proteins or differences in mitochondrial health could still account for some of the observed variation.

Do not award: Assuming that overlapping or nonoverlapping SE bars alone establishes statistical significance. A formal inference would require an appropriate statistical test and study design.

Alignment: 2.1.A.5; accurate graph construction, percent-change computation, evaluating evidence and proposing a test.

FRQ 3 · Functional Specialization of the Endoplasmic Reticulum (4 points)

Scoring criteria (4 points)

Part Point awarded for Pts
a Region B is smooth endoplasmic reticulum (smooth ER). 1
b Region A is studded with membrane-associated ribosomes whereas Region B lacks ribosomes / is made of smooth tubules. 1
c The exposed cells have 1.7 times as much Region B membrane area and a shorter chemical-metabolism time (29 versus 48 min), supporting an association with detoxification. 1
d Lipid synthesis can be supported by increased smooth ER membranes. 1

Scientific explanation / examiner guidance

Rough ER bears ribosomes and participates in protein synthesis. Smooth ER lacks attached ribosomes and has roles in detoxification and lipid synthesis. Because only observational comparisons are reported, the changed surface area and metabolism time support a relationship but need not demonstrate direct causation.

Do not award: Identifying smooth ER as the site of ribosome-mediated protein synthesis; using highly specialized smooth-ER functions not required by the AP Topic 2.1 framework.

Alignment: 2.1.A.3; organelle identification, evidence selection and prediction.

FRQ 4 · Lysosomal Enzymes and Cellular Digestion (4 points)

Scoring criteria (4 points)

Part Point awarded for Pts
a The lysosome. 1
b A membrane encloses hydrolytic enzymes that digest proteins or other cellular material. 1
c 82 − 24 = 58 percentage points. 1
d 100% − 24% = 76%. Therefore 76% of the introduced protein remains undigested in inhibitor-treated cells at 60 minutes. 1

Scientific explanation / examiner guidance

Hydrolytic enzymes function inside membrane-enclosed lysosomes to digest cellular material. Under the matched initial-loading conditions, inhibition reduces the fraction digested. At 60 min, the undigested proportions are 18% for control (100 − 82) and 76% for inhibitor-treated cells (100 − 24).

Do not award: Stating that the inhibitor increases lysosomal digestion or that lysosomes synthesize the newly introduced protein.

Alignment: 2.1.A.6; lysosomal digestion and analysis of an inhibitor experiment.

FRQ 5 · Central Vacuoles and Plant-Cell Support (4 points)

Scoring criteria (4 points)

Part Point awarded for Pts
a The large central vacuole. 1
b Storage of water in the large central vacuole helps maintain turgor pressure and structural firmness. 1
c Condition A has a larger relative vacuole area (71% vs 36%) and higher leaf firmness score (8.4 vs 3.1); larger vacuoles are associated with greater firmness in this comparison. 1
d Firmness/turgor would decrease, potentially causing the cells or leaf to become less rigid or wilt. 1

Scientific explanation / examiner guidance

The large central vacuole in plant cells is a membrane-bound reservoir that helps maintain turgor pressure through storage of water and solutes. This contributes to the stiffness of nonwoody plant tissues. A lower measured vacuole area and lower firmness are consistent with reduced turgor in Condition B, without requiring calculation of water potential.

Do not award: Stating that a central vacuole provides rigid structural support by replacing the cell wall; the turgor pressure depends on water stored within the cell against its wall.

Alignment: 2.1.A.7; vacuole structure, water storage and turgor.

FRQ 6 · Chloroplast Structure and Cellular Function (4 points)

Scoring criteria (4 points)

Part Point awarded for Pts
a Chloroplast. 1
b The organelle has an envelope consisting of an outer membrane and an inner membrane. 1
c The line with 24 chloroplasts per cell has photosynthetic output 100, while the line with 12 has output 61, consistent with chloroplasts contributing to photosynthesis. 1
d The mutant may also differ in unmeasured characteristics that affect photosynthesis; the two measured traits do not isolate chloroplast number as the only cause. 1

Scientific explanation / examiner guidance

Chloroplasts are double-membrane organelles found in plants and photosynthetic algae that are the locations for photosynthesis. A decline in chloroplast number accompanied by a lower photosynthetic output supports their functional role. The experiment does not establish whether chloroplast quantity or an unmeasured trait explains the entire difference.

Do not award: Claiming that the numerical results prove a proportional causal relationship: the chloroplast count decreases 50% while photosynthetic output decreases 39%.

Alignment: 2.1.A.8; chloroplast structure, cellular function, claim evaluation.

FRQ 7 · Ribosomes and Common Features of Cells (4 points)

Scoring criteria (4 points)

Part Point awarded for Pts
a Ribosomes contain ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and proteins. 1
b Bacteria have free ribosomes that translate mRNA into proteins; rough ER is not necessary for ribosome-based protein synthesis. 1
c All three cell types contain ribosomes / all synthesize proteins according to mRNA sequences. 1
d The widespread shared ribosome-based system supports common ancestry of known life. 1

Scientific explanation / examiner guidance

Ribosomes are not membrane-bound and occur in all forms of known life. They synthesize proteins by translating mRNA. Their fundamental shared structure and role, even in organisms without ER, provide evidence for an evolutionarily conserved system and common ancestry.

Do not award: Claiming bacteria cannot make proteins without rough ER, or that ribosomes are membrane-bound organelles.

Alignment: 2.1.A.1; universal ribosome occurrence, structure, protein synthesis and common ancestry.
