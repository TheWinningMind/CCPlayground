Facilitated Diffusion: Channel and Carrier Experiments

Problem 1 | Transport saturation and concentration

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 4.B, 5.A, 6.E

Two cultured-cell lines contain different abundances of a glucose carrier. Glucose is more concentrated outside than inside during the brief initial-rate measurements. Uptake rates are measured in arbitrary units.

Figure M06.1: Original instructional model. Labels are intentional, not to physical scale.

External glucose (mM) Line A uptake Line B uptake
0 0 0
2 8 4
8 15 8
16 16 9

(a) Calculate the rate increase for Line A as concentration rises from 2 to 8 mM. [2 points]

(b) Describe evidence for saturation in Line A. [2 points]

(c) Explain why Line B has lower rates at every tested concentration if it expresses fewer functional carriers. [3 points]

(d) Predict whether an ATP-synthesis inhibitor must stop all uptake immediately, and justify. [2 points]

Problem 2 | Aquaporin abundance

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 3.C, 6.E

A plant cell line has more aquaporin channels than a comparison line, with otherwise matched membrane area and osmotic conditions.

(a) Identify the transported substance. [1 point]

(b) Predict the effect on initial water movement rate. [1 point]

(c) Explain whether increased aquaporins necessarily change the direction of net water movement. [1 point]

(d) State one variable to match across groups. [1 point]

Problem 3 | Selectivity of an ion channel

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 2.C, 6.E

A membrane channel allows K⁺ to pass but strongly limits Na⁺ passage even though both ions carry positive charge.

(a) Identify the membrane component controlling this selectivity. [1 point]

(b) State one feature that could contribute to K⁺ selectivity. [1 point]

(c) Predict a possible effect of a selectivity-filter mutation. [1 point]

(d) Explain why both ions cannot simply move freely through the bilayer. [1 point]

Problem 4 | A competing substrate reduces uptake

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 3.C, 5.A, 6.B

A carrier transports solute X by facilitated diffusion. Solute Y binds the same site but is not transported. Rates are measured with a fixed downhill X gradient.

Y concentration (mM) X uptake rate (units)
0 20
2 17
6 12
12 8

(a) Calculate the percentage change when Y is added at 6 mM. [2 points]

(b) Explain why the data support competitive binding. [2 points]

(c) Design a test to distinguish competition from irreversible carrier destruction. [3 points]

(d) Explain why uptake remains passive under the scenario. [2 points]

Problem 5 | Reversal of a permeant-solute gradient

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.E

A carrier protein remains functional while the solute concentration gradient reverses across the plasma membrane.

(a) Predict the new direction of net carrier-mediated movement. [1 point]

(b) State whether the carrier necessarily becomes an ATP-driven pump. [1 point]

(c) Explain the direction change. [1 point]

(d) Identify a condition that yields no net solute movement. [1 point]

Problem 6 | Channel closure and membrane flux

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 3.C, 6.B

An ion channel is open in one treatment and chemically blocked in a second, with the same ion gradient across the membrane.

(a) Predict the initial ion flux with the channel blocked. [1 point]

(b) Identify what must be measured to distinguish permeability from gradient change. [1 point]

(c) State a control for nonspecific effects of the blocking agent. [1 point]

(d) Explain why ion movement through an open channel does not directly require ATP. [1 point]
