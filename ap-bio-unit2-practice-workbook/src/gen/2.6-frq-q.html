FRQ 1 · Carrier kinetics and experimental design [9 points]

A cultured-cell preparation contains a glucose carrier. Intracellular free glucose is maintained near zero during short uptake assays. External glucose (mM): 1, 2, 4, 8, 16; measured initial uptake (units/min): 12, 20, 30, 40, 46. In a second treatment, the carrier is partially blocked.

External glucose (mM) 1 2 4 8 16
Initial uptake (units/min) 12 20 30 40 46

(a) [1 point] Describe the trend in uptake rate as external glucose increases.

(b) [2 points] Calculate the percentage increase in uptake between 2 mM and 4 mM. Show work.

(c) [2 points] Explain why the pattern is consistent with a limited number of functional carriers.

(d) [1 point] Identify an appropriate negative control that isolates carrier-mediated uptake.

(e) [2 points] Predict the effect of partial carrier blockade on initial uptake rate at 16 mM and justify your prediction.

(f) [1 point] Explain why the saturating curve alone cannot establish that the uptake is passive.

FRQ 2 · Aquaporins and equilibrium [9 points]

Investigators compare matched cell populations in a fixed osmotic environment. Control cells show an initial volume-change rate of 1.8% per second and an aquaporin variant shows 0.5% per second. Both reach a relative equilibrium volume of 0.85.

(a) [1 point] Identify the membrane protein responsible for rapid water passage.

(b) [2 points] Calculate the ratio of the control to variant initial rates.

(c) [2 points] Describe the difference between the initial response and equilibrium response of the two groups.

(d) [2 points] Explain why lower aquaporin activity changes rate but need not change the final equilibrium volume.

(e) [2 points] Predict the initial response to adding functional aquaporins to the variant and justify the prediction.

FRQ 3 · Ion channels and membrane polarization [4 points]

In an artificial membrane, selective K⁺ channels permit outward K⁺ movement under a defined electrochemical gradient. Investigators measure outward flux and membrane voltage before and after a selective channel blocker is added.

(a) [1 point] Describe the protein pathway that enables K⁺ movement through the membrane.

(b) [1 point] Predict how outward K⁺ flux changes immediately following selective channel blockade.

(c) [1 point] Explain why the K⁺ flux direction does not necessarily reverse when channels are blocked.

(d) [1 point] Identify a measurement that would detect a change in membrane polarization.

FRQ 4 · Carrier specificity and competing ligand [4 points]

Artificial vesicles contain a carrier selective for solute X. Y binds the carrier but is not transported. X uptake (units/min) with 0, 2, 6, 12 mM Y is 30, 24, 15, 10 respectively; X concentration is constant.

Y (mM) 0 2 6 12
X uptake (units/min) 30 24 15 10

(a) [1 point] Identify the independent variable.

(b) [1 point] Calculate the percentage reduction in X uptake when Y rises from 0 to 6 mM.

(c) [1 point] Explain a molecular mechanism consistent with the data.

(d) [1 point] Justify why the data do not prove Y is itself transported.

FRQ 5 · Molecular structure and transport route [4 points]

A researcher compares three substances across a pure phospholipid bilayer and a protein-containing membrane: a small nonpolar gas, Na⁺, and a large polar sugar. Temperature and concentration differences are controlled.

(a) [1 point] Identify the substance most likely to cross the pure bilayer readily.

(b) [1 point] Explain why Na⁺ crosses the pure bilayer poorly.

(c) [1 point] Predict the effect of adding an appropriately selective sugar carrier on the initial inward sugar flux when external sugar concentration is higher.

(d) [1 point] Explain why the increased inward sugar flux in part (c) need not consume ATP directly.
