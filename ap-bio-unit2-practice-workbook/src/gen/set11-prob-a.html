Cumulative Investigations: Designing and Evaluating Experiments

Problem 1 | Secretory pathway and membrane energy

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Group Protein secreted after 20 min (units)
Normal ATP 60
ATP limited 24

(a) Calculate the percentage decrease in secretion. [2 points]

  • Point 1: (60 − 24)/60 × 100 = 60%.

  • Point 2: The normal ATP group is the reference.

(b) Explain TWO possible ATP-dependent processes relevant to secretion. [2 points]

  • Point 1: ATP can support intracellular trafficking/transport steps.

  • Point 2: ATP-dependent cellular processes also contribute to vesicle movement, docking, and fusion regulation.

(c) Design a control that tests whether the effect is reversible. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Restore ATP availability after the short depletion treatment.

  • Point 2: Include an otherwise identical group that remains depleted.

  • Point 3: Measure subsequent secretion while monitoring viability and protein abundance.

(d) Explain why secretion data alone cannot locate the blocked organelle. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Reduced extracellular cargo can result from problems at different stages.

  • Point 2: Localization of cargo among ER, Golgi, and vesicles is needed to identify the affected step.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 2 | A permeability-dye control

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify the confounding factor. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Loss of membrane integrity.

(b) Explain how the confound complicates the conclusion. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Dye movement/retention could change because the barrier is damaged rather than because the target carrier is blocked.

(c) Propose one additional measurement. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Assess cell membrane integrity with an independent exclusion dye or other viability assay.

(d) State one experimental constant. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Match dye concentration, incubation time, temperature, and starting cell number.

Total for Problem 2: 4 points.

Problem 3 | Osmosis with altered aquaporins

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify the independent variable. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Aquaporin abundance.

(b) Identify the dependent variable. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Initial rate of water movement or cell-volume change.

(c) Predict which group changes volume more quickly. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The aquaporin-rich group.

(d) Explain why equal final equilibrium need not imply equal initial rates. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Both may approach the same water-potential equilibrium at different permeability-dependent speeds.

Total for Problem 3: 4 points.

Problem 4 | Controlling for cell size in transporter expression

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Line Uptake (pmol/min/cell) Accessible area (µm²)
A 18 60
B 27 90

(a) Calculate uptake per unit membrane area if Line A has 60 µm² and B has 90 µm². [2 points]

  • Point 1: Line A: 18/60 = 0.30 pmol·min⁻¹·µm⁻².

  • Point 2: Line B: 27/90 = 0.30 pmol·min⁻¹·µm⁻².

(b) Evaluate the claim that Line B has higher transport activity per unit membrane. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The claim is unsupported by the normalized data.

  • Point 2: Both lines have the same uptake per unit membrane area.

(c) Propose an experiment to test whether carrier abundance per membrane area differs between lines. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Quantify surface carrier number per unit membrane area for both lines.

  • Point 2: Use the same labeling efficiency and microscopy/calibration method.

  • Point 3: Compare area-normalized abundance with area-normalized uptake.

(d) Explain why reporting uptake per cell alone could be misleading. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Larger membrane area can raise total uptake even without higher density-specific activity.

  • Point 2: Normalization is needed to separate size effects from transport efficiency.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.

Problem 5 | Cell-wall removal as a controlled investigation

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify the independent variable. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Presence or absence of the cell wall.

(b) Identify one dependent variable. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Fraction of cells lysed or cell volume change after a fixed time.

(c) Predict the group with more osmotic lysis. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Protoplasts without walls.

(d) Explain the difference. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Walls mechanically resist swelling even though the plasma membrane determines selective permeability.

Total for Problem 5: 4 points.

Problem 6 | Distinguishing common ancestry from organelle function

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify evidence more directly relevant to evolutionary origin. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Homologous gene/ribosomal sequences indicating shared ancestry.

(b) Identify evidence more directly relevant to current function. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Inner membrane folding or thylakoid arrangement.

(c) Explain why one kind of evidence cannot substitute for the other. [1 point]

  • Point 1: A structure can function similarly without proving a particular origin, and evolutionary homology does not specify its present performance.

(d) State one claim that multiple lines of evidence could support. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Mitochondria and chloroplasts are consistent with endosymbiotic origins.

Total for Problem 6: 4 points.
