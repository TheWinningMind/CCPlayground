Plasma Membrane: Structure and Evidence

Problem 1 | Interpreting fluorescence recovery after photobleaching

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Time (s) Control (%) Cross-linked (%)
0 0 0
30 43 8
60 63 16
120 74 25

(a) Describe the pattern across the two groups between 0 and 120 s. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Both start at 0% fluorescence recovery at 0 s.

  • Point 2: Control recovery reaches 74% at 120 s, compared with 25% in the cross-linked group.

(b) Explain what recovery indicates about the membrane. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Unbleached labeled molecules enter the bleached region.

  • Point 2: This supports lateral mobility within the membrane.

(c) Predict the effect of greater protein cross-linking on recovery and justify. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Greater cross-linking is predicted to reduce recovery.

  • Point 2: Cross-linking restricts lateral diffusion.

  • Point 3: The result depends on the proteins remaining fluorescent and otherwise comparable.

(d) Identify a useful control variable and explain its importance. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Hold bleaching intensity/duration or temperature constant.

  • Point 2: This prevents procedural variation from being mistaken for changed lateral mobility.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 4 | The role of cholesterol in membrane organization

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Preparation Order at 10°C Order at 40°C
No cholesterol 0.82 0.31
With cholesterol 0.65 0.5

(a) Interpret the change in the order index of the preparation without cholesterol. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The index falls from 0.82 to 0.31 as temperature rises.

  • Point 2: This indicates a greater disordering at higher temperature.

(b) Describe how the cholesterol-containing preparation differs. [2 points]

  • Point 1: It has a lower order index at low temperature (0.65 versus 0.82).

  • Point 2: It has a higher order index at high temperature (0.50 versus 0.31).

(c) Explain how the data support a temperature-buffering role for cholesterol. [3 points]

  • Point 1: At low temperature cholesterol interferes with tight packing.

  • Point 2: At high temperature its rigid structure restrains phospholipid motion.

  • Point 3: The measurements show a smaller temperature-dependent change in the cholesterol condition.

(d) Identify one limitation in applying these artificial-membrane results to intact cells. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Intact membranes contain additional lipids, proteins, and regulatory processes.

  • Point 2: These additional factors could alter responses to temperature.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.
