FRQ 1 · Maintaining ion gradients in cultured animal cells — 9 points
Part Point Criterion
a 1 ATP hydrolysis (metabolic energy from ATP).
b 1 Exports three Na⁺ from the cytosol to the extracellular fluid per completed cycle.
b 1 Imports two K⁺ from extracellular fluid to the cytosol per completed cycle.
c 1 Correct setup: (30 − 12) / 12 × 100.
c 1 Correct result: 150% increase, with work consistent with the data.
d 1 Inhibitor-treated cells accumulate more Na⁺ inside than the vehicle controls.
d 1 K⁺ decreases in inhibitor-treated cells; both outcomes are consistent with impaired active Na⁺ export and K⁺ import as leakage continues.
e 1 The Na⁺ gradient will tend to dissipate/weaken over time, not necessarily disappear immediately.
f 1 A vehicle-only group matched for solvent concentration, timing, cell number, and measurements.

Why these responses earn credit: Percent increase is (30 − 12)/12 × 100 = 150%. The vehicle control shows minimal drift, whereas both ion concentrations change appreciably in the inhibitor group. This is consistent with a role for ATP-dependent pumping; the experiment does not establish that every observed ion movement occurs through the pump.

Common error to avoid: A single inhibited-group change does not prove that the pump is the only transporter involved.

FRQ 2 · ATP dependence of purified transport proteins — 9 points
Part Point Criterion
a 1 Independent variable: ATP concentration (mM).
a 1 Dependent variable: completed transport cycles per second.
b 1 Appropriate x and y axes with labels, units, and numerical scales encompassing all values.
b 1 Both data series accurately plotted and distinguished with a legend.
b 1 SE error bars plotted appropriately for both series.
c 1 Correct setup: (5.1 − 3.0)/3.0 × 100.
c 1 Correct result: 70% increase.
d 1 The rate approaches a plateau near 8 cycles/s despite additional ATP, consistent with pump turnover/availability becoming limiting.
e 1 A valid difference from intact cells (for example, native membrane composition, ion gradients, accessory proteins, or regulation).

Why these responses earn credit: A response earns graphing credit by representing both full datasets accurately, identifying units, and displaying uncertainty using the provided ±SE values. The approximate plateau is evidence of another rate limit, not proof of a particular molecular cause.

Common error to avoid: A plateau is consistent with saturation but does not identify the unique molecular cause of that plateau.

FRQ 3 · Electrochemical driving forces — 4 points
Part Point Criterion
a 1 Chemical gradient favors outward K⁺ movement.
b 1 Negative-inside voltage attracts K⁺ inward.
c 1 Opposing forces require quantitative voltage / equilibrium information to predict net passive flux.
d 1 Without normal ATP-driven pumping, the K⁺ concentration gradient tends to weaken over time as passive movement proceeds.

Why these responses earn credit: A concentration gradient and an electrical gradient can oppose each other. The phrase “negative inside” does not provide enough quantitative information to establish the net flux direction.

Common error to avoid: Concentration differences alone do not determine the net electrochemical flux.

FRQ 4 · Electrogenic transport and experimental design — 4 points
Part Point Criterion
a 1 36,000 Na⁺ exported = 3 × 12,000.
b 1 24,000 K⁺ imported = 2 × 12,000.
c 1 Net 12,000 positive elementary charges outward = 1 × 12,000.
d 1 Membrane potential also depends on membrane capacitance, other ion movements/permeabilities, gradients, and geometry; charge count alone is insufficient.

Why these responses earn credit: Each completed pump cycle has a 3:2 Na⁺-to-K⁺ stoichiometry, yielding a net outward movement of one positive charge. It is invalid to convert the number of charges into a definite membrane voltage without additional information.

Common error to avoid: A net outward movement of charges is not numerically equivalent to a measured membrane voltage.
