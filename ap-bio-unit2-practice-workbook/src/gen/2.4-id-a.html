01. Selective permeability — Membrane structure and solute properties affect crossing.

02. Hydrophobic core — Hydrocarbon tails form an energetic barrier for hydrophilic solutes.

03. Oxygen (O₂) — Oxygen is small and nonpolar, so it diffuses readily through lipids.

04. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — Carbon dioxide is small and nonpolar and crosses without a transporter.

05. Water (H₂O) — Some water passes directly, but aquaporins increase water permeability.

06. Ammonia (NH₃) — Small uncharged polar molecules have limited bilayer permeability.

07. Sodium ion (Na⁺) — Its charge makes direct penetration of the lipid core unfavorable.

08. Chloride ion (Cl⁻) — Ions need channels or transport proteins to cross efficiently.

09. Glucose — Glucose does not freely traverse the hydrophobic core.

10. Electrical charge — Charge is strongly unfavorable in the hydrophobic bilayer core.

11. Polarity — Polar solutes interact poorly with the bilayer's nonpolar interior.

12. Channel protein — A channel shields transported solutes from the hydrophobic lipid region.

13. Plasma membrane — This selectively permeable boundary separates two aqueous environments.

14. Plant cell wall — The wall supports structure and helps resist excessive swelling.

15. Cellulose — Cellulose microfibrils provide mechanical reinforcement.

16. Chitin — Chitin contributes to the rigidity of fungal walls.

17. Peptidoglycan — Bacterial walls often contain peptidoglycan.

18. Osmotic lysis — Cells without sufficiently protective walls can burst in hypotonic conditions.

19. Cell wall — A strong wall resists expansion and bursting.

20. Liposome — A protein-free liposome can isolate the behavior of a lipid bilayer.

21. Hydrophobic core — The nonpolar core limits direct passage of hydrophilic solutes.

22. Small nonpolar molecule — Such molecules cross the bilayer readily.

23. Ion — Charges resist passage through a hydrophobic core.

24. Channel protein — Channels shield ions from hydrocarbon tails.

25. Cell wall — The wall lies outside the plasma membrane.
