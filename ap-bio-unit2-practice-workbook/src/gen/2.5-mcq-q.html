Directions: Select the single best answer for each question. Scientific data are hypothetical unless explicitly stated. All questions are original, unofficial AP-style practice. Shared-stimulus groups contain five questions each.

Section I | Foundational applications (Questions 1–20)

1

  1. The membrane limits movement of X, allowing different concentrations on its two sides.
  2. Solute X must move only from low to high concentration.
  3. The membrane continually produces solute X on both sides.
  4. Every solute crosses the membrane at the same rate.
2

  1. No molecular movement until concentrations on both sides become equal.
  2. Net movement of the solute from outside to inside without direct metabolic energy input.
  3. Net movement from outside to inside only if the cell hydrolyzes ATP.
  4. Net movement from inside to outside using ATP.
3

  1. Only passive transport can involve a membrane protein.

  3. Only passive transport can change a concentration gradient.
  4. Active transport always moves material toward lower concentrations.
4

  1. Passive net transport of the solute into the cell.
  2. Equal movement in both directions with no net flux.
  3. Passive transport of the solute out of the cell only.
  4. Energy-dependent active transport of the solute into the cell.
5

  4. The extracellular fluid is enclosed within a new internal vesicle.

  1. The total number of molecules moving in either direction.

Which example best represents exocytosis rather than endocytosis?
  1. A cell engulfs a fluorescent particle by membrane invagination.

  3. Extracellular liquid becomes enclosed within a newly formed vesicle.

15

  1. The solute remains more concentrated on the same side at every time point.

19

  1. A concentration gradient and a pathway through which the solute can move.

  3. The cell is producing new secretory vesicles.
  4. The solute is packaged in a membrane-bound vesicle.
20

  1. Untreated cells continue taking up the solute.

  3. Extracellular solute concentration is measured before treatment.
  4. The inhibitor also causes extensive membrane rupture and cell death during the assay.

24

  4. The cells are exposed to the stimulus for a longer period.
25

  4. Passive transport begins to use ATP whenever active transport ceases.
