FRQ 1 · Spatial separation of competing pathways (9 points)

Researchers engineer cultured eukaryotic cells so that an artificial membrane-bounded compartment contains substrate S and enzyme A. Enzyme A converts S into desired product P; enzyme B, located mainly outside the compartment, competes for S and converts it into W. A second cell line has the same compartment but receives a treatment that increases membrane leakiness without changing the total amounts of A and B. Both enzyme reactions consume one molecule of S per molecule of product. The hypothetical measurements follow.

Time (min) P, intact (nmol) P, leaky (nmol) W, intact (nmol) W, leaky (nmol)
0 0 0 0 0
5 25 17 5 13
10 47 29 11 29
15 65 36 15 44

Each culture starts with 100 nmol S. The table reports cumulative amounts of the indicated products. The two lines use equal observation intervals; other named conditions are held constant.

(a) Describe ONE general way a membrane-bound compartment changes the environment in which cellular enzymes operate. (1 point)

(b) Calculate the amount of substrate remaining in the intact cells at 15 minutes. Then determine the difference in desired product P between intact and leaky cells at that time. Show work for both values. (2 points)

(c) Identify the independent variable. Explain why equal initial amounts of S, A, and B are important to the experimental design. Propose ONE additional measurement that tests whether the leakiness treatment affects enzyme A directly. (3 points)

(d) Predict how a loss of the membrane-targeting signal for enzyme A would affect the amount of P formed, assuming A remains catalytically active. Justify the prediction in terms of enzyme and substrate localization. State ONE inference about enzyme A activity that cannot be established from the table alone. (3 points)

FRQ 2 · Inner membrane area and metabolic capacity (9 points)

Scientists compare three eukaryotic cell strains with different degrees of mitochondrial inner-membrane folding. The mean mitochondrial volume per cell and the density of relevant membrane-associated proteins per unit of inner-membrane area are similar across strains. Measurements below represent the mean ATP synthesis capacity of isolated mitochondria in a standardized assay with abundant substrates, together with standard errors (SE). These values are hypothetical.

Low folds 6 30 3
Moderate folds 10 53 4
Extensive folds 15 79 4

Figure. Blank graph workspace for the three means and ±1 SE error bars; use the categories and axes supplied.

(a) Describe ONE structural feature of the inner mitochondrial membrane that can increase surface area without an equivalent increase in organelle volume. (1 point)

(b) Construct a bar graph showing the mean ATP synthesis capacity for all three strains. Include appropriate ±1 SE error bars and accurate labeling. (4 points)

(c) Calculate the percentage increase in ATP synthesis capacity from low-fold to extensive-fold mitochondria, using the low-fold value as the reference. Identify ONE variable already controlled in the experiment. (2 points)

(d) Predict the direction of change in ATP synthesis capacity if an additional mutation reduces inner-membrane area while maintaining enzyme density and substrate supply. Justify the prediction by linking membrane area to the number of available sites for membrane-associated proteins. (2 points)

Two membrane-associated enzymes, X and Y, catalyze successive steps in a pathway. A model proposes that their spatial co-localization on a folded internal membrane increases final-product formation, even when the total amounts of X and Y are unchanged. Researchers compare equal-volume synthetic systems supplied with identical enzyme concentrations and equal substrate amounts. Enzyme attachment density per unit of available membrane area is held constant; consequently, systems with more area can bind more enzyme molecules.

(a) Describe one physical difference between the two membrane configurations in the model. (1 point)

(b) Explain how the difference can influence the number of available sites for membrane-associated enzyme reactions. (1 point)

(c) Predict the direction of change in maximum product formation if membrane folding decreases while enzyme density per unit membrane area is maintained. (1 point)

(d) Provide one reason the model alone cannot justify the claim that reaction rate must increase whenever membranes become more folded. (1 point)
