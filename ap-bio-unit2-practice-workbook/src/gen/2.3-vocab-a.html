Learning objective(s): 2.3.A–2.3.B • 25 retrieval prompts • Answers with explanations

01. Phospholipid — Phospholipids form the basic membrane bilayer.

02. Hydrophilic head — The polar heads face the aqueous exterior and cytosol.

03. Hydrophobic fatty-acid tails — The hydrocarbon tails create the nonpolar membrane interior.

04. Amphipathic — Phospholipids are amphipathic molecules.

05. Phospholipid bilayer — This arrangement allows a stable barrier between aqueous environments.

06. Hydrophobic core of the membrane — Charged substances interact unfavorably with the lipid interior.

07. Fluid mosaic model — Membranes contain a dynamic mixture of lipids and proteins.

08. Membrane fluidity — Membrane fluidity reflects motion and rearrangement of its components.

09. Lateral diffusion — Lateral movement is much more common than spontaneous movement between leaflets.

10. Integral membrane protein — Integral proteins interact with the membrane's hydrophobic interior.

11. Transmembrane protein — A transmembrane protein crosses the bilayer and often has hydrophobic spanning regions.

12. Peripheral membrane protein — Peripheral proteins can interact with integral proteins or polar membrane surfaces.

13. Hydrophobic protein region — Nonpolar side chains favor contact with the bilayer's hydrocarbon core.

14. Hydrophilic protein region — Hydrophilic protein surfaces interact favorably with water.

15. Cholesterol — Cholesterol is a membrane steroid and modulates membrane fluidity.

16. Steroid — Cholesterol is a steroid lipid found in animal membranes.

17. Glycoprotein — Glycoproteins can participate in cell recognition and interactions.

18. Glycolipid — Glycolipids are part of the membrane mosaic.

19. Extracellular carbohydrate chain — Many membrane-associated carbohydrates face the extracellular environment.

20. Cytosolic leaflet (inner face) — The inner leaflet faces the cytosolic aqueous environment.

21. Extracellular leaflet (outer face) — The outer leaflet contacts the external aqueous environment.

22. Phospholipid orientation in aqueous environments — Amphipathic organization helps the bilayer form.

23. Mosaic component of the fluid mosaic model — The mosaic describes compositional diversity rather than static rigidity.

24. Membrane receptor protein — Receptor proteins are one functional class of proteins in the membrane mosaic.

25. Plasma membrane — The plasma membrane separates the cytosol from the external environment.

Teacher note: Award one point for each conceptually correct answer. Accept standard biological synonyms and appropriate notation, but use the clue’s defining detail to distinguish closely related structures and mechanisms.
