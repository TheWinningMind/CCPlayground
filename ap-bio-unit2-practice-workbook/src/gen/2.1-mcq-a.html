Framework and cognitive-level summary

Learning objective 2.1.A: Explain how the structure and function of subcellular components and organelles contribute to cell function. Items 1–35 emphasize recall and understanding; items 36–70 emphasize application, analysis, evaluation of evidence, and experimental prediction. Shared-stimulus sets: 21–25, 36–40, 41–45, 46–50, 51–55, and 56–60.

PART I | ANSWERS AND EXPLANATIONS

Question 01 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.1

Ribosomes are non-membranous complexes composed of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and proteins. mRNA serves as a template during protein synthesis but is not a permanent structural component of the ribosome. Distractor analysis: The temporary association of mRNA with a ribosome does not make mRNA one of its defining structural components.

Question 02 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.1

A ribosome is a non-membrane-bound subcellular structure. Membrane-bound organelles, such as mitochondria and chloroplasts, are distinct from ribosomes. Distractor analysis: Membrane-associated ribosomes are attached to rough ER, not enclosed by an ER-derived membrane.

Question 03 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.1

The presence of ribosomes in all forms of life supports the idea that protein synthesis is a conserved process and is consistent with common ancestry. Bacteria do not have nuclei or the full complement of eukaryotic organelles. Distractor analysis: Shared ribosome function does not require every cell to have rough ER or identical ribosomal sequences.

Question 04 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.1

Ribosomes translate the coding information carried by mRNA into a polypeptide. The other features do not specify amino acid order. Distractor analysis: Although a gene in DNA ultimately encodes a protein, the ribosome decodes the mRNA sequence directly.

Question 05 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.2

The endomembrane system includes ER, Golgi complex, nuclear envelope, lysosomes, vacuoles, vesicles, and plasma membrane. These components cooperate in modifying, packaging, and transporting cellular products. Distractor analysis: Mitochondria and chloroplasts are double-membrane organelles but are not components of the endomembrane system.

Question 06 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.3

The ER forms a membrane network that helps support cellular organization and shape. Its network also contributes to intracellular transport.

Question 07 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.3

Ribosomes bound to the cytosolic surface give rough ER its rough appearance. Smooth ER lacks associated ribosomes. Distractor analysis: Rough ER is not double-membrane-bounded; the defining visible feature is surface-associated ribosomes.

Question 08 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.3

Ribosomes associated with rough ER translate polypeptides as they enter the ER compartment en route to secretion. Translation typically begins on a cytosolic ribosome; a targeting signal can direct the ribosome–nascent-chain complex to rough ER, where translation continues. Because the stem specifies entry into rough ER, choice A is the best answer. Distractor analysis: Free ribosomes synthesize many cytosolic proteins, but the ribosome must become ER-associated for the secretory polypeptide to enter the ER during translation.

Question 09 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.3

Smooth ER is involved in lipid synthesis. Translation takes place on ribosomes, digestion occurs in lysosomes, and the Golgi packages many proteins for trafficking. Distractor analysis: Ribosome-mediated translation and Golgi-mediated protein trafficking should not be confused with smooth-ER lipid synthesis.

Question 10 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.3

The smooth ER contributes to detoxification in many cells. Detailed pathways of smooth-ER specialization are not needed for Topic 2.1. Distractor analysis: Rough ER primarily participates in protein synthesis, whereas the CED specifies detoxification as a smooth-ER function.

Question 11 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.4

The Golgi complex consists of flattened membrane sacs and functions in modification, processing, and trafficking of cellular products. Distractor analysis: Cristae, hydrolytic enzymes, and attached ribosomes instead point to mitochondria, lysosomes, and rough ER.

Question 12 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.4

Golgi processing can chemically modify proteins, including modifications that affect function or where a protein is delivered. Translation, ATP generation, and plant water storage occur elsewhere. Distractor analysis: Synthesis of a polypeptide and its entry into rough ER precede the Golgi-dependent modification and sorting stages.

Question 13 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.2

Transport vesicles are membrane-bound carriers that move cargo between endomembrane-system components. They do not directly perform DNA replication, rRNA synthesis, or photosynthesis.

Question 14 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.2

The nuclear envelope is part of the endomembrane system, along with ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles, vesicles, and the plasma membrane. Mitochondrial and chloroplast membranes are not classified as part of this system.

Question 15 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.5, 2.1.A.8

Both mitochondria and chloroplasts are bounded by double membranes. Mitochondria participate in aerobic respiration; chloroplasts are associated with photosynthesis.

Question 16 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.5

The inner mitochondrial membrane forms folds called cristae, increasing membrane surface area associated with efficient ATP production. Figure 2 identifies this as the folded inner membrane.

Question 17 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.5

Mitochondria provide compartments used in aerobic cellular respiration, which is linked to ATP production. The other processes are performed mainly by Golgi, vacuoles, and lysosomes.

Question 18 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.6

Lysosomes are membrane-enclosed sacs that contain hydrolytic enzymes used in intracellular digestion.

Question 19 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.6

The AP Topic 2.1 framework includes a role for lysosomes in apoptosis, alongside their primary digestive function.

Question 20 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.7

The large central vacuole stores water and dissolved substances and helps maintain the internal pressure supporting plant cells. The other functions belong primarily to Golgi, mitochondria, and ribosomes.

Question 21 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.1

Label A points to a ribosome associated with the rough ER. Ribosomes carry out translation and lack a surrounding membrane; B is the ER, C a transport vesicle, and D the Golgi complex.

Question 22 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.3

Label B identifies rough ER. It is associated with ribosomes and supplies a membrane compartment for newly synthesized secretory proteins.

Question 23 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.2

Label C identifies a transport vesicle between rough ER and Golgi. Vesicles move cargo between the major components of the endomembrane system.

Question 24 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.4

Label D is the Golgi complex, recognizable from its stack of flattened sacs. Its functions include modifying proteins and packaging them for transport.

Question 25 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.2

Label E identifies the plasma membrane, the boundary of the cell and a component of the endomembrane system. Secretory vesicles can deliver cargo to this boundary for release.

Question 26 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.7

Animal cells can have multiple relatively small vacuoles for storage, whereas a mature plant cell typically has one prominent central vacuole that also supports turgor. Distractor analysis: The question concerns typical mature cells; exceptions in particular cell types do not reverse the general comparison.

Question 27 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.8

Chloroplasts are characteristic of plants and photosynthetic algae and are the sites of photosynthesis in those organisms. Distractor analysis: Photosynthetic bacteria such as cyanobacteria carry out photosynthesis without chloroplasts.

Question 28 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.8

Chloroplasts possess a double-membrane envelope. This structural feature distinguishes the organelle from several single-membrane endomembrane components. Distractor analysis: The figure specifies a plant-type chloroplast with two surrounding membranes, distinct from its internal thylakoids.

Question 29 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.8

Chloroplasts are the organelles in which photosynthesis occurs in plants and photosynthetic algae.

Question 30 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.5

Mitochondria possess a smooth outer membrane and a highly folded inner membrane. The inner folds support efficient ATP generation.

Question 31 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.3

A membrane network without attached ribosomes is consistent with smooth ER, which supports lipid synthesis and detoxification.

Question 32 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.6, 2.1.A.7

Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes; vacuoles are membrane-bound sacs with storage functions and, in plants, a role in turgor. Both can occur in eukaryotic cells.

Question 33 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.4

The Golgi complex receives many proteins synthesized at rough ER and modifies and packages them for trafficking. This is an essential contribution of the endomembrane system.

Question 34 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.2

Mitochondria and chloroplasts are double-membrane energy-processing organelles that are not part of the endomembrane system. The other pairs are endomembrane components.

Question 35 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.2

The plasma membrane can receive materials delivered by vesicles, contributing to exchange and trafficking between cellular compartments and the cell exterior.

PART II | ANSWERS AND EXPLANATIONS

Question 36 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.2, 2.1.A.4

The proportion of tagged secretory reporter declines in ER, peaks in Golgi at the intermediate time, and later increases in vesicles/extracellular medium. This sequential pattern supports traffic along the secretory endomembrane route; the other models conflict with the order of observations. Distractor analysis: Only a time-dependent shift in localization is supported; percentages alone cannot prove that no degradation occurred.

Question 37 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.2, 2.1.A.3

Blocking the exit of ER cargo predicts retention of the reporter in the ER and less subsequent appearance in Golgi and the extracellular fraction. The intervention does not imply changes to ribosome abundance or photosynthesis. Distractor analysis: Blocking vesicle export from ER should retain reporter within ER rather than increase the proportion in Golgi or outside.

Question 38 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.4

When onward packaging is blocked while synthesis and ER-to-Golgi delivery continue, reporter cargo is expected to build up in the Golgi. This is a prediction about trafficking, not about the loss of mRNA. Distractor analysis: A block in Golgi export is downstream from ER-to-Golgi delivery, so Golgi-associated reporter should accumulate.

Question 39 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.2

If the total recovered reporter remains approximately constant while its location changes, relocation is better supported than extensive degradation. A substantial decline in total recovered tag would warrant investigating degradation or loss. Distractor analysis: Because each distribution is reported as percentages summing to 100, an absolute total-recovery measurement is needed to assess loss of labeled material.

Question 40 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.1, 2.1.A.3

Blocking ribosome-mediated translation before the pulse predicts less newly synthesized protein available to enter all later secretory compartments. This connects ribosome structure/function to the pathway. Distractor analysis: An inhibitor of translation decreases the amount of newly synthesized reporter, not simply the rate of its later movement.

Question 41 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.2, 2.1.A.3

X shows high reporter accumulation in rough ER (80 units) but low reporter in Golgi (12 units), together with low secretion (28 units). This is most consistent with impaired traffic after ER synthesis and before Golgi arrival.

Question 42 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.4

Y has high reporter accumulation in the Golgi (85 units) and low secretion (35 units) while ER signal is near control. The pattern is consistent with a late Golgi/export trafficking defect.

Question 43 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.3

Z strongly decreases the lipid-synthesis index (32 units) but leaves secretion and the reporter distribution close to untreated values. Smooth ER has a major function in lipid synthesis.

Question 44 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.1, 2.1.A.2

If reporter synthesis is normal but reporter accumulates in ER, the secretion defect is more parsimoniously explained by trafficking rather than translation. The alternatives provide little relevant evidence about reporter synthesis. Distractor analysis: A normal initial synthesis rate directly contradicts the assertion of failed translation; accumulated ER cargo alone cannot establish synthesis failure.

Question 45 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.4

Restoring the implicated trafficking step and observing recovery in secretion directly tests a causal link. Measurements unrelated to reporter trafficking do not isolate the proposed mechanism. Distractor analysis: Restoration of the suspected impaired step plus recovery of secretion is a direct functional rescue test.

Question 46 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.5

The AP framework identifies folded inner mitochondrial membranes as structures supporting efficient ATP synthesis. The reduced fold count in M is consistent with reduced area for ATP-producing processes under comparable conditions. Distractor analysis: The folded inner membrane, not the outer membrane, is where much of the ATP-producing machinery is situated.

Question 47 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.5

The control ATP-output index is 100, and M has an index of 58. The decrease is (100 - 58)/100 × 100 = 42%.

Question 48 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.5

The rescue group restores the fold count from 9 to 19 and ATP index from 58 to 94. This co-recovery strengthens a structure-function hypothesis, though additional tests would help establish a specific mechanistic cause. Distractor analysis: Recovery of both structure and function offers stronger support than an uncontrolled comparison alone, but it does not prove causation.

Question 49 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.5

Normalizing ATP production to equal amounts of mitochondrial protein helps distinguish changes in ATP-producing performance from differences in total mitochondrial material loaded into each assay. A matched oxygen and substrate supply remains important but is already controlled in the stimulus. Distractor analysis: Varying oxygen introduces a confounding variable; equal numbers of whole cells need not contain equal mitochondrial amounts; cytosolic protein does not provide a mitochondrial-specific normalization.

Question 50 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.5

Fold number and ATP output covary in the experimental groups, but other conditions and components of aerobic respiration could influence ATP output. An absolute claim about all cells goes beyond these results. Distractor analysis: Association within tested groups cannot establish an exclusive, universal cause.

Question 51 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.6

Reducing lysosomal hydrolytic activity lowers digestion from 84% to 23%, and restoring activity raises digestion to 79%. The lysosome counts remain nearly constant, supporting the importance of enzyme function. Distractor analysis: Nearly equal lysosome numbers do not explain the large functional differences when enzyme activity changes.

Question 52 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.6

The counts (20, 21, and 20 lysosomes per cell) remain similar across groups. Thus reduced abundance does not account for the large differences in digestion, although other causal explanations may still require controls.

Question 53 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.6

Equivalent initial uptake controls for differences in how much substrate reaches cells. Without that control, lower digestion values could partly reflect differences in cargo availability rather than lysosomal enzyme function. Distractor analysis: Equal amounts provided to cultures need not imply that equal amounts entered the cells; uptake must be measured.

Question 54 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.6

Only 23% of internalized cargo is digested when lysosomal enzymes are impaired, compared with 84% and 79% in the other groups. Consequently, the impaired group should retain the most undigested cargo. Distractor analysis: The group with the lowest fraction digested retains the greatest fraction undigested when uptake is matched.

Question 55 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.6

The fraction of cells undergoing apoptosis after the standardized stress falls from 42% to 18% when lysosomal activity is impaired and returns to 39% upon restoration. This supports a role in this model but not a universal assertion for every form of apoptosis. Distractor analysis: The association is specific to the treatment and stress conditions tested; it does not imply a universal pathway.

Question 56 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.8

At the same light condition, disrupting chloroplasts decreases the photosynthesis index from 100 to 15. This is consistent with chloroplasts being the location of photosynthesis.

Question 57 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.8

The comparison between intact algal cells in light (100) and in darkness (8) changes illumination while holding chloroplast condition constant. This supports evaluating the light-dependence of measured photosynthetic output.

Question 58 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.8

Chloroplast-damaged algae under illumination have an index of 15, whereas intact algae in light have 100. Light was supplied to both groups, so an absence of light cannot solely explain the difference. Distractor analysis: Both intact and damaged groups were exposed to light in the decisive comparison.

Question 59 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.8

If both groups remain similarly viable during measurement, the large difference in photosynthetic output is less likely to be a nonspecific consequence of generalized cell death. This helps focus the interpretation on organelle function. Distractor analysis: Similar short-term viability isolates the organelle-specific interpretation better than counting an unrelated structure.

Question 60 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.8

Figure 4 shows the double-membrane organization of a chloroplast, and the experiment connects chloroplast damage with lower photosynthetic output. The other options misidentify chloroplast structure and function.

Question 61 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.1

Both ribosome types use mRNA to direct protein synthesis, supporting functional conservation across life. Conservation does not imply that organisms share all organelles or that every ribosomal feature is identical. Distractor analysis: Functional conservation supports shared ancestry without implying identical protein sequences or membranes in every cell.

Question 62 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.3

High secretory-protein production is associated with rough ER and membrane-bound ribosomes, while lipid production is associated with smooth ER. These predicted structural differences reflect cellular specialization. Distractor analysis: Secretory peptide production calls for rough-ER-associated ribosomes, whereas lipid synthesis is associated with smooth ER.

Question 63 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.4

The protein is produced and trafficked but shows abnormal chemical processing, making defective Golgi modification the strongest explanation. Ribosome failure would impair synthesis, which was observed to be normal. Distractor analysis: Both normal synthesis and successful passage to Golgi rule out a complete ribosome or ER-to-Golgi failure.

Question 64 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.2

Cargo present in ER-derived vesicles indicates synthesis and at least some vesicle formation occurred. Reduced arrival at Golgi is consistent with defective movement or delivery of those vesicles. Distractor analysis: The presence of tagged cargo in ER-budded vesicles means synthesis and vesicle budding occurred.

Question 65 | Correct answer: D | 2.1.A.3

Smooth ER plays a role in detoxification, so an expansion under the stated conditions is consistent with increased capacity for that activity. The observation alone does not establish the extent of any increase. Distractor analysis: A correlation between membrane expansion and detoxification demand does not prove any particular quantitative change.

Question 66 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.7

The large plant central vacuole helps maintain turgor through water storage. A loss of vacuolar water-storage capacity is expected to reduce that support without directly abolishing unrelated organelles. Distractor analysis: Reduced storage of water can reduce vacuolar turgor despite the presence of a cell wall.

Question 67 | Correct answer: C | 2.1.A.7

Cell A has the large, dominant vacuole characteristic of many mature plant cells; Cell B has smaller, more numerous vacuoles, a pattern more typical of animal cells. Vacuole structure supports a reasoned inference but does not prove the identity of either cell without additional evidence. Distractor analysis: A large vacuole alone does not demonstrate photosynthesis, nor do smaller vacuoles mean that other organelles are absent.

Question 68 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.6

A reversible, selectively altered lysosomal function that changes apoptotic response supports a causal contribution in the tested model. Observations unrelated to apoptosis offer weaker evidence. Distractor analysis: A reversible change in apoptosis after selective impairment and rescue is stronger evidence than correlation alone.

Question 69 | Correct answer: B | 2.1.A.4, 2.1.A.5

The accumulation of protein in Golgi together with low secretion indicates a trafficking or packaging defect, while ATP measurements suggest that a broad mitochondrial energy deficit is not necessary to explain it. Distractor analysis: Normal ATP output does not rule out an independent Golgi trafficking defect.

Question 70 | Correct answer: A | 2.1.A.1, 2.1.A.2

Both cultures have comparable ribosome counts, but they differ in the membrane structures that help process and traffic secreted proteins. This supports the importance of coordinated organelle structure and function rather than ribosome count alone. Distractor analysis: Ribosome number alone is insufficient to account for differences in secretion when secretory-pathway structures differ.
