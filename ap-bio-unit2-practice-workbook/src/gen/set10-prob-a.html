Origins of Cell Compartmentalization: Evolutionary Evidence

Problem 1 | Sequence similarities in organelles and bacteria

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Homolog sampled Identity to mitochondrial gene (%)
Bacterial lineage X 89
Eukaryotic nuclear homolog Y 54
Unrelated cellular lineage Z 41

(a) Identify the closest sequence match to the mitochondrial gene. [2 points]

  • Point 1: The bacterial lineage has the highest identity (89%).

  • Point 2: The other sampled sequences are less similar (54% and 41%).

(b) Explain why the pattern is consistent with endosymbiotic origin. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Mitochondrial genetic sequences share substantial similarity with some bacterial homologs.

  • Point 2: This is consistent with an ancestral bacterium contributing to the organelle lineage.

(c) Evaluate why percent identity in one gene is insufficient to prove a unique evolutionary history. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Different genes can evolve at different rates.

  • Point 2: A single gene can be affected by sampling, transfer, or convergent constraints.

  • Point 3: A stronger inference uses multiple independent genes and structural evidence.

(d) Propose one additional observation that would strengthen the claim. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Mitochondria contain their own circular DNA or bacterial-like ribosomes.

  • Point 2: Such structural/molecular characteristics supply evidence independent of the sequence comparison.

Total for Problem 1: 9 points.

Problem 4 | Comparing competing evolutionary hypotheses

9 POINTS | Extended response

Evidence supplied in the problem:

Evidence Chloroplast observation
Genome Small circular DNA molecule present
Ribosomes Bacterial-like characteristics
Sequence homology Close matches to cyanobacterial genes

(a) Identify evidence predicted more strongly by Hypothesis E. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Chloroplast genetic sequences should be closely related to those of photosynthetic bacteria.

  • Point 2: Chloroplasts may retain bacterial-like ribosomes or DNA.

(b) Describe an evidence pattern that favors Hypothesis E over I. [2 points]

  • Point 1: High similarity to bacterial genes and presence of circular DNA support E.

  • Point 2: The pattern is more directly expected from bacterial ancestry than from de novo host membrane origin.

(c) Explain why retained nuclear genes derived from the symbiont would not contradict Hypothesis E. [3 points]

  • Point 1: Gene transfer from the ancestral symbiont to the host nucleus can occur.

  • Point 2: A descendant organelle may depend on nuclear-encoded proteins.

  • Point 3: These possibilities remain consistent with the original bacterial ancestry.

(d) Propose a comparative investigation that could test the hypotheses. [2 points]

  • Point 1: Construct a phylogeny using several homologous chloroplast and bacterial genes.

  • Point 2: Determine whether chloroplast sequences cluster with photosynthetic bacterial lineages.

Total for Problem 4: 9 points.

Problem 5 | Comparing prokaryotic and eukaryotic organization

4 POINTS | Short response

(a) Identify which cell contains a nucleus. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Plant cell.

(b) Identify which sample has ribosomes. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Both samples.

(c) State one organizational difference. [1 point]

  • Point 1: The plant cell has several membrane-bound organelles; the bacterium lacks those typical eukaryotic compartments.

(d) Explain why shared ribosomes do not imply identical cell organization. [1 point]

  • Point 1: Ribosomes are common to cellular life while many internal membrane compartments differ among lineages.

Total for Problem 5: 4 points.
