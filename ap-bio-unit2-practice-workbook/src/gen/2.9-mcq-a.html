Question 1 | D | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Isolating S with enzyme A shifts more substrate toward P without changing the initial substrate amount.

Explanation: Product P rises from 40 to 72 nmol while W falls from 40 to 8 nmol; S initially and the enzyme quantities were held constant.

Common misconception addressed: The stimulus holds the inputs constant. Greater P does not prove a faster enzyme A or irreversible inactivation of enzyme B.

Question 2 | D | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: 80%

Explanation: (72 − 40)/40 × 100 = 80%.

Common misconception addressed: The 32 nmol increase is an absolute difference; percent increase uses 40 nmol as the denominator, not 72.

Question 3 | C | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Product P is intermediate between the no-compartment and intact-compartment values.

Explanation: The disrupted group produces 45 nmol P, between 40 and 72 nmol, as expected for greater competition than in the intact compartment.

Common misconception addressed: Partial leakage changes substrate access, not the initial stock of substrate or the presence of both enzymes.

Question 4 | B | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: The total amounts of both enzymes and the initial substrate are held constant.

Explanation: Matching enzymes, substrate, and incubation time reduces alternative explanations for product differences.

Common misconception addressed: An effective comparison varies membrane integrity rather than simultaneously varying enzymes or starting substrate.

Question 5 | A | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Purified enzyme A has the same catalytic activity in samples from all three groups when tested under identical conditions.

Explanation: Equal intrinsic activity in a common assay helps separate enzyme properties from access to the substrate in the cells.

Common misconception addressed: Equal abundance is not equivalent to equal catalytic activity; testing purified A under shared conditions resolves that confound.

Question 6 | D | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: A membrane-bounded lumen can maintain a chemical environment different from the cytosol.

Explanation: Membrane compartmentalization makes distinct local conditions possible; lysosomes are bounded by one membrane, not two.

Common misconception addressed: A lysosomal lumen is a single-membrane compartment; distinct pH does not require the whole cell to become acidic.

Question 7 | D | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: The measured hydrolase activity will decrease under the specified assay conditions.

Explanation: Relative activity declines from 100 at pH 5.0 to 28 at pH 6.5 in the controlled assay.

Common misconception addressed: Unchanged enzyme number cannot ensure unchanged catalytic rate if the local environment changes.

Question 8 | D | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: About 3.6 times

Explanation: 100/28 ≈ 3.57.

Common misconception addressed: The ratio is 100 ÷ 28; interpreting 28% activity as 28-fold lower would confuse percentages and fold changes.

Question 9 | B | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: Restoring the lysosomal lumen to approximately pH 5.0.

Explanation: The observed enzyme response indicates that returning the reaction environment to its favorable pH could restore activity.

Common misconception addressed: Returning toward the favorable lumen pH is supported; simply changing cytosolic pH is not the same intervention.

Question 10 | A | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: Quantifying hydrolase protein per cell in both treatment groups.

Explanation: Protein abundance analysis separates a quantity change from the measured effect of lumen pH.

Common misconception addressed: Product formation and pH measurement alone do not directly quantify how many enzyme molecules remain.

Question 11 | D | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: A larger membrane area can accommodate more membrane-associated enzyme molecules at the same density.

Explanation: With equal enzyme density and other factors controlled, increasing area increases the number of available membrane enzyme sites.

Common misconception addressed: At equal density, higher area can accommodate more enzymes; it does not establish a change in turnover for each enzyme.

Question 12 | B | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: 100%

Explanation: (40 − 20)/20 × 100 = 100%.

Common misconception addressed: The increase is 20 on a baseline of 20; system C is 200% of system A, corresponding to a 100% increase.

Question 13 | C | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Change membrane folding while maintaining enclosed volume and enzyme density per membrane area.

Explanation: Changing folding alters available area while holding competing variables constant.

Common misconception addressed: A controlled manipulation must avoid changing area, enzyme amount and substrate simultaneously.

Question 14 | C | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Lower total product formation because fewer membrane-associated enzymes can operate.

Explanation: Less area supports fewer membrane-bound enzymes when density is held constant.

Common misconception addressed: Volume is held stable; the proposed effect follows from changed area, not automatically from a change in organelle volume.

Question 15 | A | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Increasing membrane surface area always increases every cellular reaction rate, regardless of enzyme abundance or substrate supply.

Explanation: The inference is conditional; other processes or limiting factors could prevent a general increase.

Common misconception addressed: A relationship in this model cannot justify universal claims about every reaction in every cell.

Question 16 | A | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Localizing successive enzymes in the same membrane region can increase final-product formation.

Explanation: The same-region condition yields 84 nmol final product compared with 48 nmol for dispersed enzymes under equal quantities.

Common misconception addressed: Higher pathway yield does not prove direct physical binding between X and Y or faster intrinsic catalysis.

Question 17 | C | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: 36 nmol

Explanation: 84 − 48 = 36 nmol.

Common misconception addressed: The correct difference subtracts the dispersed value (48) from the co-localized value (84).

Question 18 | D | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: The third arrangement, with 59 nmol of final product.

Explanation: The third condition matches the altered localization of Y while X remains associated with the membrane.

Common misconception addressed: Mislocalizing only Y corresponds to the third arrangement; it does not remove both enzymes or guarantee unchanged output.

Question 19 | A | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Measure and match total amounts of enzyme X and enzyme Y across all arrangements.

Explanation: Matching enzyme abundances isolates spatial organization as a variable.

Common misconception addressed: Matching total enzyme amounts is not enough if the relative amounts of X and Y differ across conditions.

Question 20 | C | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: The two enzymes must form a stable protein-protein complex when localized together.

Explanation: Co-localization and yield do not demonstrate direct molecular binding; additional interaction assays would be required.

Common misconception addressed: Co-localization describes position, not necessarily a stable protein-protein complex; binding needs independent evidence.

Question 21 | A | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: Nuclear envelope

Explanation: The double-membraned nuclear envelope delimits a specialized nuclear environment.

Common misconception addressed: Ribosomes and centrosomes are important structures, but they do not enclose the nucleoplasm.

Question 22 | D | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: Its surrounding membrane selectively regulates movement between the region and the cytosol.

Explanation: A limiting membrane helps maintain distinct molecular composition and conditions; it does not exclude all water.

Common misconception addressed: Enzyme concentration without a separating lipid boundary does not alone create a membrane-bounded compartment.

Question 23 | A | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: The membrane-bound compartment encloses a region separated from cytosol by a lipid bilayer.

Explanation: A lipid-bilayer boundary defines membrane-bounded compartments; nonmembranous complexes can also be highly functional.

Common misconception addressed: Proteins and enzymatic complexes can function without an enclosing bilayer; not all organelles are double membrane bound.

Question 24 | C | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Output also depends on substrate availability, enzyme activity, and coordination among compartments.

Explanation: Compartmentation is a potential advantage, not a guarantee of higher throughput if other factors limit metabolism.

Common misconception addressed: Compartment number alone does not establish substrate supply, catalytic ability or pathway coordination.

Question 25 | A | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Folds of the inner membrane (cristae)

Explanation: Cristae expand inner membrane area; their specific ATP synthesis roles are explored more deeply in Unit 3.

Common misconception addressed: Cristae are folds of the inner, not outer, mitochondrial membrane.

Question 26 | C | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: Two enzymes with different optimal conditions are found in separate membrane-enclosed regions.

Explanation: Spatial separation and different local conditions are direct evidence of functional compartmentation.

Common misconception addressed: Physical isolation plus differing localized conditions supports functional compartmentation; uniform cytosol does not.

Question 27 | A | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: Compartment-specific substrates and enzymes become less spatially isolated from other cellular reactions.

Explanation: Disrupting a membrane boundary changes local isolation and can expose other regions to released contents.

Common misconception addressed: Loss of the membrane alters separation; not every protein or membrane in the cell must disappear.

Question 28 | B | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: Maintaining conditions suited to that set of reactions without imposing them on the whole cytosol.

Explanation: A distinct lumen can support specialized conditions while the cytosol maintains different conditions.

Common misconception addressed: Compartmentation permits different local conditions but does not eliminate controlled molecular exchange.

Question 29 | B | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: More membrane surface can provide additional sites for membrane-associated enzymes.

Explanation: Area can increase available sites, conditional on sufficient enzyme and substrate.

Common misconception addressed: Area expands potential sites; substrate availability and enzyme supply may still limit production.

Question 30 | B | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: The enzyme is no longer concentrated in the compartment where its substrate or partners are available.

Explanation: Mislocalization can reduce access to the correct substrates or partners even if total enzyme quantity is unchanged.

Common misconception addressed: Equal total enzyme abundance is consistent with a targeting defect causing mislocalization.

Question 31 | B | 2.9.A.1

Correct option: Lumen of a lysosome

Explanation: The lysosomal lumen is surrounded by a lipid bilayer; a ribosome and cytoskeletal polymers are not membrane-bounded compartments.

Common misconception addressed: Only the lysosomal lumen among the listed options is enclosed by a membrane.

Question 32 | B | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: Compartments can limit unwanted interactions while still requiring regulated exchange of metabolites and products.

Explanation: Functional compartments balance separation with selective traffic, not total impermeability.

Common misconception addressed: Selective traffic and compartment boundaries coexist; neither perfect sealing nor abolition of enzymes is implied.

Question 33 | C | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: A lower potential number of membrane-associated reaction sites.

Explanation: Reduced membrane area can reduce available binding/embedding sites for membrane enzymes.

Common misconception addressed: Loss of folds reduces available area for membrane-associated sites, even when total cell volume is unchanged.

Question 34 | B | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: A greater fraction of substrate becomes the product of enzyme A in the sequestering line.

Explanation: Improved partitioning into the A pathway at matched inputs supports the spatial separation hypothesis.

Common misconception addressed: Evidence must compare equal inputs; unequal starting substrate or elimination of enzyme B changes the question.

Question 35 | C | 2.9.B.1

Correct option: They separate potentially competing processes and expand surfaces for certain membrane-associated reactions.

Explanation: The official scope identifies both reduction of competing interactions and increased reaction area.

Common misconception addressed: Both separation of competing processes and area expansion are supported; none removes the need for exchange or substrates.
