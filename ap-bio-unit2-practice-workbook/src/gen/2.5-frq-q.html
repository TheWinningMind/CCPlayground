FRQ 1 · Energy dependence of uphill solute uptake (9 points)

Cultured cells are placed in a medium that maintains solute Y at 5 mM. Initially, the cells contain 25 mM Y. Researchers measure the intracellular Y concentration after 10 minutes in three groups. A second freely permeant tracer Z begins at 5 mM inside and 20 mM outside. Researchers verify that the membrane remains intact in all groups. Data represent means from independent trials.

Schematic reference model for this investigation; not drawn to molecular scale.

Treatment Y inside, 0 min (mM) Y inside, 10 min (mM) Z inside, 10 min (mM)
Untreated 25 35 11
Energy restricted 25 26 11
Energy restored after restriction 25 34 11

(a) Describe the direction of the Y concentration gradient at the start and identify the direction of net Y movement in the untreated group. (2 points)

(b) Calculate the change in intracellular Y concentration in the untreated group over the 10-minute interval. Show your work and units. (1 point)

(c) Explain how the treatment results support an energy-dependent mechanism for net inward Y transport. Refer to two specific observations. (2 points)

(d) Propose a follow-up investigation that would test whether the reduction in Y uptake is due to energy restriction rather than damage to the plasma membrane. Identify a controlled variable and a measurable outcome. (2 points)

(e) Predict the result of returning restricted cells to an energy-sufficient medium if the inhibition is reversible. (1 point)

(f) Justify why the similar Z uptake in untreated and energy-restricted cells is useful evidence in evaluating the model. (1 point)

FRQ 2 · Quantifying vesicle-mediated uptake and secretion (9 points)

Researchers study a eukaryotic cell population that internalizes fluorescent beads and separately releases a preloaded fluorescent protein. They use a treatment thought to interfere with membrane remodeling. External bead fluorescence is quenched before internalized puncta are counted. Extracellular secreted protein is measured in the medium. Cell survival exceeds 92% in both groups.

Schematic reference model for this investigation; not drawn to molecular scale.

Measurement at 10 min Control Membrane-remodeling treatment
Mean internal bead vesicles/cell 12 3
Extracellular secreted protein (relative units) 42 12
Mean internal bead vesicles/cell at 0 min 1 1

(a) Identify the mechanism of bead uptake and the mechanism of protein release. (2 points)

(b) Calculate the fold increase in the mean number of internal bead vesicles in control cells between 0 and 10 minutes. (1 point)

(c) Use the data to justify a claim that the treatment reduces both uptake and release. Include one quantitative comparison for each process. (2 points)

(d) Propose an investigation to distinguish a defect in protein secretion by vesicle fusion from a defect in initial production or packaging of the protein. Specify a measurable result that would distinguish the mechanisms. (2 points)

(e) Predict the change in intracellular protein-containing vesicles if packaging continues but membrane fusion is inhibited. (1 point)

(f) Explain why monitoring cell survival is important when interpreting extracellular fluorescence. (1 point)

8 55 45

(a) Identify the initial direction of net solute movement. (1 point)

Schematic reference model for this investigation; not drawn to molecular scale.

An experiment measures net inward movement of two solutes over the same ten-minute period. Solute R begins at 10 mM outside and 30 mM inside. Solute S begins at 60 mM outside and 10 mM inside. The cell membrane is permeable to S. Researchers compare normally functioning cells with cells whose energy availability is temporarily lowered, while monitoring cell integrity.

Group Change in internal R (mM) Change in internal S (mM)
Control +8 +12
Low-energy +1 +11

(a) Identify which solute shows net inward movement against its stated concentration gradient. (1 point)

(b) Calculate the difference between the control and low-energy changes in internal R concentration. (1 point)

(c) Explain why the different responses of R and S support an energy-dependent contribution to R uptake. (1 point)

(d) Identify one additional result that would strengthen the causal conclusion that low energy, rather than general cell damage, reduced R uptake. (1 point)
