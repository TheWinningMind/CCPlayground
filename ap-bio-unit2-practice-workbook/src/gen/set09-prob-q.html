Cell Compartmentalization: Reaction Environments

Problem 1 | Localized enzyme activity and pH

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 3.C, 4.B, 6.E

Scientists measure an enzyme that normally acts within an acidic organelle. The enzyme is assayed in buffer at two pH values, using identical substrate concentration and temperature.

Assay pH Enzyme activity (units/min)
5.0 82
7.0 21

(a) Calculate the difference in reaction rate between the two buffers. [2 points]

(b) Explain why maintaining a distinct organelle environment can be advantageous. [2 points]

(c) Propose a way to test whether an intact organelle membrane is necessary to maintain the low internal pH. [3 points]

(d) Predict the enzyme activity after membrane disruption if lumenal pH approaches 7. [2 points]

Problem 2 | Separating two incompatible reactions

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.E

Reaction A functions best at pH 5; reaction B functions best at pH 7. A eukaryotic cell carries out both reactions.

(a) Identify one organizational feature that can allow both reactions. [1 point]

(b) Explain the role of the separating membrane. [1 point]

(c) Predict a consequence of eliminating the separation. [1 point]

(d) State why both reactions need not be in different cells. [1 point]

Problem 3 | Comparing inner-membrane area

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 2.A, 6.B

Two mitochondria have equal external dimensions, but the inner membrane of mitochondrion A has twice the surface area of mitochondrion B.

(a) Identify the feature producing the additional inner-membrane area. [1 point]

(b) Predict the capacity for membrane-associated processes under otherwise equal conditions. [1 point]

(c) Explain why internal membrane area matters. [1 point]

(d) State why doubling area does not prove a doubling of actual metabolic rate. [1 point]

Problem 4 | The consequences of breaking a compartment barrier

EXTENDED PROBLEM • 9 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 3.C, 6.B, 6.E

An isolated membrane-bound compartment contains a substrate-converting enzyme. A treatment makes the surrounding membrane permeable to protons without directly denaturing the enzyme. Rates and compartment pH are measured.

Condition Compartment pH Conversion rate (units/min)
Intact membrane 5.0 75
Proton-permeable membrane 6.5 28

(a) Describe the treatment-associated changes. [2 points]

(b) Explain a mechanism connecting the two observations. [2 points]

(c) Propose a test to distinguish loss of enzyme protein from loss of a favorable environment. [3 points]

(d) Predict whether selectively restoring acidic lumenal conditions should improve activity. [2 points]

Problem 5 | Preventing pathway interference

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 1.A, 6.E

Enzyme A modifies a substrate to product P; enzyme B degrades P. The cell places enzymes A and B in different organelles.

(a) Identify the advantage of separating the enzymes. [1 point]

(b) Predict an effect if the enzymes are mixed and simultaneously active. [1 point]

(c) Explain how membrane separation controls the interaction. [1 point]

(d) State a limitation of assuming compartmentalization always increases reaction rate. [1 point]

Problem 6 | Membrane folding and biochemical organization

SHORT PROBLEM • 4 POINTS | Skill emphasis: 2.D, 6.B

A cell type increases the folds of a specialized intracellular membrane without changing total cell volume.

(a) Predict what happens to intracellular membrane area. [1 point]

(b) Identify the main type of molecules often embedded in membranes to carry out localized reactions. [1 point]

(c) Explain a potential functional benefit. [1 point]

(d) State one empirical measurement needed to support an increase in actual cellular performance. [1 point]
