Learning objective(s): 2.1.A • 25 retrieval prompts • Answers with explanations

01. Ribosome — Ribosomes occur in all known forms of cellular life and carry out translation.

02. Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) — rRNA combines with ribosomal proteins to form the ribosome.

03. Free ribosome — Free ribosomes are not permanently associated with the ER.

04. ER-bound ribosome — Targeting to the ER occurs during translation of many secreted or membrane proteins.

05. Rough endoplasmic reticulum (rough ER) — The rough ER contains bound ribosomes and receives many proteins destined for secretion or membranes.

06. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum (smooth ER) — Smooth ER is particularly associated with lipid production and related functions.

07. Golgi apparatus (Golgi complex) — The Golgi helps process and direct proteins and lipids in the endomembrane system.

08. Transport vesicle — Vesicles transfer material between components of the endomembrane system.

09. Endomembrane system — These membranes work together in synthesis, modification, packaging, and transport.

10. Nuclear envelope — The nuclear envelope is continuous with the endoplasmic reticulum.

11. Lysosome — Lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes used in intracellular digestion.

12. Hydrolytic enzyme (hydrolase) — Hydrolytic enzymes cleave biological molecules during digestion.

13. Central vacuole — The central vacuole contributes to plant-cell storage and support.

14. Tonoplast — The tonoplast separates vacuolar contents from the cytosol.

15. Mitochondrion — Mitochondrial structures support ATP generation during cellular respiration.

16. Cristae — Cristae provide additional inner-membrane surface area.

17. Mitochondrial matrix — The matrix is a distinct compartment within a mitochondrion.

18. Chloroplast — Chloroplasts contain internal thylakoid membranes.

19. Thylakoid — Thylakoid membranes house photosynthetic pigments and related proteins.

20. Granum — A granum is one stack; grana is the plural term.

21. Stroma — The stroma is a chloroplast compartment distinct from the thylakoid interior.

22. Glycosylation — Glycosylation can influence protein function, stability, or targeting.

23. Secretory pathway — Secreted proteins are processed and transported through the endomembrane system.

24. Secretory vesicle — Secretory vesicles deliver cargo to the surface for exocytosis.

25. Plasma membrane — The plasma membrane receives membrane and cargo during vesicle fusion.

Teacher note: Award one point for each conceptually correct answer. Accept standard biological synonyms and appropriate notation, but use the clue’s defining detail to distinguish closely related structures and mechanisms.
