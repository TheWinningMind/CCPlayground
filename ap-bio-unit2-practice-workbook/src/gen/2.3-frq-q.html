FRQ 1 · Structure–Function Analysis of a Transmembrane Protein [9 points]

An investigator compares two cell populations expressing comparable amounts of a transmembrane protein. The wild-type protein contains a hydrophobic membrane-contacting region (B) between two water-exposed regions (A and C). The experimental variant has six surface nonpolar amino acids in region B replaced with charged amino acids. All membranes contain equivalent phospholipid amounts. The investigator measures the percentage of total protein detected in isolated plasma membranes.

Figure associated with FRQ 1; schematic, not to scale.

Protein Total expression (arbitrary units) Membrane-associated fraction (%)
Wild type 100 88
Variant 102 31

(a) [2 points] Describe how phospholipid head and tail orientation produces two different chemical environments in a bilayer.

(b) [2 points] Explain why the wild-type region B can associate with the membrane interior while regions A and C can contact extracellular fluid and cytosol.

(c) [2 points] Calculate the difference in percentage points between wild type and variant membrane association, and determine the relative decrease as a percentage of the wild-type value. Show your work.

(d) [2 points] Use the results to support a claim that the amino acid substitutions affect membrane association; identify one limitation of the evidence.

(e) [1 point] Predict what would happen to membrane association if the variant’s charged region-B residues were replaced with nonpolar residues, assuming the overall protein folds normally.

FRQ 2 · Testing the Fluid Mosaic Model with Photobleaching [9 points]

Researchers fluorescently label two membrane-associated proteins in living cells. A brief laser pulse photobleaches a small defined region. They measure fluorescence recovery in the same region for 60 seconds as a percentage of the fluorescence immediately before photobleaching. The reported values are means from replicate cells; the experiment does not directly track individual molecules. Assume protein synthesis is negligible during this time.

Figure associated with FRQ 2; schematic, not to scale.

Time (s) Protein A fluorescence (%) Protein B fluorescence (%)
0 15 14
10 39 18
20 55 21
30 65 24
40 71 26
60 74 29

(a) [2 points] Describe what is measured after bleaching and explain how recovery is consistent with the fluid mosaic model.

(b) [2 points] Calculate the average fluorescence recovery rate, in percentage points per second, for each protein from 0 to 20 seconds. Show your work.

(c) [2 points] Identify the protein showing greater recovery at 60 seconds and use numerical evidence to justify your identification.

(d) [2 points] Propose a controlled experimental manipulation to test whether the recovery depends on lateral movement of membrane proteins. State the predicted observation if that explanation is supported.

(e) [1 point] Explain why the 60-second measurements cannot establish that Protein B is permanently immobile.

FRQ 3 · Evaluating Two Bilayer Models [4 points]

Two students construct schematic models of a plasma membrane. Model 1 shows phosphate-containing heads contacting aqueous solutions on both sides and hydrocarbon tails pointing inward. Model 2 places hydrophilic heads at the membrane midplane and nonpolar tails in contact with both aqueous solutions. Both models omit proteins for simplicity.

Figure associated with FRQ 3; schematic, not to scale.

(a) [1 point] Identify which model is most consistent with phospholipid amphipathic properties.

(b) [2 points] Explain the molecular reason that the selected model is more stable in aqueous conditions.

(c) [1 point] Describe one limitation of either model as a complete representation of the fluid mosaic membrane.

FRQ 4 · Cholesterol and Membrane Organization [4 points]

Investigators compare matched vertebrate cell groups. A treatment reduces the amount of cholesterol present in the plasma membrane. A mobility assay reports the percentage of labeled membrane proteins that change position within two minutes. All cells are kept at the same temperature, and total labeled protein amounts do not differ between groups.

Group Relative membrane cholesterol Mobile protein fraction (%)
Control 1.00 61
Treatment 0.55 76

(a) [1 point] Identify the experimental independent variable.

(b) [1 point] Calculate the difference in the mobile protein fraction between treatment and control.

(c) [1 point] Use the data to describe the relationship observed between cholesterol content and membrane-protein mobility.

(d) [1 point] Explain why the results should not be interpreted to mean that decreasing cholesterol always increases mobility in every membrane.
