How to Use This Guide

Unit 1 is the foundation of the entire AP Biology course. Every later unit—cell membranes, enzymes, cellular respiration, DNA replication, gene expression, evolution—depends on the chemistry you learn here. Students who truly understand why water behaves the way it does and how structure determines function in macromolecules find the rest of the course far easier.

This guide is organized around the six topics of AP Biology Unit 1. Each topic follows the same pattern so you always know where to look:

  • Learning Targets — what you should be able to do by the end of the lesson. Use them as a checklist.
  • The Lesson — a clear explanation of the content, with diagrams and tables.
  • Misconception Alerts — the mistakes that cost students points year after year.
  • Exam Tips — how the idea tends to be tested and how to phrase strong answers.
  • Key Terms — the vocabulary you must be able to use precisely.
  • Check Your Understanding — exam-style multiple-choice questions. Full explanations are in Part 4.

After the lessons, four mind maps summarize each macromolecule on a single page, and the Who Am I? Vocabulary Challenge in Part 3 gives you 175 quick clues to sharpen your vocabulary.

A Four-Week Study Plan

Week 4Study the mind maps, the Master Comparison, and the One-Page Review Sheet. Work through the Who Am I? Vocabulary Challenge. Take the full Practice Test under timed conditions, score it, and record your results in the Score Tracker.

Study Habits That Work

  • Retrieve, don’t reread. Close the book and write down everything you remember about a topic, then check. Testing yourself is far more effective than highlighting.
  • Draw it. If you can sketch a water molecule’s hydrogen bonds, an amino acid, and a nucleotide from memory, you understand Unit 1.
  • Explain the “because.” AP questions rarely ask what; they ask why and what would happen if. Practice connecting every fact to its cause.
  • Space it out. Return to earlier topics every few days. Short, repeated reviews beat one long cram session.

The One Big Idea

Structure determines function. If you remember only one sentence from Unit 1, make it this one. The shape of water explains its properties. The sequence of amino acids determines a protein’s shape, and that shape determines what the protein does. The type of bond between sugars determines whether a polysaccharide is used for energy storage or for structure. On almost every Unit 1 free-response question, the winning answer connects a specific structural feature to a specific function.
