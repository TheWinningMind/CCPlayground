Glossary

Adhesion
Attraction between molecules of different substances, such as water and glass.
Amino acid
Monomer of proteins: amino group, carboxyl group, hydrogen, and R group on a central carbon.
Amphipathic
Having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions.
Antiparallel
Arrangement of DNA strands running in opposite 5′→3′ directions.
Capillary action
Rise of water in a narrow tube due to adhesion and cohesion.
Carbohydrate
Sugar or sugar polymer made of C, H, and O.
Cellulose
Structural polysaccharide of β-glucose in plant cell walls.
Chargaff’s rules
In double-stranded DNA, %A = %T and %G = %C.
Chitin
Structural polysaccharide in fungal cell walls and arthropod exoskeletons.
Cholesterol
A steroid in animal membranes; precursor to steroid hormones.
Cohesion
Attraction between molecules of the same substance.
Complementary base pairing
A with T (or U) and G with C via hydrogen bonds.
Covalent bond
A strong bond formed by sharing electrons.
Dehydration synthesis
Reaction joining monomers by removing water.
Denaturation
Loss of a protein’s native shape due to heat, pH, or chemicals.
Deoxyribose
Five-carbon sugar of DNA; lacks an –OH at the 2′ carbon.
Disulfide bridge
Covalent bond between sulfur atoms of two cysteines.
Electronegativity
An atom’s pull on shared electrons.
Enzyme
A biological catalyst, usually a protein.
Evaporative cooling
Cooling of a surface as high-energy water molecules evaporate.
Fatty acid
Hydrocarbon chain with a carboxyl group at one end.
Functional group
Atom cluster that gives a molecule characteristic properties.
Glycerol
Three-carbon alcohol that forms the backbone of fats and phospholipids.
Glycogen
Highly branched storage polysaccharide in animals.
Glycosidic linkage
Covalent bond between two monosaccharides.
Hydrogen bond
Weak attraction between a δ+ hydrogen and a δ− O or N atom.
Hydrolysis
Breaking a bond by adding water.
Hydrophilic
Interacting readily with water; polar or charged.
Hydrophobic
Not interacting favorably with water; nonpolar.
Hydrophobic interaction
Clustering of nonpolar groups away from water.
Ionic bond
Attraction between oppositely charged ions.
Isomers
Compounds with the same formula but different structures.
Lipid
Diverse hydrophobic molecules: fats, phospholipids, steroids.
Monomer
Small building-block molecule of a polymer.
Monosaccharide
Simple sugar such as glucose or fructose.
Nucleic acid
Polymer of nucleotides (DNA or RNA).
Nucleotide
Pentose sugar + phosphate group + nitrogenous base.
Peptide bond
Covalent bond joining two amino acids.
pH
−log[H+]; measures acidity on a 0–14 scale.
Phosphodiester bond
Bond linking nucleotides through a phosphate group.
Phospholipid
Glycerol + two fatty acids + phosphate head; forms membranes.
Polar molecule
Molecule with an uneven distribution of charge.
Polymer
Large molecule made of many linked monomers.
Polypeptide
A chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
Polysaccharide
A polymer of many monosaccharides.
Primary structure
Amino acid sequence of a polypeptide.
Protein
One or more folded polypeptides with a specific function.
Purine
Double-ring nitrogenous base: A, G.
Pyrimidine
Single-ring nitrogenous base: C, T, U.
Quaternary structure
Association of two or more polypeptide subunits.
R group
Variable side chain of an amino acid.
Ribose
Five-carbon sugar of RNA with a 2′ –OH.
Saturated fatty acid
Fatty acid with no C=C double bonds.
Secondary structure
α helices and β pleated sheets from backbone hydrogen bonds.
Specific heat
Energy to raise 1 g of a substance by 1°C.
Starch
Storage polysaccharide of α-glucose in plants.
Steroid
Lipid with four fused carbon rings.
Surface tension
Resistance of a liquid surface to breaking, from cohesion.
Tertiary structure
Overall 3-D shape of a polypeptide from R-group interactions.
Triglyceride
Glycerol bonded to three fatty acids; a fat.
Unsaturated fatty acid
Fatty acid with one or more C=C double bonds.
