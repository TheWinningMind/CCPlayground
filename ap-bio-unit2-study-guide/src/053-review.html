Unit 2 One-Page Review Sheet

2.1–2.2 Cell Structure and Function

  • All cells: membrane, cytoplasm, DNA, ribosomes (evidence of common ancestry).
  • Prokaryotes: no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles; nucleoid; plasmids. Eukaryotes: nucleus + organelles.
  • Rough ER makes proteins for export → Golgi modifies, sorts, packages → vesicles → exocytosis.
  • Smooth ER: lipids, detox, Ca2+. Lysosome: hydrolytic enzymes, pH 5. Central vacuole: turgor.
  • Mitochondria: cristae increase surface area for ATP synthesis; Krebs cycle in matrix.
  • Chloroplasts: light reactions on thylakoids (grana); Calvin cycle in stroma.

2.3 Cell Size

  • Cube: SA = 6s2, V = s3. Sphere: SA = 4πr2, V = (4/3)πr3.
  • Bigger cell → volume grows faster than SA → lower SA:V → slower exchange per unit volume.
  • Increase SA: microvilli, root hairs, flattened or long shapes, folded membranes.
  • Small animals: high SA:V → lose heat fast → high metabolic rate per gram.

2.4–2.5 Membranes

  • Amphipathic phospholipids → bilayer; fluid mosaic model.
  • Integral (transmembrane: nonpolar middle, polar ends) vs. peripheral proteins.
  • Cholesterol buffers fluidity; unsaturated tails increase fluidity.
  • Glycoproteins and glycolipids: cell recognition (outer surface).
  • Cross freely: small nonpolar, hydrophobic. Need proteins: ions, large polar. Water: slowly or via aquaporins.
  • Cell walls are porous; the membrane is selectively permeable.

2.6–2.7 Transport

  • Passive: down gradient, no energy (diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis). Equilibrium = no net movement.
  • Active: against gradient, energy + protein.
  • Channels (gated; aquaporins) and carriers (saturate).
  • Na+/K+ pump: 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in, 1 ATP; inside negative (about −70 mV).

2.8 Tonicity and Water Potential

  • Water moves toward more solute; from high Ψ to low Ψ.
  • Ψ = ΨP + ΨS; ΨS = −iCRT (R = 0.0831; K = °C + 273; i = 1 sugar, 2 NaCl).
  • Pure water, open: Ψ = 0. 1 MPa = 10 bars.
  • Animal cells: lyse / normal / crenate. Plant cells: turgid / flaccid / plasmolyzed.
  • Osmoregulation: contractile vacuoles; fish gills and kidneys.

2.9 Mechanisms

  • Primary active: pumps use ATP. Secondary (cotransport): symport, antiport; powered by an ion gradient.
  • Exocytosis out; endocytosis in: phagocytosis, pinocytosis, receptor-mediated (LDL).

2.10–2.11 Compartments and Origins

  • Compartments: simultaneous reactions, special conditions, more membrane surface area.
  • Endomembrane system from membrane infolding.
  • Endosymbiosis: mitochondria (aerobic bacterium, first) and chloroplasts (cyanobacterium, later). Evidence: double membrane, circular DNA, bacteria-like ribosomes, binary fission.
