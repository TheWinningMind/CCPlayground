Lab Focus: Diffusion and Osmosis

Unit 2 is the home of one of the most frequently tested laboratory investigations in AP Biology: measuring diffusion and osmosis. The exam often describes an experiment like the ones below and asks you to identify variables, graph data, calculate water potential, and predict what would happen under new conditions. Work through each experiment as if it were a free-response question. Answers are in Part 4.

Experiment 1 · Water Potential of Potato Cells

A student cut cylinders (cores) of potato with a cork borer, blotted them dry, and measured the mass of each group of cores. Each group was placed in a sealed cup containing a different concentration of sucrose solution at 22°C. After 24 hours, the cores were removed, blotted, and weighed again.   4. A second student repeats the experiment with potatoes that were left out on the counter for a week and became partly dehydrated. Predict how the point where the line crosses 0% would change, and justify your prediction.

Experiment 2 · A Model Cell Made of Dialysis Tubing

Dialysis tubing is an artificial membrane with tiny pores. Small molecules such as water, glucose, and iodine can pass through it; large molecules such as starch cannot. A student filled a bag of dialysis tubing with a solution of 15% glucose and 1% starch, tied it closed, weighed it, and placed it in a beaker of distilled water containing a few drops of iodine solution (IKI). Iodine turns blue-black in the presence of starch. After 30 minutes:

ObservationInside the bagIn the beaker
ColorChanged from clear to blue-blackStayed light amber (iodine color)
Glucose test stripPositivePositive (negative at the start)
Mass of bagIncreased from 24.6 g to 26.9 g
  1. Identify each substance (water, glucose, starch, and iodine) as having crossed the membrane or not, and cite the evidence for each.
  2. Explain why the mass of the bag increased.
  3. The dialysis bag is a model of a cell. Describe one way the dialysis membrane is similar to a plasma membrane and one way it is different.

Cubes of agar containing phenolphthalein (an indicator that turns pink in basic solutions) were cut to 1 cm, 2 cm, and 3 cm on a side and soaked in a sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution for 10 minutes. In every cube, the NaOH diffused 0.2 cm in from each face, turning that outer layer pink.
