Mind Map 1 · The Eukaryotic Cell

Information Center

  • Nucleus: double membrane + pores; stores DNA; transcription
  • Nucleolus: assembles ribosomal subunits
  • Ribosomes: rRNA + protein; no membrane; make proteins; in all cells
  • Free ribosomes → cytosol proteins; bound → secreted/membrane proteins

Endomembrane System

  • Rough ER: ribosomes; folds and modifies proteins
  • Smooth ER: lipids, detox, Ca2+ storage
  • Golgi: modifies, sorts, ships (cis in, trans out)
  • Vesicles link the parts
  • Path: ER → vesicle → Golgi → vesicle → membrane

Digestion & Storage

  • Lysosome: hydrolytic enzymes, pH ≈ 5; autophagy, apoptosis
  • Peroxisome: breaks down fatty acids; catalase destroys H2O2
  • Central vacuole (plants): water storage, turgor pressure
  • Contractile vacuole: pumps out excess water

membrane-bound organelles

Energy Organelles

  • Mitochondria: double membrane; cristae ↑ surface area; matrix; ATP by cellular respiration
  • Chloroplasts: double membrane; thylakoids (grana) + stroma; photosynthesis
  • Both have own circular DNA and ribosomes (endosymbiosis)
  • Plants have both

Support & Movement

  • Cytoskeleton: microtubules, microfilaments (actin), intermediate filaments
  • Shape, vesicle tracks, chromosome movement, cilia/flagella
  • Centrosome/centrioles (animals): organize microtubules
  • Cell wall: cellulose (plants), chitin (fungi), peptidoglycan (bacteria)

Compare Cell Types

  • Prokaryote: no nucleus (nucleoid), no membrane organelles, ribosomes, small
  • Animal: lysosomes, centrioles; no wall, no chloroplasts
  • Plant: wall, chloroplasts, central vacuole + mitochondria
Link to Unit 3: mitochondria and chloroplasts are where cellular respiration and photosynthesis happen.

Mind Map 2 · The Plasma Membrane

Phospholipid Bilayer

  • Amphipathic phospholipids: hydrophilic heads, hydrophobic tails
  • Heads face water on both sides; tails form the core
  • Forms spontaneously (hydrophobic interactions)
  • Hydrophobic core is the main barrier

Membrane Proteins

  • Integral: embedded; transmembrane span bilayer
  • Nonpolar amino acids in core, polar/charged outside
  • Peripheral: on the surface
  • Jobs: transport, enzymes, receptors, recognition, junctions, attachment

Carbohydrates

  • Glycoproteins and glycolipids on the outer surface
  • Cell–cell recognition: self vs. nonself, blood types
Plasma Membranefluid mosaic
selectively permeable

Fluidity

  • ↑ temperature → more fluid
  • Unsaturated (kinked) tails → more fluid
  • Saturated (straight) tails → less fluid
  • Cholesterol buffers: ↓ fluidity when warm, prevents packing when cold

Permeability

  • Free: small nonpolar (O2, CO2, steroids)
  • Slow: small polar (H2O, glycerol)
  • Need proteins: ions, large polar (glucose)
  • Need vesicles: macromolecules

Outside the Membrane

  • Cell wall: porous, structural, prevents bursting
  • ECM (animals): collagen and other glycoproteins
Link to Unit 1: phospholipid structure (amphipathic) and R-group polarity explain the whole membrane.

Mind Map 3 · Membrane Transport

Passive Transport

  • Down the gradient (high → low); no energy
  • Simple diffusion: through bilayer
  • Facilitated diffusion: channels (aquaporins, gated ion channels) and carriers (GLUT)
  • Facilitated rate saturates

Osmosis

  • Diffusion of water across a membrane
  • Water moves toward higher solute concentration
  • Hypotonic → water in: animal lyses, plant turgid
  • Isotonic → no net change: plant flaccid
  • Hypertonic → water out: crenation, plasmolysis

Water Potential

  • Water moves from high Ψ to low Ψ
  • Ψ = ΨP + ΨS; ΨS = −iCRT
  • Pure water Ψ = 0; solutes lower Ψ
  • Osmoregulation: contractile vacuole, kidneys

Active Transport

  • Against the gradient (low → high); requires energy
  • Na+/K+ pump: ATP; 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in
  • Phosphorylation → shape change
  • Proton pumps build H+ gradients

Gradients as Energy

  • Membrane potential: inside negative
  • Electrochemical gradient = concentration + charge
  • Cotransport: one substance down powers another up (Na+–glucose; H+–sucrose)

Bulk Transport

  • Exocytosis: vesicle fuses, releases outside
  • Phagocytosis: large particles
  • Pinocytosis: fluid, nonspecific
  • Receptor-mediated: specific (LDL)
Link to Units 3 and 4: H+ gradients drive ATP synthase; ion gradients drive nerve signals.
