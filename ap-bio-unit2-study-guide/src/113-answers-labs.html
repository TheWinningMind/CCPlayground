Answers: Lab Investigations

Lab 1: Cell Size and Diffusion

1. Independent variable: cube size (side length, and therefore SA:V ratio). Dependent variable: depth of diffusion and percentage of volume reached by the acid.
2. The acid travels the same distance into each cube in 10 minutes, but in a larger cube that distance is a smaller fraction of the way to the center. Volume increases faster than surface area, so a smaller share of a large cube is reached.
3. As SA:V increases, the percentage of volume diffused increases (6 : 1 → 99.2%; 2 : 1 → 60.6%).
4. Cells depend on diffusion across the membrane to supply their whole volume. Small cells have a high SA:V ratio and short diffusion distances, so materials reach all parts of the cell quickly enough to meet its needs. A very large cell would have regions that materials could not reach fast enough.
5. Higher: 100%. The sheet is only 0.5 cm thick, so acid diffusing 0.4 cm inward from the top and bottom faces meets in the middle and reaches all of it. Its SA:V (SA = 2(3 × 3) + 4(3 × 0.5) = 24 cm2; V = 4.5 cm3; SA:V ≈ 5.3 : 1) is slightly lower than the 1 cm cube’s, but what matters most is the short distance from every point to the surface. This is why flattened cells, such as the cells lining the lungs, exchange materials efficiently.

Lab 2: Diffusion and Osmosis with Dialysis Tubing

6. Into the bag: iodine (the bag turned blue-black, showing iodine reached the starch) and water (the bag gained mass). Out of the bag: glucose (the beaker tested positive for glucose at the end but negative at the start).
7. Starch molecules are far too large to pass through the pores of the tubing (the beaker stayed amber, showing no starch entered it).
8. (16.9 − 15.2) ÷ 15.2 × 100 = +11.2%. The bag gained mass because water moved into it by osmosis, from the beaker (lower solute concentration, higher water potential) to the bag (higher solute concentration, lower water potential).
9. Hypertonic means the bag had a higher solute concentration than the beaker. Water moves toward the higher solute concentration, which is why the bag gained mass.
10. The beaker would turn blue-black (iodine moves out of the bag into the starch), the bag would stay amber, glucose would appear in the bag, and the bag would lose mass because water moves out of it toward the more concentrated beaker.

Lab 3: Water Potential of Plant Tissue

11. Independent variable: sucrose molarity. Dependent variable: percent change in mass. Controlled variables (any two): carrot source, cylinder size, number of cylinders, solution volume, time, temperature, blotting method.
12. Weighing several cylinders together reduces the effect of measurement error and of variation between individual pieces. Percent change allows fair comparison because the groups did not start at exactly the same mass.
13. About 0.34 M. The line crosses 0% between 0.2 M (+5.8%) and 0.4 M (−2.6%): 0.2 + (5.8 ÷ 8.4) × 0.2 ≈ 0.34 M. (Accept 0.30–0.36 M.)
14. ΨS = −iCRT = −(1)(0.34)(0.0831)(295) ≈ −8.3 bars (−0.83 MPa). At the isotonic point the cells’ water potential equals that of the solution.
15. Water would evaporate, making every solution more concentrated (lower water potential). The cylinders would lose more mass (or gain less), so the graph would shift and the estimated isotonic molarity would be too low, which would underestimate the cells’ solute concentration.
