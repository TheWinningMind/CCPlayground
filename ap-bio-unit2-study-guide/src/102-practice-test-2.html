Question 1 (Long Response, 9 points)

Three days later, they placed the oocytes in a hypotonic solution and measured the percent increase in cell volume after 3 minutes. Some aquaporin-expressing oocytes were first treated with mercury chloride (HgCl2), which binds to aquaporins; some of these were then treated with mercaptoethanol, which removes mercury from proteins. Results are shown below (mean ± 2SEx̄; n = 10 oocytes per group).

GroupVolume increase after 3 min (%)
I. Water-injected (control)4 ± 1
II. Aquaporin mRNA42 ± 5
III. Aquaporin mRNA + HgCl29 ± 2
IV. Aquaporin mRNA + HgCl2 + mercaptoethanol38 ± 4
(a) Describe the structure of an aquaporin and explain how it allows water to cross the membrane faster than through the phospholipid bilayer alone. [2 pts]
(b) Identify the control group and the dependent variable. Identify one variable that should be held constant among the groups. [2 pts]
(c) Construct an appropriately labeled graph of the data, including error bars. [2 pts]

(e) Explain the results for Group III. Then explain how the results for Group IV support the claim that the effect of HgCl2 was due to its binding to aquaporins rather than to permanent damage to the cells. [2 pts]
