Part 2 · Skills and Review

Similarities and Differences

Many AP questions ask you to compare two structures or processes that are easy to confuse: a channel and a carrier, a hypotonic and a hypertonic solution, a lysosome and a vacuole. This chapter gives you 101 focused comparisons across all ten Unit 2 topics. Each one has three parts:

  • Both — the key feature the two concepts share.
  • The difference — a side-by-side statement of how they differ.
  • AP cue — the misconception or inference an exam question is likely to test.
How to Use a Comparison in a Free Response
  1. State one precise similarity grounded in structure, function, or mechanism.
  2. Explain at least one scientifically meaningful difference.
  3. Link the difference to the result expected from a model, experiment, or disruption.
  4. Avoid absolutes such as “always,” “never,” or “only” unless the conditions justify them.
Formula Snapshot

Cube: SA = 6s2; V = s3; SA:V = 6/s.  Sphere: SA = 4πr2; V = 4⁄3πr3; SA:V = 3/r.  Water potential: Ψ = ΨP + ΨS; ΨS = −iCRT.  Percent change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100.

Topic 2.1 · Cell types, structures, and organelles

001 Prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic cells

BOTH Both contain genetic material, ribosomes, cytoplasm, and a plasma membrane.

Prokaryotic cellsEukaryotic cells
DNA is generally located in a nucleoid; no membrane-enclosed nucleus; bacterial cells lack the standard eukaryotic endomembrane system.DNA is typically enclosed in a nucleus; cells possess a compartmentalized endomembrane system and other organelles.

AP CUE Ribosomes alone do not distinguish prokaryotes from eukaryotes.

002 Plant vs. animal cells

BOTH Both are eukaryotic and contain nuclei, mitochondria, ER, Golgi apparatus, ribosomes, cytosol, and plasma membranes.

Typical plant cellTypical animal cell
Typically has a cellulose cell wall, large central vacuole, and chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues.Lacks a cellulose wall and chloroplasts; often has smaller storage vesicles or vacuoles.

AP CUE Do not assume every plant cell contains a chloroplast (for example, many root cells do not).

003 Nucleus vs. nucleoid

BOTH Both localize much of a cell’s genetic information.

NucleusNucleoid
Membrane-enclosed region found in eukaryotic cells; separates most genomic DNA from cytoplasm.Non-membrane-enclosed DNA-containing region typical of prokaryotic cells.

AP CUE A nucleoid is not a membrane-bound organelle.

004 Ribosome vs. membrane-bound organelle

BOTH Both contribute to specialized cell functions.

RibosomeMembrane-bound organelle
An rRNA–protein complex lacking a surrounding membrane; carries out translation.Examples include mitochondria, lysosomes, and the Golgi apparatus; a delimiting membrane creates a compartment.

AP CUE Ribosomes occur in prokaryotes and eukaryotes, unlike most named membrane-bound organelles.

005 Free vs. ER-bound ribosomes

BOTH Both are ribosomes that translate mRNA; ribosomes may switch between states.

Free ribosomesER-bound ribosomes
Translate proteins generally destined to function in the cytosol or in some organelles.Translate proteins entering the secretory or endomembrane pathway as a targeting signal directs the ribosome to ER.

AP CUE Translation of a secreted protein begins on a free ribosome before ER targeting.

006 Rough ER vs. smooth ER

BOTH Both form parts of the ER membrane network and support cellular organization.

Rough endoplasmic reticulumSmooth endoplasmic reticulum
Ribosomes are attached to the cytosolic surface; supports synthesis and initial processing of proteins entering the ER.Lacks attached ribosomes; involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification.

AP CUE Neither ER type is equivalent to the Golgi apparatus.

007 Endoplasmic reticulum vs. Golgi apparatus

BOTH Both are membrane-bound parts of the endomembrane system and participate in trafficking.

Endoplasmic reticulumGolgi apparatus
Network of tubules and flattened sacs where many proteins enter the secretory pathway and lipids are synthesized.Stack of flattened cisternae that modifies, sorts, and packages cellular products.

AP CUE In a secretory-pathway experiment, newly synthesized protein generally reaches ER before Golgi.

008 Golgi apparatus vs. transport vesicle

BOTH Both participate in sorting or moving cell products within the endomembrane system.

Golgi apparatusTransport vesicle
An organized set of cisternae for processing and sorting cargo.A small membrane-bound carrier that moves cargo between compartments or to the plasma membrane.

AP CUE A vesicle is the carrier, not the same structure as its source or destination.

009 Lysosome vs. vacuole

BOTH Both are membrane-bound compartments; functions depend on cell type and contents.

LysosomeVacuole
Contains hydrolytic enzymes for intracellular digestion and recycling, typically in animal-cell models.Often stores water, ions, and other materials; central plant vacuoles also maintain turgor.

AP CUE Some vacuoles perform digestive functions; avoid claiming the categories are absolutely exclusive.

010 Central plant vacuole vs. animal-cell vacuoles

BOTH Both can contain water and dissolved substances and are bounded by membranes.

Central plant vacuoleAnimal-cell vacuoles
Often occupies much of a mature plant cell and plays a major role in turgor pressure.Usually smaller and more numerous when present, with various storage/transport roles.

AP CUE Relate turgor to cell-wall resistance, not simply to vacuole size.

011 Mitochondrion vs. chloroplast

BOTH Both have double membranes, internal membranes, and an evolutionary history consistent with endosymbiosis.

MitochondrionChloroplast
Involved in aerobic cellular respiration; inner membrane forms cristae.Involved in photosynthesis; internal thylakoids form stacks and other membranes.

AP CUE Both can occur in the same photosynthetic eukaryotic cell.

012 Mitochondrial cristae vs. thylakoids

BOTH Both increase internal membrane area for membrane-associated energy transformations.

CristaeThylakoids
Folds of the mitochondrial inner membrane.Distinct internal chloroplast membrane sacs that may form grana.

AP CUE A crista is continuous with the mitochondrial inner membrane; a thylakoid is not.

013 Lysosome vs. mitochondrion

BOTH Both have membranes that maintain specialized internal conditions.

LysosomeMitochondrion
Contains hydrolytic enzymes for breakdown and recycling.Contains compartments involved in reactions supplying ATP through respiration.

AP CUE Digestive enzyme activity does not identify a mitochondrion.

014 Chloroplast vs. central vacuole

BOTH Both are membrane-bound structures prominent in many plant cells.

ChloroplastCentral vacuole
Hosts photosynthesis and has double surrounding membranes plus thylakoids.Stores solutes and water, often supplying internal pressure that supports the cell wall.

AP CUE Plant-cell turgor can be explained without invoking photosynthesis.

Topic 2.2 · Cell size, geometry, and exchange

015 Small vs. large cubes

BOTH Both have surface area 6s² and volume s³ if each is a cube of side s.

Small cubeLarge cube
Has higher surface area-to-volume ratio 6/s, with shorter internal diffusion distances.Has lower surface area-to-volume ratio 6/s, even though total surface area is greater.

AP CUE Doubling the cube side halves SA:V; total area increases fourfold and volume eightfold.

016 Surface area vs. volume

BOTH Both depend on a cell’s dimensions and affect exchange physiology.

Surface areaVolume
Represents the membrane interface available for exchange and scales with length squared.Represents internal space and demand for resources, scaling with length cubed.

AP CUE Do not treat surface area as synonymous with surface area-to-volume ratio.

017 Total surface area vs. surface area-to-volume ratio

BOTH Both can influence the capacity for exchange.

Total surface areaSA:V ratio
Absolute membrane area that may increase as a cell enlarges.Relative membrane area per unit of cell volume; generally decreases as similar-shaped cells grow.

AP CUE A larger cell may have more total membrane but a lower SA:V.

018 A cube vs. a sphere of equal volume

BOTH Both are geometric approximations used to evaluate cell size and exchange.

CubeSphere
A cube of a given volume has more external surface area than a sphere of equal volume.A sphere minimizes surface area for its enclosed volume.

AP CUE Specify the volume or characteristic length before making a numerical comparison.

019 Compact vs. elongated cell

BOTH Both can contain the same cell volume and comparable amounts of cytoplasm.

Compact cellElongated cell
Often has less membrane area for a given volume if near-spherical.Can have higher membrane area for the same volume and shorter local diffusion distances.

AP CUE Greater membrane area requires a specified geometry, not a vague assertion about shape.

020 Membrane folding vs. increased cell diameter

BOTH Both alter membrane geometry.

Membrane foldingIncreasing cell diameter
Can increase functional exchange area without proportionately increasing internal volume.For similar shapes, increases internal volume faster than surface area.

AP CUE Microvilli improve exchange area; they do not make a cell more spherical.

021 Microvilli vs. mitochondrial cristae

BOTH Both are folds or projections that increase membrane area.

MicrovilliCristae
Extensions of the plasma membrane important for exchange across the cell surface.Folds of the mitochondrial inner membrane providing area for respiratory proteins.

AP CUE Location identifies their function: cell surface versus inner organelle membrane.

022 Diffusion distance vs. diffusion fraction in agar

BOTH Both can be inferred from an agar-cube investigation.

Diffusion distanceFraction of cube penetrated
Measures how far a substance moves inward under specified time and conditions.Compares penetrated volume with total volume; typically higher in a smaller cube for the same penetration depth.

AP CUE A similar penetration depth does not imply the same fraction of every cube diffused.

023 Radius vs. diameter

BOTH Both describe the linear size of a circular or spherical cell model.

RadiusDiameter
Distance from center to edge; use r in 4πr² and 4/3πr³.Distance across the whole sphere; d = 2r.

AP CUE Mixing diameter and radius makes SA:V calculations wrong by a factor of two.

Topic 2.3 · Plasma membrane architecture

024 Hydrophilic heads vs. hydrophobic tails

BOTH Both are covalently connected parts of amphipathic phospholipids.

Phospholipid headsPhospholipid tails
Polar, water-interacting regions face the aqueous environments.Nonpolar fatty-acid regions form the hydrophobic interior of a bilayer.

AP CUE A complete bilayer has heads facing both sides and tails inward.

025 Phospholipid bilayer vs. cell wall

BOTH Both contribute to organization and integrity at the cell boundary.

Phospholipid bilayerCell wall
The selectively permeable membrane that regulates many molecular exchanges.An external supportive structure in plants, fungi, and many prokaryotes that resists deformation.

AP CUE A cell wall does not substitute for the selectively permeable plasma membrane.

026 Integral vs. peripheral membrane proteins

BOTH Both can participate in membrane-associated cellular functions.

Integral proteinPeripheral protein
Embedded in the bilayer, often with hydrophobic regions; may span the membrane.Associates with a membrane surface or another membrane protein without spanning the hydrophobic core.

AP CUE A peripheral protein need not be a transporter.

027 Glycoprotein vs. glycolipid

BOTH Both can display carbohydrate chains on the extracellular membrane surface.

GlycoproteinGlycolipid
Carbohydrate is linked to a protein.Carbohydrate is linked to a lipid.

AP CUE In a diagram, identify what carbohydrate is attached to, not just the carbohydrate chain.

028 Cholesterol vs. phospholipid

BOTH Both are lipids present in many animal-cell plasma membranes.

CholesterolPhospholipid
A sterol inserted among phospholipids that modulates membrane fluidity and stability.A major amphipathic building block forming the two-layer membrane framework.

AP CUE Cholesterol is not a transport channel or a phospholipid head group.

029 Saturated vs. unsaturated fatty-acid tails [Enrichment]

BOTH Both contribute to the nonpolar interior of many membrane phospholipids.

Saturated tailsUnsaturated tails
Usually pack more tightly because they lack cis double-bond kinks.Cis double bonds introduce bends, often reducing packing and increasing fluidity.

AP CUE Membrane fluidity also depends on temperature and sterols; avoid single-factor certainty.

030 Fluid mosaic vs. rigid static membrane

BOTH Both can be depicted as layers with embedded proteins, but only one captures normal membrane behavior.

Fluid mosaic modelRigid static model
Many lipids and some proteins can move laterally; distribution and movement are regulated.Incorrectly treats every membrane component as fixed and immobile.

AP CUE A mobile membrane does not imply proteins can freely flip between bilayer leaflets.

031 Plasma membrane vs. nuclear envelope

BOTH Both contain lipid bilayers and membrane proteins.

Plasma membraneNuclear envelope
Outermost membrane delimiting cytoplasm from the extracellular environment.Double-membrane enclosure separating nucleoplasm and cytosol, with nuclear pores.

AP CUE Count the nuclear envelope as two distinct bilayers, not one bilayer folded over.

032 Membrane channel vs. membrane receptor

BOTH Both may be integral membrane proteins with specialized conformations.

Channel proteinReceptor protein
Provides a route for particular solutes to cross the membrane.Recognizes a signal or ligand; may initiate a cellular response without transporting that ligand.

AP CUE A protein binding a substance is not necessarily a transport protein.

Topic 2.4 · Selective permeability and protective boundaries

033 Small nonpolar molecules vs. ions

BOTH Both may be found in aqueous solutions around a cell.

Small nonpolar moleculeIon
Usually diffuses across the hydrophobic bilayer more readily (e.g., O₂ or CO₂).Charged particle crosses the lipid core poorly and normally requires a transport pathway.

AP CUE Concentration alone does not determine membrane permeability.

034 Small uncharged polar vs. large polar molecules

BOTH Both are more hydrophilic than small nonpolar molecules.

Small uncharged polar moleculeLarge polar molecule
May cross a bare bilayer slowly depending on molecular properties.Usually crosses poorly without a suitable protein or bulk transport.

AP CUE Avoid the oversimplification that all polar molecules behave identically.

035 Cell wall vs. plasma membrane permeability

BOTH Both influence exchange between a cell and its surroundings.

Cell wallPlasma membrane
Provides a mechanically protective, generally porous boundary; properties vary among organisms.Selectively controls passage across its lipid bilayer and associated proteins.

AP CUE Water and solute passage through a wall does not prove passage through the plasma membrane.

036 Walled plant cell vs. wall-free protoplast in dilute solution

BOTH Both have a plasma membrane and may gain water when outside water potential is higher.

Intact plant cellWall-free protoplast
Wall resists expansion and contributes to turgor.Without the wall, excess water gain can cause swelling and lysis.

AP CUE The wall reduces the risk of osmotic rupture; it is not a selectively permeable pump.

037 Direct bilayer passage vs. protein-mediated passage

BOTH Both transfer molecules across the same plasma membrane.

Direct passageProtein-mediated passage
Depends strongly on solubility, size, and charge of the molecule in the lipid core.Depends on suitable protein structure and may show selectivity or saturation.

AP CUE A molecule could have nonzero bilayer permeability yet move far faster via a transporter.

038 Lipid-soluble vs. water-soluble solute

BOTH Both may be dissolved in the extracellular environment.

Lipid-soluble soluteStrongly water-soluble solute
Typically partitions into the nonpolar bilayer interior more readily.Often interacts poorly with bilayer interior if charged or large/polar.

AP CUE Water solubility does not automatically mean rapid membrane passage.

039 Oxygen vs. sodium ion crossing a membrane

BOTH Both can have concentration differences across a plasma membrane.

Oxygen (O₂)Sodium ion (Na⁺)
Small, uncharged, nonpolar gas; typically crosses by simple diffusion.Charged ion; crosses efficiently through selective proteins, not the lipid core.

AP CUE Do not label O₂ movement as facilitated diffusion unless a specific route is given.

040 Intact vs. damaged plasma membrane

BOTH Both consist of or originate from membrane material.

Intact membraneDisrupted membrane
Preserves selective permeability and concentration differences.Loss of integrity may cause uncontrolled movement and failure of gradients.

AP CUE A membrane leak can collapse gradients even if all ATP-dependent pumps remain present.

Topic 2.5 · Membrane transport and vesicular trafficking

041 Passive vs. active transport

BOTH Both can involve selective membrane proteins and contribute to homeostasis.

Passive transportActive transport
Net solute movement is down its relevant chemical/electrochemical gradient; no direct metabolic energy is required.Moves a substance against its electrochemical gradient by coupling to an energy source.

AP CUE A carrier is not necessarily an active transporter; energy coupling is the decisive feature.

042 Concentration vs. electrochemical gradient

BOTH Both can influence the direction of solute movement across a membrane.

Concentration gradientElectrochemical gradient
Describes a difference in concentration alone.For an ion, incorporates concentration differences and membrane electrical potential.

AP CUE Ions can move against their concentration gradient but down their electrochemical gradient.

043 Simple diffusion vs. vesicular bulk transport

BOTH Both can deliver or remove material across a cell boundary.

Simple diffusionBulk transport
Individual molecules move across the bilayer down a gradient.Membrane remodeling encloses or releases larger cargo using vesicles; requires cellular machinery and energy.

AP CUE Endocytosis/exocytosis should not be called diffusion of a whole vesicle through a bilayer.

044 Endocytosis vs. exocytosis

BOTH Both involve membrane fusion/remodeling and vesicular movement of materials.

EndocytosisExocytosis
The plasma membrane invaginates or engulfs material to bring it into the cell.An internal vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane to release cargo outside.

AP CUE Identify direction relative to the cell, not the direction of a drawn arrow alone.

045 Phagocytosis vs. pinocytosis [Enrichment]

BOTH Both are forms of endocytosis.

PhagocytosisPinocytosis
Engulfs comparatively large particles, such as microorganisms, into vesicles.Takes up extracellular fluid and dissolved substances in smaller vesicles.

AP CUE These terms illustrate bulk uptake; specific molecular machinery is enrichment beyond core Topic 2.5.

046 Receptor-mediated endocytosis vs. nonspecific uptake [Enrichment]

BOTH Both internalize material in membrane-bound vesicles.

Receptor-mediated uptakeLess selective fluid uptake
Receptors preferentially bind particular ligands before internalization.Fluid-phase uptake is not dependent on the same ligand-specific receptor selection.

AP CUE A ligand binding a receptor is not the same as that ligand crossing a channel.

047 Vesicle budding vs. vesicle fusion [Enrichment]

BOTH Both are membrane-remodeling steps in intracellular trafficking.

Vesicle buddingVesicle fusion
Produces a transport vesicle from a donor membrane.Merges a vesicle membrane with a target membrane to deliver cargo.

AP CUE Distinguish creation of a carrier from delivery of its contents.

048 Net diffusion vs. random molecular motion

BOTH Both describe molecular movement in a fluid.

Net diffusionRandom motion
A statistical overall movement from higher to lower concentration when a gradient exists.Individual particles continue to move in random directions even at equilibrium.

AP CUE Equilibrium means no net flux, not no movement.

049 Gradient present vs. dynamic equilibrium

BOTH Both states involve moving particles.

Gradient presentDynamic equilibrium
Different concentrations provide a driving force for net diffusion if a permeable route exists.Concentrations are equal (in simple diffusion scenarios), so opposing fluxes balance.

AP CUE For ions, equal concentrations do not ensure electrochemical equilibrium if voltage differs.

050 Endomembrane transport vs. plasma-membrane transport

BOTH Both relocate substances and contribute to cell function.

Intracellular vesicle transportAcross-plasma-membrane transport
Cargo is carried between organelles or internal compartments.Cargo crosses, or is transported by vesicles across, the boundary separating inside and outside.

AP CUE Trace the route of a secreted protein without confusing ER-to-Golgi trafficking with release from the cell.

Topic 2.6 · Facilitated diffusion and transport proteins

051 Channel vs. carrier protein

BOTH Both can mediate passive, selective transfer of molecules down appropriate gradients.

Channel proteinCarrier protein
Provides a water-filled passage or pore for qualifying solutes; gating may regulate access.Binds a solute and changes conformation to carry it across.

AP CUE A saturation curve alone does not prove direct ATP use.

052 Aquaporin vs. ion channel

BOTH Both are transmembrane proteins that can increase movement across membranes.

AquaporinIon channel
Selectively facilitates rapid water movement.Allows particular ions to pass according to channel selectivity and electrochemical driving force.

AP CUE Aquaporins do not pump water against water potential.

053 Carrier-mediated uptake vs. simple diffusion rates

BOTH Both can show increasing flux as the driving gradient initially increases.

Carrier-mediated uptakeSimple lipid-bilayer diffusion
Can show saturation when a finite number of carriers become occupied.For a simple model, increases approximately with gradient rather than showing transporter saturation.

AP CUE A plateau is consistent with limited carrier capacity, but alternative explanations should be considered.

054 Functional vs. nonfunctional aquaporins

BOTH Both situations retain a phospholipid bilayer.

Functional aquaporinDisrupted aquaporin
Usually increases water permeability when appropriately expressed in the membrane.Often decreases water permeability relative to a functional channel; water may still cross the bilayer.

AP CUE A loss-of-function channel does not make the entire membrane impermeable to water.

055 Open vs. closed channel [Enrichment]

BOTH Both states may occur for a regulated channel protein.

Open channelClosed channel
Permits appropriate solute passage according to the electrochemical gradient.Restricts or prevents passage through that channel.

AP CUE Channel gating regulates permeability; it does not reverse the direction imposed by the gradient.

056 Downhill vs. uphill transport through proteins

BOTH Both may be mediated by specific proteins.

Downhill solute movementUphill solute movement
Occurs down the relevant electrochemical or chemical potential gradient.Requires energy coupling such as ATP hydrolysis or another gradient.

AP CUE An integral membrane protein can support either process; the source of energy distinguishes them.

057 Selective channel vs. nonspecific membrane leak

BOTH Both can permit some substance to move across a membrane.

Selective channelMembrane leak
Protein architecture restricts solute identity, often by charge and size.Nonspecific disruption increases unintended permeability and weakens homeostasis.

AP CUE Evidence of selective transport is not explained by a general membrane rupture.

058 Facilitated water movement vs. solute facilitated diffusion

BOTH Both are passive when movement is down the relevant driving gradient.

Water through aquaporinSolute through transporter
Net flow follows water potential differences across a water-permeable membrane.Net transfer follows the solute’s chemical/electrochemical driving force.

AP CUE Water potential and solute electrochemical potential are different comparisons.

Topic 2.7 · Tonicity, osmosis, and water potential

059 Osmosis vs. solute diffusion

BOTH Both result in net movement driven by physical gradients without direct ATP input.

OsmosisSolute diffusion
Net water movement across a selectively permeable membrane from higher to lower water potential.Net movement of a permeable solute from higher to lower chemical potential/concentration in a simple case.

AP CUE A solution can be hypertonic while a particular solute still diffuses into the cell.

060 Hypotonic vs. hypertonic solution

BOTH Both describe an external solution relative to a specified cell and its effectively nonpenetrating solutes.

Hypotonic environmentHypertonic environment
Has lower effective nonpenetrating solute concentration than inside; tends to cause net water gain.Has higher effective nonpenetrating solute concentration; tends to cause net water loss.

AP CUE Tonicity is relative and depends on solute permeability; it is not an intrinsic label for a solution.

061 Hypotonic vs. isotonic environment

BOTH Both describe how the extracellular solution affects cell volume.

HypotonicIsotonic
Typically causes net water uptake when membrane conditions are as assumed.Causes no sustained net change in cell volume at equilibrium.

AP CUE An isotonic environment does not mean water molecules stop crossing the membrane.

062 Hypertonic vs. isotonic environment

BOTH Both can be established with different solute concentrations.

HypertonicIsotonic
Tends to reduce cell volume through net water loss.Maintains approximately stable cell volume under the specified conditions.

AP CUE Different cells can interpret the same external solution differently if their internal solute composition differs.

063 Animal vs. plant cell in hypotonic conditions

BOTH Both may gain water as net osmosis proceeds inward.

Animal cell in hypotonic solutionPlant cell in hypotonic solution
May swell and lyse if water influx is sufficiently great.Typically becomes turgid as the cell wall resists expansion and pressure potential rises.

AP CUE A plant cell can remain turgid because of its wall, not because its membrane blocks water.

064 Animal vs. plant cell in hypertonic conditions

BOTH Both lose water when external water potential is lower.

Animal cell in hypertonic solutionPlant cell in hypertonic solution
Typically shrivels or crenates.May undergo plasmolysis as membrane and cytoplasm pull away from the wall.

AP CUE Plasmolysis is a specific wall-associated outcome, not simply a synonym for shrinking.

065 Turgid vs. plasmolyzed plant cell

BOTH Both retain a cell wall and plasma membrane.

Turgid cellPlasmolyzed cell
Water-filled vacuole presses the cell contents against the wall; positive turgor pressure.Water loss reduces volume and separates the plasma membrane from the wall in places.

AP CUE Do not describe plasmolysis as the cell wall shrinking away from the membrane.

066 Turgor pressure vs. pressure potential

BOTH Both concern mechanical pressure inside a cell.

Turgor pressurePressure potential (Ψp)
Positive outward pressure exerted by cell contents against the wall in a turgid plant cell.The pressure term in Ψ = Ψp + Ψs, which can vary with conditions.

AP CUE For an open solution exposed to the atmosphere, Ψp is commonly taken as zero.

067 Central vacuole vs. contractile vacuole

BOTH Both participate in water balance in their respective cell contexts.

Central plant vacuoleContractile vacuole
Large storage compartment that contributes to turgor in many plant cells.Collects and expels excess water in certain freshwater protists.

AP CUE Contractile vacuole discharge is an active cellular process; water entry itself follows a gradient.

068 Osmolarity gradient vs. water-potential gradient

BOTH Both can predict water movement if contextual conditions are specified.

Osmolarity differenceWater potential difference
Relates to concentration of osmotically active particles; higher osmolarity tends to reduce Ψs.Includes solute and pressure contributions to overall water potential.

AP CUE Water can move contrary to a simple solute-concentration prediction when pressure differs.

069 Solute potential vs. pressure potential

BOTH Both add to give total water potential in the AP model.

Solute potential (Ψs)Pressure potential (Ψp)
Usually zero or negative; decreases as idealized solute concentration increases.May be positive in a turgid plant cell; commonly zero in an open beaker.

AP CUE With Ψs = −iCRT, use Kelvin and the matching pressure constant.

070 Pure water vs. dissolved-solute solution

BOTH Both have water potential under defined conditions.

Pure water (reference)Solute-containing solution
At atmospheric pressure and reference conditions, Ψ = 0 bars.At zero pressure potential, dissolved solute makes Ψ negative.

AP CUE A pressure term may make total Ψ differ from the sign suggested by solute concentration alone.

071 Percent mass gain vs. percent mass loss

BOTH Both can be reported as (final − initial)/initial × 100%.

Mass gainMass loss
Produces a positive percentage change.Produces a negative percentage change.

AP CUE Retain the sign; do not describe −12% as a 12% increase.

072 Osmolarity vs. tonicity [Enrichment]

BOTH Both relate external solutions to water balance.

OsmolarityTonicity
Counts all osmotically active solute particles per volume of solution.Predicts sustained cell-volume change based on effectively nonpenetrating solutes.

AP CUE A permeant solute can increase osmolarity without creating lasting hypertonicity.

Topic 2.8 · Active transport and electrochemical gradients

073 ATP-dependent pump vs. passive ion channel

BOTH Both are proteins that allow regulated ion movement across membranes.

ATP-dependent pumpPassive ion channel
Couples metabolic energy to uphill transport and can generate gradients.Allows ions to move down their electrochemical gradients when open.

AP CUE A channel opening can dissipate a gradient established by a pump.

074 Sodium–potassium pump vs. potassium channel

BOTH Both participate in controlling ionic composition and electrical properties of animal cells.

Na⁺/K⁺-ATPaseK⁺ channel
Typically moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP hydrolyzed.Permits K⁺ flow according to electrochemical driving force; does not directly hydrolyze ATP for each passage.

AP CUE Direction and pump stoichiometry must be read together; they are not interchangeable.

075 Sodium export vs. potassium import in the Na⁺/K⁺ pump

BOTH Both occur during one ATP-dependent cycle of the same pump.

Na⁺ exportK⁺ import
Typically transports 3 Na⁺ from cytosol to outside.Typically transports 2 K⁺ from outside into cytosol.

AP CUE One net positive charge moves outward per cycle in the simplified model.

076 ATP hydrolysis vs. passive transport work

BOTH Both can occur in the same living cell and influence ion distributions.

ATP hydrolysisPassive ion flow
Provides a chemical-energy source for primary active transport.Follows a preexisting driving force without direct ATP coupling.

AP CUE A cell can use ATP to build a gradient that later supports passive movement.

077 Primary vs. secondary active transport [Enrichment]

BOTH Both can move a solute uphill by energy coupling.

Primary active transportSecondary active transport
Couples solute movement directly to an energy source such as ATP hydrolysis.Couples uphill solute movement to downhill movement of a different ion using an established gradient.

AP CUE Secondary active transport need not hydrolyze ATP at the cotransporter itself.

078 Symport vs. antiport [Enrichment]

BOTH Both are coupled movements of two substrates through a transporter.

SymportAntiport
Moves coupled species in the same direction across the membrane.Moves coupled species in opposite directions.

AP CUE Directions of the species—not whether transport is active—define symport and antiport.

079 Membrane potential vs. ion-concentration gradient

BOTH Both contribute to the electrochemical gradient.

Electrical potential differenceIon concentration difference
Reflects separation of charge across the membrane.Reflects unequal ion concentrations across the membrane.

AP CUE The electrical and concentration forces on an ion may oppose each other.

080 Pump inhibitor vs. channel blocker

BOTH Both may reduce ion transport but act through different routes.

ATPase inhibitorChannel blocker
Directly impairs ATP-dependent gradient maintenance; secondary changes accumulate over time.Reduces flow through a specific channel while existing gradients may initially persist.

AP CUE Specify measurement time; immediate channel effects differ from progressive loss of an ion gradient.

Topic 2.9 · Functional compartmentalization

081 Compartmentalized vs. noncompartmentalized reaction pathways

BOTH Both can be catalyzed by enzymes and use metabolic substrates.

Compartmentalized reactionsNoncompartmentalized reactions
Physical separation permits specialized pH, reactant concentrations, and compatible simultaneous reactions.Reactants and enzymes share a common space, possibly increasing interference between incompatible reactions.

AP CUE A claim about compartment benefits needs a plausible mechanism, not merely “more efficient.”

082 Membrane area vs. organelle volume

BOTH Both influence organelle capacities.

Membrane surface areaEnclosed volume
Supplies sites for membrane-bound catalysts and transport proteins.Provides space for soluble enzymes and reactants inside an organelle.

AP CUE Increased folding can expand reaction surface without proportional volume expansion.

083 Lysosomal lumen vs. cytosol

BOTH Both contain water and enzymes, but they are different chemical environments.

Lysosomal lumenCytosol
Usually more acidic and contains active hydrolytic enzymes.Near-neutral environment for many cytosolic reactions.

AP CUE Loss of proton retention may impair lysosomal digestion even when hydrolytic enzymes are present.

084 Mitochondrial outer vs. inner membranes

BOTH Both bound the organelle and regulate compartment interactions.

Outer mitochondrial membraneInner mitochondrial membrane
Separates the organelle from cytosol and is comparatively permeable to small molecules through porins.Highly selective and folded into cristae; carries energy-conserving complexes.

AP CUE The inner membrane, not outer membrane, is the usual location of most ATP synthesis machinery.

085 Mitochondrial matrix vs. intermembrane space

BOTH Both are mitochondrial compartments on opposite sides of the inner membrane.

MatrixIntermembrane space
Contains enzymes and mitochondrial DNA, among other constituents.Space between inner and outer membranes that can differ in ion conditions from matrix.

AP CUE A membrane is useful because the two sides can maintain different chemical conditions.

086 Chloroplast stroma vs. thylakoid lumen

BOTH Both are aqueous compartments within a chloroplast.

StromaThylakoid lumen
Region inside the inner envelope but outside thylakoids; contains enzymes, DNA, and ribosomes.Aqueous interior enclosed by the thylakoid membrane.

AP CUE Do not confuse chloroplast envelope membranes with the thylakoid membrane itself.

087 Membrane-bound compartments vs. ribosomes

BOTH Both may be essential to cellular biochemical processes.

Membrane-bound compartmentRibosome
A limiting membrane can retain solutes and establish conditions distinct from cytosol.An rRNA–protein machine performs translation without forming a lumen.

AP CUE A prokaryotic cell can translate proteins without membrane-bound organelles.

088 ER lumen vs. surrounding cytosol

BOTH Both contain biological molecules and interact through regulated traffic.

ER lumenCytosol
An internal compartment through which many secreted proteins pass during processing.Continuous cellular fluid outside organelles where free ribosomes initiate translation.

AP CUE Proteins cannot be assumed to freely cross the ER membrane without an appropriate mechanism.

Topic 2.10 · Evolutionary origins and evidence

089 Bacterial cell vs. mitochondrion

BOTH Both may have circular DNA, ribosomes, and division features consistent with shared ancestry.

Bacterial cellMitochondrion
An independently living cell with its own plasma membrane and diverse cellular functions.A specialized organelle dependent on its eukaryotic host for many proteins and functions.

AP CUE Resemblance supports endosymbiosis; it does not mean a modern mitochondrion is a free-living bacterium.

090 Photosynthetic bacterium vs. chloroplast

BOTH Both can carry out light-energy conversion and share molecular ancestry-related features.

Photosynthetic bacteriumChloroplast
A prokaryotic organism capable of independent cellular life.An organelle in photosynthetic eukaryotes with thylakoids and extensive host-cell dependence.

AP CUE Chloroplasts are related evolutionarily to cyanobacteria, not to modern green plant cells directly.

091 Double membranes vs. organellar DNA as evidence

BOTH Both are evidence consistent with endosymbiotic origins.

Double membrane organizationOrganellar genetic system
A structural feature compatible with engulfment and subsequent integration.DNA and translation machinery showing relationship to bacterial systems.

AP CUE One piece of evidence rarely proves a historical mechanism alone; multiple lines strengthen the claim.

092 Mitochondrial/chloroplast DNA vs. nuclear DNA

BOTH Both encode genetic information in eukaryotic cells.

Organellar DNANuclear DNA
Located inside mitochondria or chloroplasts; often relatively small and bacterial-like.Located in the nucleus and encodes most proteins needed for organelle function.

AP CUE Mitochondria and chloroplasts are not genetically independent of the nucleus.

093 Organelle division vs. eukaryotic cytokinesis [Enrichment]

BOTH Both increase the number of biological compartments/units.

Mitochondrial/chloroplast divisionWhole-cell cytokinesis
Organelles may divide by mechanisms resembling bacterial fission.Separates one eukaryotic cell into daughter cells.

AP CUE The fact that an organelle divides does not make it a complete cell.

094 Endosymbiotic origin vs. internal membrane invagination [Enrichment]

BOTH Both describe processes invoked in models of early eukaryotic complexity.

EndosymbiosisMembrane infolding/invagination
Explains the origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts from engulfed bacterial ancestors.Helps account for some internal membrane systems in models of eukaryotic cell evolution.

AP CUE Do not claim that mitochondria originated merely by nuclear-envelope infolding.

095 Inherited ribosomes vs. independently originating ribosomes

BOTH Both are historical hypotheses about similar biological machinery.

Shared ancestryIndependent origin hypothesis
Conserved translation machinery across life is consistent with shared evolutionary history.Would require unrelated origins yielding comparable core machinery without descent.

AP CUE Consistent structural/molecular evidence is stronger than superficial resemblance.

096 Structural vs. molecular evidence

BOTH Both can support evolutionary claims when interpreted appropriately.

Structural evidenceMolecular evidence
Includes double membranes, organelle division, and size similarities.Includes sequence and biochemical relationships between organelles and bacteria.

AP CUE Independent evidence types support a stronger endosymbiotic explanation than one observation alone.

097 Origins of mitochondria vs. chloroplasts [Enrichment]

BOTH Both are understood as consequences of endosymbiotic events.

Mitochondrial lineageChloroplast lineage
Associated with a bacterial ancestor related to alphaproteobacteria.Associated with a photosynthetic cyanobacterial ancestor.

AP CUE Mitochondria occur in most eukaryotic lineages; chloroplasts are restricted to photosynthetic lineages.

098 Evidence vs. unsupported assertion

BOTH Both are statements about a biological explanation.

Evidence-based claimUnsupported assertion
Explicitly connects observations—such as organelle DNA and membranes—to a mechanism.Repeats a conclusion without identifying a supporting observation or causal connection.

AP CUE Strong AP answers identify evidence AND explain why it supports the claim.

All topics · Across-topic high-value distinctions

099 Membrane area limitation vs. transporter abundance

BOTH Both can reduce total capacity for exchange across a cell membrane.

Low membrane areaFew transport proteins
Limits the total available interface even when protein density is constant.Limits specific protein-mediated flux even if area and driving gradient are unchanged.

AP CUE A low measured rate alone cannot distinguish these causes without a controlled design.

100 Osmotic swelling vs. active solute uptake

BOTH Both may change cell water content and volume.

Osmotic swellingEnergy-dependent solute uptake
Net water movement follows water potential differences without direct ATP input for water itself.Pumps or coupled transporters can change solute distributions and thus indirectly alter water movement.

AP CUE An ATP inhibitor changing cell volume does not prove water is pumped directly.

101 Organelle membrane disruption vs. enzyme inhibition

BOTH Both may reduce measured biochemical output.

Membrane disruptionEnzyme inhibition
Collapses specialized internal conditions and may cause solute leakage.Directly lowers catalytic activity while the compartment membrane might remain intact.

AP CUE Differentiate mechanisms by measuring compartment pH, leakage, or retained enzyme activity.

Cross-Topic Synthesis: Eight High-Value Exam Decisions
  1. “What crosses the membrane?” First determine size, polarity, and charge. Then identify whether the route is through the lipid core, a channel/carrier, an energy-coupled pump, or a vesicle.
  2. “Which way will it move?” For neutral solutes use chemical potential or concentration differences in simple cases; for ions account for both electrical and chemical forces; for water compare total water potential.
  3. “Does transport require ATP?” Distinguish direct ATP use from downhill movement through proteins and from secondary gradients indirectly established using ATP.
  4. “Will the cell change size?” Identify nonpenetrating solutes, membrane permeability, external water potential, and whether the cell has a resistant wall.
  5. “What limits exchange?” Check available membrane area, internal volume, diffusion distance, transporter number, and the relevant driving gradient.
  6. “How does an organelle help?” Specify the membrane feature, specialized compartment, biochemical function, and predicted result of disruption.
  7. “What supports endosymbiosis?” Use multiple independent observations such as organellar DNA, ribosomes, membranes, and molecular similarity to bacterial ancestors.
  8. “Is the evidence causal?” Look for manipulated and measured variables, relevant controls, replicates, uncertainty, and an explanation that rules out alternative causes.
