Part 3 · Practice

Free-Response Problem Workbook

These 72 data-based problems are the kind of reasoning the AP free-response section rewards: read a table, calculate, explain a mechanism, predict an outcome, and design or critique an experiment. There are twelve sets of six problems. Sets 1–10 follow Topics 2.1–2.10 in order, and Sets 11 and 12 mix every topic, the way the exam does. Each set has two long problems (9 points) and four short problems (4 points), for 408 points in all.

Work each set right after you finish its topic, or save Sets 11 and 12 for the week before the exam. Write on the lines provided, then score yourself with the point-by-point answers in Part 4. Every scoring point is worth 1 point, so you can see exactly what an answer needs to earn credit. The science practice codes (for example, 4.B or 6.E) tell you which AP skill each problem exercises.

Formulas You Will Need

Cube: SA = 6s2, V = s3  ·  Sphere: SA = 4πr2, V = 4⁄3πr3, so SA:V = 3 ÷ r  ·  Rectangular solid: SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)

Water potential: Ψ = ΨP + ΨS  ·  ΨS = −iCRT, with R = 0.0831 L·bar·mol−1·K−1 and T in kelvins (°C + 273)

Percent change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100

All data are illustrative values written for practice, not results from published studies.
