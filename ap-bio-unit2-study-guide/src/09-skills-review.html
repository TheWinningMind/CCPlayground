Science Practices and Unit 2 Math

The AP Biology Exam has two sections: multiple choice and free response. In recent years the free-response section has included two long questions (one of which involves interpreting an experiment) and four short questions that target specific skills such as experimental design, data analysis, models, and evolutionary trees. Always confirm the current format in the official College Board course description. Unit 2 is especially rich in calculation and lab-analysis questions, so this chapter focuses on them.

Know Your Task Verbs

Unit 2 Calculation Toolkit

Percent Change in Mass

% change = (final mass − initial mass) ÷ initial mass × 100. Example: a potato core goes from 2.50 g to 2.20 g. (2.20 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = −12%. A negative value means the core lost water, so the solution was hypertonic to the potato. Using percent change (instead of raw mass) lets you compare cores that started at different masses.

Finding the Isotonic Point from a Graph
  1. Plot percent change in mass (y-axis) against solution molarity (x-axis).
  2. Draw a line of best fit through the points.
  3. Find where the line crosses 0% change. Read the molarity on the x-axis.
  4. That molarity is isotonic to the tissue; it estimates the tissue’s solute concentration. Use it as C in ΨS = −iCRT to estimate the tissue’s solute potential.
SA:V Quick Reference

Cube: SA:V = 6/s. Sphere: SA:V = 3/r. When the side or radius doubles, SA:V is halved; surface area becomes 4 times larger but volume becomes 8 times larger.

Experimental Design Vocabulary

Independent variable
The factor the experimenter changes (x-axis). Example: sucrose molarity.
Dependent variable
The factor measured in response (y-axis). Example: % change in mass.
Controlled variables
Conditions kept the same: tissue type, core size, time, temperature.
Negative control
A group in which no response is expected (e.g., cores in distilled water show the effect of zero solute).
Positive control
A group in which a known response is expected; shows the method can detect an effect.
Null hypothesis
The prediction that the independent variable has no effect.
Error bars
Often ±2 SEx̄ (about 95% confidence). Non-overlapping bars suggest a significant difference; heavily overlapping bars suggest no significant difference.
The Claim–Evidence–Reasoning Template

Claim: State your answer in one sentence. Evidence: Point to specific data. Reasoning: Explain the biological principle. Example: “Claim: The potato cells have a solute concentration of about 0.3 M. Evidence: Cores gained 5% mass in 0.2 M sucrose and lost 4% in 0.4 M; the line crosses 0% at about 0.3 M. Reasoning: At the isotonic concentration, water potential inside and outside the cells is equal, so there is no net osmosis and no change in mass.”

Unit 2 One-Page Review Sheet

2.1–2.2 Organelles

  • All cells: membrane, cytoplasm, DNA, ribosomes (evidence of common ancestry).
  • Prokaryote: nucleoid, no membrane organelles. Eukaryote: nucleus + organelles.
  • Rough ER (proteins) → vesicle → Golgi (modify, sort) → vesicle → exocytosis.
  • Smooth ER: lipids, detox, Ca2+. Lysosome: hydrolysis at pH 5. Peroxisome: H2O2.
  • Mitochondria: cristae ↑ area for ATP. Chloroplasts: thylakoids + stroma.
  • Plants: wall, chloroplasts, central vacuole, and mitochondria.

2.3 Cell Size

  • Volume ∝ L3, area ∝ L2 → bigger cell = lower SA:V = less efficient exchange.
  • Increase area: microvilli, root hairs, folds, flattened shapes.
  • Small animals: high SA:V, lose heat fast, high metabolic rate per gram.

2.4–2.5 Membranes

  • Fluid mosaic: phospholipid bilayer + proteins + cholesterol + carbohydrates.
  • Integral (transmembrane) vs. peripheral proteins.
  • Glycoproteins/glycolipids: recognition.
  • Fluidity ↑: heat, unsaturated tails. Cholesterol buffers.
  • Small nonpolar cross freely; ions and large polar need proteins.
  • Cell wall: porous, structural.

2.6–2.7 Passive Transport

  • Passive = down gradient, no energy. Active = up gradient, energy.
  • Facilitated diffusion: channels (aquaporins, gated ion channels), carriers (GLUT). Rate saturates.

2.8 Tonicity & Water Potential

  • Water moves toward higher solute concentration = from high Ψ to low Ψ.
  • Hypotonic: water in. Hypertonic: water out. Isotonic: no net.
  • Ψ = ΨP + ΨS; ΨS = −iCRT; R = 0.0831; T in K; i = 1 sucrose, 2 NaCl.
  • −2 bars is higher than −5 bars.
  • Osmoregulation: contractile vacuoles, kidneys.

2.9 Mechanisms

  • Na+/K+ pump: ATP, 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in → inside negative.
  • Electrochemical gradient = concentration + charge.
  • Cotransport: Na+–glucose, H+–sucrose.
  • Endocytosis: phago-, pino-, receptor-mediated. Exocytosis: secretion.

2.10–2.11 Compartments

  • Separate conditions, simultaneous reactions, concentrated enzymes, isolated hazards.
  • Endosymbiosis: mitochondria then chloroplasts. Evidence: double membrane, circular DNA, own bacteria-like ribosomes, binary fission, DNA similarity.
  • Nucleus and ER: infolding of plasma membrane.
