How to Use This Guide

Unit 2 is where biology moves from molecules to living cells. You will take the macromolecules from Unit 1 and see how cells use them to build organelles, membranes, and transport systems. Almost every later topic—enzymes, cellular respiration, photosynthesis, cell signaling, the cell cycle, gene expression—happens inside one of the structures you learn about here, so the time you invest in Unit 2 pays off all year.

This guide covers all eleven topics of AP Biology Unit 2. Each topic follows the same pattern so you always know where to look:

  • Learning Targets — what you should be able to do by the end of the lesson. Use them as a checklist.
  • The Lesson — a clear explanation of the content, with diagrams and tables.
  • Misconception Alerts — the mistakes that cost students points year after year.
  • Exam Tips — how the idea tends to be tested and how to phrase strong answers.
  • Key Terms — the vocabulary you must be able to use precisely.
  • Check Your Understanding — six exam-style multiple-choice questions per topic. Full explanations are in Part 4.

Unit 2 is the most visual and the most mathematical unit of the course, so this guide includes extra tools that go beyond the lessons:

  • Unit 2 Master Comparisons — prokaryotes vs. eukaryotes, plant vs. animal cells, and every type of membrane transport side by side.

Study Habits That Work

  • Retrieve, don’t reread. Close the book and write down everything you remember about a topic, then check. Testing yourself is far more effective than highlighting.
  • Draw it. If you can sketch a cell with its organelles, the fluid mosaic model, and a cell in hypertonic solution from memory, you understand Unit 2.
  • Follow the molecule. For any transport question, ask: Which way is the gradient? Is the molecule small and nonpolar, or charged? Does it need a protein? Does it need energy?
  • Explain the “because.” AP questions rarely ask what; they ask why and what would happen if. Practice connecting every structure to its function.
  • Space it out. Return to earlier topics every few days. Short, repeated reviews beat one long cram session.

The Two Big Ideas

1. Structure fits function. A cell that makes lots of protein is packed with ribosomes and rough ER. A cell that needs lots of energy is packed with mitochondria. A membrane’s folds increase its surface area. On almost every Unit 2 free-response question, the winning answer connects a specific structure to a specific function.

2. Membranes control what moves. A selectively permeable membrane, together with its transport proteins, lets a cell keep its inside different from its outside. Gradients across membranes store energy and drive transport, and that idea returns in Unit 3 when cells make ATP.
