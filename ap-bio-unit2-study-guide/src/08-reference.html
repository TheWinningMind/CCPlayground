Glossary

Active transport
Movement of a substance against its gradient using energy and a membrane protein.
Amphipathic
Having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions.
Antiport
Cotransport of two substances in opposite directions.
Apoptosis
Programmed cell death.
Aquaporin
Channel protein that allows rapid movement of water.
Autophagy
Lysosomal digestion and recycling of a cell’s own parts.
Binary fission
Division of a prokaryote or organelle into two.
Carrier protein
Transport protein that binds a solute and changes shape to move it.
Cell theory
All living things are made of cells; cells come from cells.
Cell wall
Rigid, porous layer outside the plasma membrane of plants, fungi, and most prokaryotes.
Central vacuole
Large plant vacuole for storage and turgor pressure.
Channel protein
Protein that forms a hydrophilic tunnel for specific ions or water.
Chloroplast
Double-membrane organelle of photosynthesis.
Cholesterol
Steroid that buffers the fluidity of animal membranes.
Compartmentalization
Division of a cell into membrane-bound regions with specialized functions.
Concentration gradient
Difference in concentration across a space or membrane.
Contractile vacuole
Organelle that expels excess water from freshwater protists.
Cotransport
Use of one substance’s gradient to move another against its gradient.
Cristae
Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane.
Cytoskeleton
Network of protein fibers that supports and moves cell parts.
Diffusion
Net movement of molecules from high to low concentration.
Dynamic equilibrium
Equal movement in both directions; no net change.
Electrochemical gradient
Combined concentration and charge gradient of an ion.
Endocytosis
Uptake of materials by inward folding of the plasma membrane.
Endomembrane system
Nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vesicles, vacuoles, plasma membrane.
Endosymbiotic theory
Mitochondria and chloroplasts descended from engulfed prokaryotes.
Eukaryotic cell
Cell with a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
Exocytosis
Release of materials when a vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane.
Facilitated diffusion
Passive transport through a channel or carrier protein.
Fluid mosaic model
Model of a membrane as a fluid bilayer with embedded proteins.
Glycolipid / glycoprotein
Membrane lipid / protein with an attached carbohydrate chain.
Golgi complex
Stack of cisternae that modifies, sorts, and packages proteins.
Hypertonic
Having a higher solute concentration than another solution.
Hypotonic
Having a lower solute concentration than another solution.
Integral protein
Protein embedded in the lipid bilayer.
Isotonic
Having the same solute concentration.
Ligand
Molecule that binds specifically to a receptor.
Lysosome
Membrane sac of hydrolytic enzymes, pH about 5.
Membrane potential
Voltage across a membrane from unequal ion distribution.
Microvilli
Finger-like projections that increase membrane surface area.
Mitochondrion
Double-membrane organelle of cellular respiration.
Nucleoid
Region of a prokaryote containing its chromosome.
Nucleolus
Region of the nucleus where rRNA is made.
Nucleus
Double-membrane organelle that holds the cell’s DNA.
Osmoregulation
Control of water and solute balance.
Osmosis
Diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
Passive transport
Movement down a gradient with no metabolic energy.
Peripheral protein
Protein loosely bound to a membrane surface.
Peroxisome
Organelle that breaks down fatty acids and H2O2.
Phagocytosis
Engulfing of large particles (“cell eating”).
Phospholipid bilayer
Double layer of phospholipids forming a membrane.
Pinocytosis
Nonspecific uptake of extracellular fluid (“cell drinking”).
Plasma membrane
Selectively permeable boundary of every cell.
Plasmid
Small circular DNA molecule in many bacteria.
Plasmodesmata
Channels connecting the cytoplasm of adjacent plant cells.
Plasmolysis
Shrinking of a plant cell’s contents away from its wall.
Prokaryotic cell
Cell without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.
Proton pump
Pump that uses ATP to move H+ across a membrane.
Receptor-mediated endocytosis
Uptake of specific ligands bound to receptors.
Ribosome
rRNA–protein complex that synthesizes proteins.
Rough ER
Ribosome-studded ER that makes secreted and membrane proteins.
Selective permeability
Allowing some substances to cross more easily than others.
Smooth ER
ER without ribosomes; lipid synthesis and detoxification.
Sodium–potassium pump
Na+/K+ ATPase: 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in per ATP.
Solute potential (ΨS)
Part of water potential due to solutes; −iCRT.
Stroma
Fluid around the thylakoids; site of the Calvin cycle.
Surface area-to-volume ratio
Surface area divided by volume; falls as size increases.
Symport
Cotransport of two substances in the same direction.
Thylakoid
Membrane sac in a chloroplast; site of the light reactions.
Tonicity
Effect of a solution on a cell’s water balance.
Turgor pressure
Pressure of cell contents against the cell wall.
Vesicle
Small membrane sac that transports materials.
Water potential (Ψ)
Potential energy of water; water moves from high to low Ψ.
