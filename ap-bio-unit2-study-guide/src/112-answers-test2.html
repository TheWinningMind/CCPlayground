Free Response: Scoring Guidelines

Question 1 (9 points)

(a) 2 points

  • 1 pt — Describes structure: an aquaporin is a transmembrane (integral) channel protein that forms a narrow hydrophilic pore through the membrane.
  • 1 pt — Explains: the polar interior of the channel lets polar water molecules pass rapidly (in single file) without crossing the hydrophobic core of the bilayer, where polar molecules cross slowly.

(b) 2 points

  • 1 pt — Control group: Group I (water-injected oocytes). Dependent variable: percent increase in cell volume after 3 minutes. (Both required.)
  • 1 pt — Any one constant: concentration of the hypotonic solution, temperature, time (3 minutes), time since injection (3 days), oocyte source/size, volume injected.

(c) 2 points

  • 1 pt — Bar graph (groups are categories) with labeled axes: x = treatment group; y = volume increase (%); appropriate scale; bars plotted correctly.
  • 1 pt — Error bars plotted correctly (e.g., Group II spans 37 to 47%).

(d) 1 point — Significant. The error bars for Group I (3–5%) and Group II (37–47%) do not overlap, so the difference is very likely not due to chance.

(e) 2 points

  • 1 pt — Group III: HgCl2 binds to the aquaporins and blocks or changes the shape of the channel, so water enters much more slowly and the volume increase drops to near the control level.
  • 1 pt — Group IV: removing the mercury restored nearly full swelling (38%), so the aquaporins were still present and functional; the effect of HgCl2 was reversible binding to the channel, not permanent damage to the cells.

Question 2 (4 points)

  • (a) 1 pt — Rough ER → Golgi complex → secretory vesicles (the percentage peaks in each location in that order over time).
  • (b) 1 pt — The Golgi complex (47%). Proteins there are modified (for example, sugar chains are added or trimmed), sorted, and packaged into vesicles.
  • (c) 1 pt — Much lower than 60% (near 4–10%), with radioactive protein accumulating in the Golgi instead.
  • (d) 1 pt — Ribosomes on the rough ER insert the growing protein into the ER lumen, which places it inside the endomembrane system so it can be processed and packaged into vesicles for exocytosis; proteins made by free ribosomes stay in the cytosol.

Question 3 (4 points)

  • (a) 1 pt — Any two: circular DNA; small (70S-type) ribosomes; division by fission.
  • (b) 1 pt — The inner membrane corresponds to the plasma membrane of the engulfed prokaryote, and the outer membrane comes from the host cell’s membrane that surrounded it during engulfment.
  • (c) 1 pt — The nucleus has linear DNA, contains no ribosomes of its own, does not divide by fission, and its envelope is continuous with the ER. These features are unlike bacteria and consistent with formation by infolding of the plasma membrane.
  • (d) 1 pt — Protein synthesis inside mitochondria will decrease, because mitochondrial ribosomes resemble bacterial 70S ribosomes and the drug inhibits that type of ribosome.
