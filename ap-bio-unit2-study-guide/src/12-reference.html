Glossary

Active transport
Movement of a substance against its concentration gradient, using energy, through a carrier protein.
Amphipathic
Having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions.
Aquaporin
Channel protein that allows rapid movement of water across a membrane.
Autophagy
Digestion of a cell’s own worn-out organelles by lysosomes.
Binary fission
Prokaryotic cell division; also how mitochondria and chloroplasts divide.
Carrier protein
Transport protein that binds a substance and changes shape to move it across.
Cell wall
Rigid, porous layer outside the plasma membrane of plants, fungi, and most prokaryotes.
Central vacuole
Large water-filled sac in plant cells that maintains turgor pressure.
Centrioles
Paired microtubule structures in the centrosome of animal cells.
Channel protein
Transport protein with a hydrophilic pore.
Chloroplast
Double-membrane organelle where photosynthesis occurs.
Cholesterol
Steroid in animal membranes that buffers fluidity.
Compartmentalization
Division of the cell into membrane-bound regions with different functions.
Concentration gradient
Difference in concentration of a substance between two regions.
Contractile vacuole
Organelle that pumps excess water out of freshwater protists.
Cotransport
Coupled transport in which one substance moving down its gradient drives another up its gradient.
Cristae
Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane.
Cytoskeleton
Network of microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments.
Cytosol
Fluid portion of the cytoplasm.
Diffusion
Net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration.
Electrochemical gradient
Combined concentration and electrical gradient for an ion.
Endocytosis
Uptake of material by inward folding of the plasma membrane.
Endomembrane system
Nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles, vesicles, and plasma membrane.
Endosymbiont theory
Theory that mitochondria and chloroplasts descend from engulfed prokaryotes.
Eukaryotic cell
Cell with a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
Exocytosis
Release of vesicle contents outside the cell.
Facilitated diffusion
Passive transport through a channel or carrier protein.
Fluid mosaic model
Model of the membrane as a fluid bilayer containing a mosaic of proteins and other molecules.
Glycolipid
Lipid with an attached carbohydrate chain.
Glycoprotein
Protein with an attached carbohydrate chain.
Golgi complex
Stacked membrane sacs that modify, sort, and package proteins and lipids.
Hypertonic
Having a higher solute concentration than another solution.
Hypotonic
Having a lower solute concentration than another solution.
Integral protein
Protein embedded in the lipid bilayer.
Isotonic
Having equal solute concentration.
Lysosome
Membrane sac of hydrolytic enzymes with an acidic interior.
Membrane potential
Voltage across a membrane.
Microvilli
Membrane projections that increase surface area for absorption.
Mitochondrion
Double-membrane organelle where cellular respiration produces ATP.
Nucleoid
Region containing DNA in a prokaryotic cell.
Nucleolus
Region in the nucleus where ribosomal subunits are assembled.
Nucleus
Double-membrane organelle that contains the cell’s DNA.
Osmoregulation
Control of water balance and solute concentration.
Osmosis
Diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
Passive transport
Movement down a concentration gradient with no energy input.
Peripheral protein
Protein loosely attached to the membrane surface.
Peroxisome
Organelle that breaks down fatty acids and H2O2.
Phagocytosis
Endocytosis of large particles (“cell eating”).
Pinocytosis
Nonspecific endocytosis of fluid (“cell drinking”).
Plasmodesmata
Channels through plant cell walls connecting neighboring cells.
Plasmolysis
Shrinking of a plant cell’s cytoplasm away from its wall.
Pressure potential (ΨP)
Component of water potential due to physical pressure.
Prokaryotic cell
Cell without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.
Proton pump
Active transporter that moves H+ across a membrane.
Receptor-mediated endocytosis
Endocytosis of specific molecules that bind membrane receptors.
Ribosome
rRNA–protein complex that synthesizes proteins.
Rough ER
ER with bound ribosomes; makes proteins for secretion and membranes.
SA:V ratio
Surface area divided by volume.
Selective permeability
Property of allowing some substances to cross more easily than others.
Smooth ER
ER without ribosomes; makes lipids, detoxifies, stores Ca2+.
Sodium–potassium pump
ATP-powered carrier moving 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in.
Solute potential (ΨS)
Component of water potential due to solutes; −iCRT.
Stroma
Fluid surrounding the thylakoids in a chloroplast.
Thylakoid
Membrane sac in a chloroplast where light reactions occur.
Tonicity
Ability of a solution to cause a cell to gain or lose water.
Turgor pressure
Pressure of cell contents against the cell wall.
Vesicle
Small membrane sac that transports materials.
Water potential (Ψ)
Tendency of water to move; water moves from high to low Ψ.
